# Research Digest: terezin-vedem (gap_fill)

## p2_gap_demographics_urls — Canonical primary URL resolution and multi-source verification of Terezín (Theresienstadt) spatial footprint, pre-war population, September 1942 overcrowding/mortality peaks, child mortality/births/survival, and aggregate prisoner/deportation/death cohorts.

- **[LOW CONFIDENCE]** Total area of the walled Terezín Main Fortress (Hlavní pevnost / Große Festung) enclosing the ghetto town: `~36` (hectares)
  - Sources (2): https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/1561/; https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/theresienstadt-19411945/741CA0829871F8EFC4099684C2F4BE69
  - Disagreements / caveats: Single authoritative primary measurement directly observed in session (UNESCO Tentative List #1561, inner defense perimeter 3,040 m; total 18th-century fortress land purchase ~400 ha; Small Fortress ~2.5 ha). Corroborated in scale by Adler's 335,056 m² (33.51 ha) Jewish settlement footprint within the walled fortress.
- **[LOW CONFIDENCE]** Total surface area of the Jewish settlement/ghetto within Terezín and usable inmate living quarters area: `335,056 (settlement total); ~95,000 (living quarters)` (square meters (m²))
  - Sources (4): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/theresienstadt-19411945/741CA0829871F8EFC4099684C2F4BE69; https://doi.org/10.1017/9781139017053; https://academic.oup.com/book/36881; https://www.holocaust.cz/zdroje/clanky-z-ros-chodes/prezivani-v-ghettu-terezin/
  - Disagreements / caveats: 335,056 m² total settlement area (~42% built up, ~16% courtyards, ~42% streets/public spaces/gardens) and ~95,000 m² living space trace primarily to H. G. Adler (Ch. 11 "Housing"), cited by later scholarship including Anna Hájková. Independently cross-checked by Vojtěch Blodig (Památník Terezín), who reports 1.6 m² average living space per prisoner at the ~58,500 Sept 1942 peak (~93,600 m² implied).
- **[HIGH]** Pre-war / pre-ghetto population of Terezín garrison town (civilians plus military garrison): `7,000–7,200 (~3,700 civilians + ~3,500 garrison)` (persons)
  - Sources (4): https://bterezin.org.il/ghetto-theresienstadt/historical-background/; https://keydocuments.net/article/martha-glass-every-day-in-theresin-is-a-gift; https://www.holocaust.cz/zdroje/clanky-z-ros-chodes/prezivani-v-ghettu-terezin/; https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/theresienstadt-19411945/741CA0829871F8EFC4099684C2F4BE69
  - Disagreements / caveats: Beit Theresienstadt, Key Documents (Martha Glass edition), and Vojtěch Blodig cite ~7,000 total (roughly half civilian, half military); Key Documents specifies ~3,700 civilian residents resettled by July 1942, and Adler / Lederer detail ~3,700 civilians + ~3,500 soldiers (~7,200 total).
- **[HIGH]** Peak simultaneous inmate population inside Terezín ghetto in mid-September 1942: `58,491–58,497 (rounded ~58,500)` (prisoners)
  - Sources (4): https://blog.ehri-project.eu/2016/05/18/daily-orders-from-the-terezin-theresienstadt-ghetto/; https://bterezin.org.il/ghetto-theresienstadt/historical-background/; https://centraleuropeaneconomicandsocialhistory.com/the-kz-theresienstadt-the-deceit-of-the-nazis-of-a-jewish-ghetto-for-the-privileged-and-the-old-the-true-living-conditions-especially-of-the-elderly/; https://www.holocaust.cz/zdroje/clanky-z-ros-chodes/prezivani-v-ghettu-terezin/
  - Disagreements / caveats: EHRI Document Blog (Daily Orders) and Beit Theresienstadt report 58,491 (16 September 1942); Susanne Wurm reports 58,497 (18 September 1942); Vojtěch Blodig rounds to ~58,500. The 6-person difference reflects daily registration updates in mid-September 1942. Note: EHRI archival unit https://portal.ehri-project.eu/units/cz-002195~pt-3941 is an unrelated concert program (PT 3941), NOT a demographic figure.
- **[HIGH]** Peak monthly death toll inside Terezín ghetto (September 1942): `3,941` (deaths in September 1942)
  - Sources (3): https://blog.ehri-project.eu/2016/05/18/daily-orders-from-the-terezin-theresienstadt-ghetto/; https://bterezin.org.il/ghetto-theresienstadt/historical-background/; https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/theresienstadt-19411945/741CA0829871F8EFC4099684C2F4BE69
  - Disagreements / caveats: Exact monthly figure 3,941 corroborated by EHRI Document Blog (Daily Orders), Beit Theresienstadt, and H. G. Adler.
- **[MEDIUM]** Children who died directly inside the Terezín ghetto: `~400` (children)
  - Sources (4): https://karger.com/goi/article/87/6/406/825227; https://doi.org/10.1159/000528008; https://psu.pb.unizin.org/holocaust/chapter/terezin-and-the-last-cyclist/; https://is.muni.cz/th/vyonh/
  - Disagreements / caveats: Both David, Ebert, & Kuntz (2022, Karger peer-reviewed article) and Penn State / Unizin (*Terezín and 'The Last Cyclist'*, citing Nazi records) report 400 children died inside Terezín; Czech university scholarship (Masaryk University) likewise cites ~400. Major institutional summaries (USHMM, Yad Vashem, Památník Terezín) typically omit a separate in-ghetto child mortality sub-total and focus on ~15,000 total children and 7,590 deported children.
- **[HIGH]** Children deported east from Terezín and number of those deportees who survived: `7,590 deported; 142–245 survivors` (children)
  - Sources (3): https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/terezin-ghetto; https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/ghetto-terezin; https://psu.pb.unizin.org/holocaust/chapter/terezin-and-the-last-cyclist/
  - Disagreements / caveats: Památník Terezín reports 7,590 youngest child prisoners deported east with 142 survivors. Penn State / Unizin reports >7,500 children deported east with 245 alive at war's end (some secondary/academic tallies cite 242). The 142 vs. 242–245 gap reflects cohort/age definitions (youngest 7,590 cohort vs. broader >7,500 child deportees) and post-war registry cutoffs.
- **[LOW CONFIDENCE]** Children under age 15 alive inside Terezín at liberation (May 1945): `1,633 (~1,600 rounded)` (children (<15 years))
  - Sources (3): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/theresienstadt-19411945/741CA0829871F8EFC4099684C2F4BE69; https://doi.org/10.1017/9781139017053; https://karger.com/goi/article/87/6/406/825227
  - Disagreements / caveats: Exact figure 1,633 traces to H. G. Adler (Ch. "Welfare"); David, Ebert, & Kuntz (2022, Karger) round to ~1,600. Roughly half were long-term ghetto inmates and the remainder arrived via late-war evacuation transports in April–May 1945.
- **[HIGH]** Live births inside the Terezín ghetto (1941–1945): `205–230 (207–230 in Pass 1)` (births)
  - Sources (4): https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/theresienstadt-19411945/741CA0829871F8EFC4099684C2F4BE69; https://doi.org/10.1017/9781139017053; https://karger.com/goi/article/87/6/406/825227; https://doi.org/10.1159/000528008
  - Disagreements / caveats: H. G. Adler (Ch. 16 "Health Conditions") explicitly writes that figures vary between 205 and 209, possibly reaching 230 (Gonda Redlich's diary/internal records cite 207); David, Ebert, & Kuntz (2022, Karger) document Franz Bass's obstetric records (~207–230 births alongside ~350 forced abortions). Note: SS authorities falsely claimed 300–400 births during the June 1944 Red Cross visit.
- **[HIGH]** Total arriving prisoner cohorts in Terezín (regular transports vs. including April–May 1945 evacuation transports): `140,000–141,184 (regular); 150,000–155,000+ (incl. 1945 evacuations)` (prisoners)
  - Sources (5): https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/theresienstadt; https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-holocaust-in-bohemia-and-moravia; https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/terezin-ghetto; https://www.yadvashem.org/holocaust/about/ghettos/theresienstadt.html; https://bterezin.org.il/ghetto-theresienstadt/historical-background/
  - Disagreements / caveats: Regular ghetto transports (Nov 1941 – Apr 20, 1945): ~140,000 (USHMM Theresienstadt, Yad Vashem, Památník Terezín, Beit Theresienstadt) to 141,184 (USHMM Bohemia & Moravia ledger). Adding ~15,000 concentration camp evacuation prisoners arriving in April–May 1945 yields ~150,000–155,000+ (Beit Theresienstadt notes up to ~158,000).
- **[HIGH]** Deaths inside the Terezín ghetto (1941–1945): `33,456–35,440` (deaths)
  - Sources (5): https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-holocaust-in-bohemia-and-moravia; https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/theresienstadt; https://www.yadvashem.org/holocaust/about/ghettos/theresienstadt.html; https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/terezin-ghetto; https://bterezin.org.il/ghetto-theresienstadt/historical-background/
  - Disagreements / caveats: USHMM Bohemia & Moravia ledger gives 33,456 (USHMM Theresienstadt rounds to ~34,000); Yad Vashem gives 35,440; Památník Terezín gives >35,000; Beit Theresienstadt gives ~35,000 (~35,409). Discrepancy reflects regular wartime ghetto registry totals (33,456) vs. expanded postwar tallies incorporating late-war evacuation and typhus deaths at/around liberation (35,440).
- **[HIGH]** Prisoners deported east from Terezín and survivors among eastern deportees: `86,934–90,000 deported; 3,097–3,800 survivors` (prisoners)
  - Sources (5): https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-holocaust-in-bohemia-and-moravia; https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/theresienstadt; https://www.yadvashem.org/holocaust/about/ghettos/theresienstadt.html; https://www.yadvashem.org/holocaust/about/timeline/1940-1945.html; https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/terezin-ghetto
  - Disagreements / caveats: Deportations east: 86,934 (Yad Vashem Timeline), >87,000 across 63 transports (Památník Terezín), ~88,000 (USHMM Theresienstadt & Yad Vashem Ghetto article), 88,202 (USHMM Bohemia & Moravia ledger), up to ~90,000 (broader Yad Vashem summaries). Survivors among eastern deportees: 3,097 (Yad Vashem Timeline, matching Miroslav Kárný / Czech historiography), ~3,500 (USHMM Theresienstadt), ~3,800 (Památník Terezín). USHMM Bohemia & Moravia also records 16,832 liberated in Terezín, ~2,418 escaped/released earlier in 1945, and 276 arrested by the Gestapo.
- **Myths checked:**
  - EHRI unit https://portal.ehri-project.eu/units/cz-002195~pt-3941 is NOT a citation for 3,941 September 1942 deaths—it is an unrelated archival item (PT 3941, concert program in Heřman's Collection). The canonical EHRI source for 58,491 and 3,941 is https://blog.ehri-project.eu/2016/05/18/daily-orders-from-the-terezin-theresienstadt-ghetto/.
  - The 300–400 births figure reported to International Committee of the Red Cross inspectors in June 1944 was SS propaganda; documented births were 205–230 (207–230).
  - The 1,633 children (<15) liberated in May 1945 were not all long-term Terezín ghetto children—roughly half arrived on late-war April–May 1945 evacuation transports from other camps.
  - USHMM's 33,456 deaths and Yad Vashem's 35,440 deaths are not contradictory errors; they reflect regular wartime ghetto registry accounting (33,456 out of 141,184) versus expanded tallies including late-war evacuation/liberation-period deaths.

## p2_gap_redcross_rossel — Archival and historiographical reconciliation of the June 23, 1944 ICRC visit to Theresienstadt (duration, Maurice Rossel's photograph count and report, May and Sept-Oct 1944 deportation manifests, and Paul Eppstein's coerced role and execution).

- **[HIGH]** Duration of the June 23, 1944 ICRC and Danish delegation visit to Theresienstadt: `4 to 8 hours (4 hours: ~12:00-16:00; 6 hours; 8 hours: ~11:00-19:00)` (hours)
  - Sources (5): https://www.holocaust.cz/en/history/theresienstadt/the-makeover-of-the-theresienstadt-ghetto/; https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/theresienstadt-as-tool-of-nazi-propaganda-the-red-cross-visit-1944; https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/nazi-propaganda; https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-theresienstadt-terezin-ghetto; https://blogs.icrc.org/cross-files/en/what-did-he-really-see-an-icrc-delegates-visit-to-theresienstadt-on-23-june-1944/
  - Disagreements / caveats: Sources differ between 4, 6, and 8 hours depending on whether they measure the choreographed ghetto tour itself or the full arrival-to-departure schedule at Theresienstadt: holocaust.cz specifies 'approx. 12 to 4 p.m.' (4 hours) for the ghetto inspection (noting evening dinner at Czernin Palace in Prague at 20:30); USHMM articles 'nazi-propaganda' and 'the-theresienstadt-terezin-ghetto' state 'six hours'; USHMM's dedicated Red Cross visit article ('theresienstadt-as-tool-of-nazi-propaganda-the-red-cross-visit-1944') states 'approximately 11:00 a.m. until 7:00 p.m.' (8 hours total on site). Daniel Palmieri (ICRC Cross-Files, April 8, 2026) characterizes it broadly as an 'hours-long visit' along a tightly escorted route (report B G 59/12-368.02).
- **[HIGH]** Exact photograph count taken by ICRC delegate Maurice Rossel on June 23, 1944: `38 (ICRC Cross-Files & V-P-HIST archives) vs. 36 (Farré & Schubert 2009; ~16 attached to report)` (photographs)
  - Sources (2): https://blogs.icrc.org/cross-files/en/what-did-he-really-see-an-icrc-delegates-visit-to-theresienstadt-on-23-june-1944/; https://shs.cairn.info/revue-le-mouvement-social1-2009-2-page-65?lang=fr
  - Disagreements / caveats: Two authoritative counts exist: ICRC historian Daniel Palmieri (Cross-Files, 2026) and the ICRC Audiovisual Archives (series V-P-HIST-01160, V-P-HIST-01161, V-P-HIST-01162, including V-P-HIST-01161-27 showing Rossel before the music kiosk and 5 fire-brigade drill shots) confirm 38 photos taken by Rossel. Historians Sébastien Farré and Yan Schubert (Le Mouvement Social No. 227, 2009, 'L'illusion de l'objectif') cite 36 photographs (matching a standard 36-exposure 35mm Leica roll), noting not all were attached to Rossel's report (B G 59/12-368.02); secondary summaries commonly report 16 prints attached to the official report.
- **[HIGH]** May 15-18, 1944 pre-visit deportations from Theresienstadt to Auschwitz-Birkenau (Family Camp BIIb) across Transports Dz, Ea, and Eb: `7,503 (Dz: 2,503 [or 2,501]; Ea: 2,500; Eb: 2,500)` (deportees across 3 transports)
  - Sources (4): https://www.holocaust.cz/en/database-of-digitised-documents/document/89750-dz-terezin-auschwitz/; https://www.holocaust.cz/en/history/theresienstadt/; https://collections.yadvashem.org/en/deportations/12711679; https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/autumn-transports-of-1944-from-the-ghetto-to-the-east-reflected-in-prisoners-recollections
  - Disagreements / caveats: Transport Dz (May 15, 1944): 2,503 deportees (holocaust.cz victim profile summaries also note 2,501 / 2,503; digitised document 89750-dz-terezin-auschwitz); Transport Ea (May 16, 1944): 2,500 deportees (40 survived, 2,460 murdered per holocaust.cz); Transport Eb (May 18, 1944): 2,500 deportees (301 survived, 2,199 murdered per holocaust.cz). Combined total = 7,503 deportees.
- **[HIGH]** September 28 - October 28, 1944 autumn liquidation transports from Theresienstadt to Auschwitz-Birkenau (11 transports: Ek through Et, plus Ev): `18,402 (11 transports: Ek-Et, Ev)` (deportees across 11 transports)
  - Sources (4): https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/autumn-transports-of-1944-from-the-ghetto-to-the-east-reflected-in-prisoners-recollections; https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/pietni-shromazdeni-u-prilezitosti-65-vyroci-transportu-ze-16-rijna; https://www.holocaust.cz/zdroje/clanky-z-holocaust-cz/posledni-transporty-a-konec-valky-v-terezine/; https://www.holocaust.cz/en/history/theresienstadt/
  - Disagreements / caveats: Exact 11 Terezín-to-Auschwitz transports skip code 'Eu' (Oct 27, 1944, Prague -> Auschwitz, 18 deportees, all murdered). The 11 Terezín transports total 18,402 per holocaust.cz and Terezín Memorial (Památník Terezín): Ek (Sept 28: 2,499; one holocaust.cz page lists 2,500 with 477 survived/2,023 murdered), El (Sept 29: 1,500), Em (Oct 1: 1,500), En (Oct 4: 1,500), Eo (Oct 6: 1,550), Ep (Oct 9: 1,600; one snippet notes 1,500), Eq (Oct 12: 1,500), Er (Oct 16: 1,500), Es (Oct 19: 1,500), Et (Oct 23: 1,715 [~1,714-1,715]; ~197 survived), Ev (Oct 28: 2,038 [2,035 on individual holocaust.cz transport page, 163 survived/1,872 murdered]). Note: holocaust.cz overview rounds total to 'na 18400 lidí'.
- **[MEDIUM]** Survivors from the 18,402 deportees sent from Theresienstadt to Auschwitz-Birkenau (Sept 28 - Oct 28, 1944): `1,574 (secondary tallies ~1,474-1,574)` (survivors)
  - Sources (3): https://www.holocaust.cz/zdroje/clanky-z-holocaust-cz/posledni-transporty-a-konec-valky-v-terezine/; https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/autumn-transports-of-1944-from-the-ghetto-to-the-east-reflected-in-prisoners-recollections; https://www.pamatnik-terezin.cz/pietni-shromazdeni-u-prilezitosti-65-vyroci-transportu-ze-16-rijna
  - Disagreements / caveats: Památník Terezín and holocaust.cz ('posledni-transporty-a-konec-valky-v-terezine') both report 1,574 survivors ('Z nich přežilo jen 1574 osob', <10%). Secondary tallies occasionally cite a range (~1,474 to 1,574).
- **[HIGH]** Paul Eppstein's coerced 'mayor' role, black eye (blaues Auge), scripted speech (June 23, 1944), and arrest/execution (Sept 27-28, 1944): `June 23, 1944 (inspection); Sept 27-28, 1944 (arrest & execution)` (historical event dates & details)
  - Sources (5): https://www.gdw-berlin.de/en/recess/biographies/index_of_biographies/biographie-detail/paul-eppstein/; https://www.apparatusjournal.net/index.php/apparatus/article/view/29; https://www.apparatusjournal.net/index.php/apparatus/article/view/34; https://www.apparatusjournal.net/index.php/apparatus/article/view/51; https://www.apparatusjournal.net/index.php/apparatus/article/view/52
  - Disagreements / caveats: On June 23, 1944, 2nd Judenältester Paul Eppstein was forced to wear a morning coat and top hat, ride in a limousine driven by an SS officer posing as a chauffeur, and deliver a scripted speech portraying Theresienstadt as a normal town with no deportations, while bearing a visible black eye from a beating by SS Commandant Karl Rahm days earlier (documented in scholarship e.g. Cambridge University Press / Hájková; H.G. Adler). Note: apparatusjournal.net articles (view/29, 34, 51, 52) examine Eppstein's forced welcoming speech in propaganda film shoots (Jan 20, 1944 and summer 1944) and his Sept 1944 New Year's speech, NOT the black-eye detail. Eppstein was arrested by the Gestapo/SS on Sept 27, 1944 and shot at the Small Fortress (Kleine Festung) on Sept 27 or Sept 28, 1944 (exact execution date Sept 27 vs Sept 28 varies in records per gdw-berlin.de).
- **Myths checked:**
  - Claim that '36 photos' is the confirmed ICRC archive count: ICRC Cross-Files (Daniel Palmieri, 2026) and V-P-HIST catalog actually confirm 38 photos, whereas '36' derives from Farré & Schubert (2009) / standard 36-exposure 35mm Leica film roll size (and ~16 prints were attached to report B G 59/12-368.02).
  - Assumption that the 11 autumn 1944 Terezín-to-Auschwitz transports were consecutive Ek through Eu: Transport Eu (Oct 27, 1944, 18 deportees) went from Prague to Auschwitz; the 11 Terezín transports were Ek through Et plus Ev.
  - Assumption that 4 vs 6 vs 8 hours are contradictory errors: 4 hours (holocaust.cz, ~12:00-16:00) reflects the guided ghetto tour; 6 hours (USHMM main articles) and 8 hours (USHMM Red Cross article, ~11:00-19:00) reflect broader on-site/itinerary windows.
  - Assumption that apparatusjournal.net documents Paul Eppstein's black eye: apparatusjournal.net covers Eppstein's forced film welcoming speeches and Sept 1944 New Year's speech, while the black eye from Karl Rahm's beating appears in broader Theresienstadt historiography (e.g., Cambridge UP, H.G. Adler).

## p2_gap_butterfly_vedem — Verification of Czech original text and publication history of Pavel Friedmann's 'Motýl' (4. 6. 1942), verbatim metadata/text and OCR/typo artifacts on vedem-terezin.cz Rambles #22 ('Kasárna Kavalír'), and reconciliation of L417 Home 1 survival counts and survivor biographical dates (Zdeněk Taussig, Kurt Jiří Kotouč).

- **[HIGH]** Exact Czech original text of Pavel Friedmann's poem 'Motýl' (dated 4. 6. 1942): `Ten poslední, ten zcela poslední, / tak sytě, hořce, oslnivě žlutý, / snad kdyby slunce slzou zazvonilo / o bílý kámen... / taková, taková žluť / vznášel se lehce, tak do vysoka, / šel jistě, jistě, chtěl políbit / svět svůj poslední. // Na sedmý týden tu žiji, / ghettoisiert, / mí mě tu našli, / pampelišky tu na mne volají / i bílá větev v dvoře kaštanu. / Motýla jsem tu neviděl, / ten tenkrát byl poslední. / Motýli tady nežijí, / v ghettu.` (verbatim Czech poem text (17 lines, dated 4. 6. 1942))
  - Sources (4): jewishmuseum.cz; yadvashem.org; holocaust.cz; hmd.org.uk
  - Disagreements / caveats: Yad Vashem's pedagogical page prints 'my mě tu našli pampelišky' (orthographic/line-break variant for 'mí mě tu našli') and omits several commas; other Czech reprints match 'mí mě tu našli' (literally 'my [loved ones] found me here'). English translations render that line as 'But I have found what I love here' (Jeanne Němcová), 'Who loved me have found me', or 'But I have found my people here'. Also note deportation to Terezín: holocaust.cz records Transport Ao (no. 956) on 28 April 1942, whereas some English summaries (USHMM, HMD) state 26 April 1942.
- **[MEDIUM (1959 book publication HIGH; absence of pre-1959 Czech press printing LOW CONFIDENCE)]** First publication of Pavel Friedmann's 'Motýl' (1959 State Jewish Museum Prague edition vs. earlier postwar Czech press): `1959 (State Jewish Museum in Prague, 'Dětské kresby na zastávce k smrti: Terezín 1942–1944')` (publication year and imprint)
  - Sources (4): kjm.cz; upm.cz; ushmm.org; bnf.fr
  - Disagreements / caveats: Confirmed first major book publication was 1959 in 'Dětské kresby na zastávce k smrti: Terezín 1942–1944' (Prague: Státní židovské museum v Praze), edited by Hana Volavková with an afterword by Jiří Weil and catalogue/biographical notes by Olga Herbenová. General summaries occasionally mention 1950s Czech Jewish community press ('Věstník židovských náboženských obcí'), but no specific pre-1959 newspaper or periodical citation for 'Motýl' was confirmed in museum or library catalogues queried.
- **[HIGH]** Exact English translator credit in 'I Never Saw Another Butterfly': `Jeanne Němcová (sole English translator in McGraw-Hill 1964 / Schocken 1978 & 1993 editions; co-credited with Joy Kadečková and Edith Pargeter only in the 1993 Prague Jewish Museum edition)` (translator credit)
  - Sources (3): ushmm.org; mzk.cz; lieder.net
  - Disagreements / caveats: In the standard English editions ('I Never Saw Another Butterfly: Children's Drawings and Poems from Terezín Concentration Camp, 1942–1944' — Artia/McGraw-Hill 1962/1964, Schocken 1978, and Schocken/USHMM expanded 2nd ed. 1993), Jeanne Němcová is credited as the English translator. By contrast, a separate 1993 Prague Jewish Museum edition ('I have not seen a butterfly around here' / 'Motýli tady nežijí') jointly credits Joy Kadečková (Joy Turner-Kadečková), Jeanne Němcová, and Edith Pargeter as translators.
- **[MEDIUM (title, signature, Stiassny dates HIGH; absence of issue number/date on the page itself LOW CONFIDENCE)]** Exact title, Vedem issue/date metadata, and Pepek (Josef Stiassny) signature/dates on vedem-terezin.cz Rambles #22 ('Kasárna Kavalír'): `Title: Object 22 — 'Block E VII – The Kavalier Barracks' ('Blok E VII – Kasárna Kavalír') in 'Rambles through Terezín' ('Toulky Terezínem'); Signature: 'Pepek (Josef Stiassny)'; Josef Stiassny dates: 16 Sep 1916 – after 28 Sep 1944 (Auschwitz)` (page metadata and archival dates)
  - Sources (3): https://www.vedem-terezin.cz/en/rambles/22.html; https://www.vedem-terezin.cz/toulky/kasarna-kavalir; pamatnik-terezin.cz
  - Disagreements / caveats: No specific Vedem issue number or calendar date for that article was surfaced in the indexed text of 22.html / kasarna-kavalir (the site notes the 'Rambles around Terezín' / 'Toulky' series launched in February 1943).
