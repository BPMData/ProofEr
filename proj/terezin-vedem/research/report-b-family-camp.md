# Terezín family camp and Red Cross: fact-check

Nov 22, 2026 · @Bryan Murphy

## How to read this

The core figures hold up, but two popular claims do not: no source I read shows that the Red Cross requested or "canceled" a visit to Auschwitz, and no document shows Rossel's report was aimed at the Vrba–Wetzler report specifically.

- **Documented**: stated by a memorial, museum, archive-based institution or a historian working from documents, in a page I opened.
- **Interpretation**: a historian's reading of the evidence; no document states it directly.
- **Disputed / weak**: sources disagree, or the claim rests on speculation or a book review.
- **Secondary only**: I found the scholarly citation (author, book, page) through a secondary summary and could not open the original. Treat its page number as not verified.

What I could not open: the scholarly books themselves (Kárný, Keren, Kulka, Favez, Czech's *Auschwitz Chronicle*, Fleming), the Farré & Schubert article in *Le Mouvement social*, the second half of Farré's IRRC article, the Yad Vashem deportation database (it would not render), the Beit Terezín page (a site check blocked it), and the full text of the Vrba–Wetzler report's family-camp pages. Where these matter, a line says so. Wikipedia was used only to locate book citations, never as a source.

## A. The family camp (BIIb)

### 1. Transports, September 1943 to May 1944

Seven transports left Terezín for the family camp, in three waves. The Terezín Initiative Institute's victim database gives these counts ("deported" on each transport page, [holocaust.cz](https://www.holocaust.cz/en/transport/39-dl-terezin-auschwitz/), last change 12 Jan 2016):

| Transport | Left Terezín | Deported | Survived (database) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dl | 6 Sep 1943 | 2,484 | 32 |
| Dm | 6 Sep 1943 | 2,451 | 11 |
| Dr | 15 Dec 1943 | 2,519 | 278 |
| Ds | 18 Dec 1943 | 2,503 | 488 |
| Dz | 15 May 1944 | 2,501 | 134 |
| Ea | 16 May 1944 | 2,500 | 40 |
| Eb | 18 May 1944 | 2,500 | 301 |

The database totals are 4,935 (Sept), 5,022 (Dec), 7,501 (May), 17,458 overall. Other sources give different totals:

- **September**: 5,007 on two trains, Dl and Dm, on 6 Sep 1943 (Fedorovič 2023, p. 467). The database's 4,935 is 72 lower. The likely reason is that the database counts named individuals in its victim records, not the transport list; I could not confirm this.
- **December**: 5,007 (Kárný 1999, p. 11; secondary only). Database: 5,022.
- **May**: about 7,500 between 15 and 18 May 1944 ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/theresienstadt-red-cross-visit)); 7,503 (Adler, *Theresienstadt*, 2017 ed., p. 123; secondary only).
- **Overall**: 17,500 ([holocaust.cz family camp page](https://www.holocaust.cz/en/history/events/the-terezin-family-camp-in-auschwitz-birkenau/)); 17,517 (secondary only, no page found).

Arrival dates: the September transports reached Birkenau two days after leaving, on 8 Sep 1943 (Adler 2017, p. 48; secondary only). I did not verify the December and May arrival dates. Danuta Czech's *Auschwitz Chronicle* is the standard source for them.

### 2. Did anyone come from outside Terezín?

In the sources I opened, everyone deported to the family camp came on transports from Terezín, so Terezín's share of the deportees is effectively 100%. The difference lies in where people lived before Terezín:

- The September transports "consisted exclusively of prisoners who had come to Terezín from the Czech lands"; on later transports "almost half" were Jews first deported to Terezín from Germany, Austria and the Netherlands ([holocaust.cz](https://www.holocaust.cz/en/history/events/the-terezin-family-camp-in-auschwitz-birkenau/)).
- Fedorovič (2023, p. 467) also says the September transports were made up of Protectorate deportees. Kárný (1999, p. 10; secondary only) counts 124 German, 83 Austrian and 11 Dutch Jews among them, probably people living in the Protectorate when deported.
- Kárný (1999, p. 11; secondary only) gives the December transports as 88.5% Czech. Jahn (2007, p. 114; secondary only) says only 2,543 of the May deportees were from the Protectorate.
- The camp's prisoner functionaries are a separate question I could not source. Some may not have been Terezín deportees.

### 3. The March 1944 killing of the September group

The September group was gassed without selection on the night of 8 to 9 March 1944 ([holocaust.cz](https://www.holocaust.cz/en/history/events/the-terezin-family-camp-in-auschwitz-birkenau/): "on the night of 8 March"). The SS told them they were going to the "Heydebreck labour camp." Three totals circulate:

- **3,791**: Czech, *Auschwitz Chronicle* (1990), p. 595; Jahn 2007, p. 115 (both secondary only).
- **3,792**: Kárný 1994/1999 (secondary only). This is the figure in most Czech memorial literature.
- **3,752**: Fedorovič (2023, pp. 467–468, fn. 6), from Dr Wolken's "Quarantäne Liste" in the Auschwitz Museum archive (APMA D-AuII-3/1, inv. 29740). He calls 3,792 "erroneous."

The difference comes from the documents used: Fedorovič's figure is the newest and rests on a camp list. Before March, about 1,140 of the September deportees had already died in the camp (Bondy 2002 and others; secondary only). About 60 to 70 were held back from the gassing (secondary only).

### 4. The July 1944 liquidation

After selections for forced labor in early July, the remaining prisoners were gassed over two nights, 10–11 and 11–12 July 1944. Sources agree on the dates and differ on the numbers:

| Source | Selected for labor | Gassed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum](https://www.auschwitz.org/en/museum/news/the-60th-anniversary-of-the-liquidation-of-the-czech-family-camp-in-auschwitz,372.html), 2004 | 3,800 ("young, healthy men and women"), by Mengele "in the first days of July" | about 7,000 |
| Kárný, *Terezínská pamětní kniha* (1995), as summarized in Schur's 1997 review | 3,500 "sent to various plants" | "almost 6,500" |
| [holocaust.cz](https://www.holocaust.cz/en/history/events/the-terezin-family-camp-in-auschwitz-birkenau/) | "some" sent to other camps | "approximately 6–7,000" |
| [USHMM timeline](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/timeline-event/holocaust/1942-1945/czech-family-camp-at-auschwitz-liquidated) | "a number of selections" | "thousands," 10–12 July |

The Museum puts the camp at about 11,000 prisoners in May 1944. Its 3,800 + 7,000 matches that; Kárný's 3,500 + 6,500 implies about 10,000. Selection began on 1 or 2 July (Czech 1990, p. 656; Keren 1998, p. 439; secondary only). I could not verify which camps the selected prisoners went to.

**May transports among the dead**: I found no source giving how many of the July gassing victims came on Dz, Ea and Eb. USHMM says "most" May deportees were murdered on 10–11 July. The database records 7,026 of the 7,501 May deportees as murdered, but that figure covers every death, including people selected for labor who died later. It is not a gassing count.

### 5. Survivors

"Of the 17,500 prisoners sent to the family camp, only 1,294 survived" ([holocaust.cz](https://www.holocaust.cz/en/history/events/the-terezin-family-camp-in-auschwitz-birkenau/)). The seven database transport pages add up to 1,284. The two figures probably differ because of updates to the database. Most survivors came from the December and May transports; only 43 of the September deportees survived (database: Dl 32, Dm 11).

## B. Why the camp existed, and the Red Cross

### 6. What was the family camp for?

**No surviving SS document states why the camp was set up.** Historians agree it served to deceive the outside world. They disagree on whether it was kept ready for an actual inspection.

- **Concealment, documented in practice**: unlike other Jewish arrivals, the family camp prisoners did not go through selection on arrival ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/timeline-event/holocaust/1942-1945/czech-family-camp-at-auschwitz-liquidated)). They had to write postcards to Terezín and abroad. Fedorovič (2023, p. 468) calls the six-month stay a "decoy" and says the camp's "most probable purpose" was "primarily its role of concealment," while noting it "remained a subject of speculations to this day."
- **Pre-set death date, documented**: the September arrivals' records were marked "SB" with six months' quarantine. Historians read this as an order to kill them six months after arrival (Keren 1998, p. 429; secondary only). The March 1944 gassing came six months after the September arrival.
- **Show site for a Red Cross visit, interpretation**: Kárný, Kulka and Keren argue the camp was kept as a possible site for an outside inspection. Their evidence is that Himmler allowed a visit to a "Jewish labor camp," which they take to mean BIIb (Kárný 1994; Keren 1998, p. 429; secondary only). Prisoners' mail gave the address "Arbeitslager Birkenau bei Neu-Berun" (Kárný 1995, via Schur 1997). Holocaust.cz says only that "some historians also believe" this and that the camp's purpose is "still not altogether clear."
- **Hostages, interpretation**: Yehuda Bauer suggested the prisoners were held as possible hostages for negotiations. The only evidence for this is circumstantial (Bauer 1994, pp. 114, 118–119; secondary only).

Certainty: the deception role is well supported. The show-site theory is a respected interpretation but is not proven, because I found no document in which the SS plans to show BIIb to inspectors.

### 7. Did the ICRC plan or request a visit to Auschwitz in 1944?

**I found no evidence that the ICRC formally requested, planned or scheduled a visit to Auschwitz or the family camp in 1944.**

- The ICRC's own history says its approaches to visit the concentration camps "met with a categorical refusal" ([ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/icrc-wwii-holocaust), 2017). It names no Auschwitz request.
- The ICRC's documented requests concerned Terezín: one in September 1942, refused, and later approaches that the Germans "pushed off five times" before agreeing on 19 June 1944 (Palmieri, [ICRC Cross-Files](https://blogs.icrc.org/cross-files/theresienstadt-1944/), 2026).
- The "planned visit" in the literature is a German permission (Himmler's, as Kárný reads it), not an ICRC request. Schur's 1997 review of Kárný says the Terezín visit "may well have had the effect of persuading the ICRC not to proceed with the planned visit" to Birkenau. That is speculation, and it cites no document.

### 8. Did Rossel or another ICRC delegate visit Auschwitz in 1944?

Yes, Rossel went to Auschwitz once, in late September 1944, more than two months after the family camp was liquidated. He was not let inside.

- **Date**: 27 September 1944 ([ICRC](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/icrc-wwii-holocaust); [Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum](https://www.auschwitz.org/en/stop-denial/international-red-cross-visit/)). Palmieri (2026) says "end of September 1944." Kárný 1994 is cited for 29 September (secondary only).
- **Where and whom he met**: Auschwitz I, not Birkenau (Palmieri 2026). He spoke with the commandant or a staff officer and was not sure which ([Museum](https://www.auschwitz.org/en/stop-denial/international-red-cross-visit/)). Farré (IRRC 2012, p. 1382) writes "by his account, the camp commander."
- **What he saw**: Rossel "never entered the camp or spoke to the detainees" (Palmieri 2026). In his 1979 interview with Claude Lanzmann he said he went without authorization, on the pretext of bringing medicines, and saw emaciated prisoners (Peeters, [Mémoire d'Auschwitz](https://www.auschwitz.be/images/2022-peeters-maurice_rossel.pdf), 2022, p. 3). Lanzmann's 1997 film *Un vivant qui passe* records the interview.
- **What he reported**: I did not find a Rossel report on Auschwitz. Farré (p. 1391, fn. 44) cites McClelland's correspondence about the visit, 17 Nov 1944 (ICRC Archives G 59/40).

### 9. Did the June 23 visit lead to the July liquidation?

Some historians link them, but this is an interpretation, and others give different reasons for the timing.

- **Kárný and Kulka, interpretation**: once the ICRC accepted Terezín and did not press for a visit to Birkenau, the SS no longer needed the family camp (Kárný 1994; secondary only). Schur's summary of Kárný: the camp, "kept ready for some six months, was liquidated in July 1944, when it became clear that it was not going to be required."
- **Danuta Czech, interpretation**: BBC warnings in June 1944 probably delayed the liquidation until July (secondary only; the citation in the summary I used looked garbled, so check it before quoting).
- **Museums, no causal claim**: USHMM, the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum and holocaust.cz give the dates without saying the visit caused the liquidation. Holocaust.cz ties the camp in general to the Terezín visit and the effort to hide the genocide.
- **Timeline, documented**: Terezín visit 23 June; selections from about 1–2 July; gassing 10–12 July. Fedorovič (2023) treats the six-month SB date as the governing logic for the September group; whether the same rule set the July date is not established.

For the essay, "historians such as Miroslav Kárný argue" is accurate; "the Red Cross visit caused the liquidation" is not.

## C. The Vrba–Wetzler report and Rossel's report

### 10. How the Vrba–Wetzler report spread, and its effect

Vrba and Wetzler escaped on 7 April 1944 ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/timeline-event/holocaust/1942-1945/auschwitz-report)). Their report was written in late April 1944 in Slovak and translated into German ([FDR Library](https://www.fdrlibrary.org/vrba-wetzler-report)).

| When | Who received it | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Late Apr 1944 | Slovak Jewish Council, Žilina | FDR Library |
| By 3 May 1944 | Rezső Kasztner, Budapest | Braham 2000, p. 95 (secondary only) |
| Mid-May 1944 | A shortened version sent to Switzerland | [Yad Vashem](https://wwv.yadvashem.org/odot_pdf/Microsoft%20Word%20-%205784.pdf) |
| June 1944 | George Mandel-Mantello in Geneva, via Florian Manoliu | [USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/timeline-event/holocaust/1942-1945/auschwitz-report) |
| 14 Jun 1944 | ICRC, via Jaromír Kopecký | Fleming 2014, pp. 231–232 (secondary only) |
| 16 Jun 1944 | US State Department | Yad Vashem |
| 18–22 Jun 1944 | Swiss press campaign, picked up worldwide | [USHMM](https://newspapers.ushmm.org/events/first-public-reports-on-extermination-camp-at-auschwitz) |
| 18 Jun 1944 | BBC broadcasts parts of the report | Yad Vashem |
| Oct 1944 | First English translations reach the US government | FDR Library |
| 26 Nov 1944 | War Refugee Board releases the full report to the US press as *German Extermination Camps – Auschwitz and Birkenau* | USHMM; FDR Library |

The first publication was in the Swiss press, 18–22 June 1944. The first full publication was the War Refugee Board release of November 1944.

**Effect on Hungary, interpretation**: the Swiss press coverage "finally raised so much indignation abroad" that Horthy stopped the deportations in early July 1944 ([GHDI](https://ghdi.ghi-dc.org/pdf/eng/English45.pdf), p. 1). Horthy ordered the halt on 7 July and the trains stopped two days later (Rees 2006, pp. 242–243; secondary only). The FDR Library says the coverage helped spur international pressure, including Roosevelt's, and that deportations resumed in October. Historians treat the report as one pressure among several, alongside appeals from the Pope (25 June; Phayer 2000, p. 107, secondary only) and others; the FDR Library notes that scholars debate how much the reports changed the Allied response. By then, about 426,000 Hungarian Jews had been deported to Auschwitz (USHMM History Unfolded; no source given for the figure).

### 11. Did the report describe the family camp?

Yes, and it warned of the December group's coming death date.

- Yad Vashem: the escapees' testimony covered plans to kill "the 800,000 Jews of Hungary and 3,000 Czech Jews, who had been transferred to Auschwitz six months earlier" ([Yad Vashem](https://wwv.yadvashem.org/odot_pdf/Microsoft%20Word%20-%205784.pdf)).
- The report's text, as summarized from the GHDI translation (pp. 15–17; I could not read these pages word for word), describes: the September 1943 "family transports" from Terezín; their "special treatment" with six months' quarantine; the March 1944 gassing of about 3,791; and a December 1943 group whose "quarantine will be over on 20 June 1944." Check this against the War Refugee Board text before quoting it.
- On 16 June 1944 the BBC warned the Germans they would be held responsible for a planned massacre of Czechoslovak Jews in Birkenau around 20 June (Fleming 2014, p. 215; secondary only). One article on the subject gives the BBC date as 15 June.

The report gave a date (20 June) when the December group's six months ended. It did not, as far as I could see, use the word "liquidation" for the whole camp. The December group was in fact not killed on 20 June, but in the July liquidation.

### 12. How did German officials use Rossel's report?

Documented: Rossel sent his report "to Geneva and to the German Foreign Office" ([USHMM](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/theresienstadt-red-cross-visit)). Eberhard von Thadden of the Foreign Office's Inland II desk escorted the June 1944 visit ([Theresienstadt Lexikon](https://ghetto-theresienstadt.de/lexikon/thadden-eberhard-von), citing Adler 1960, p. 835).

- Kárný (*Terezínská pamětní kniha*, 1995, p. 47, as quoted by [Schur, H-Holocaust, 1997](https://www.h-net.org/reviews/showpdf.php?id=759)): Rossel sent photographs of the visit to von Thadden, who showed them at a foreign-press conference as evidence against "enemy propaganda on alleged irregularities in the treatment of Jews resident in Europe." No date or audience details are given.
- Farré (IRRC 2012, p. 1383): the Nazi authorities "used Rossel's visit to Theresienstadt and his subsequent report to present a distorted image of reality in the camps."
- Dawidowicz (1975, p. 134; secondary only): the RSHA saw the visit as a chance to cast doubt on extermination reports reaching the West.
- Part of Rossel's report reached the World Jewish Congress, which protested (Rothkirchen 2006, p. 259; secondary only).

**Was it used against Vrba–Wetzler specifically?** I found no document saying so. Schur's 1997 review writes that Rossel's description of Terezín as an "Endlager" (final camp) "placed in doubt the truthfulness" of the Vrba–Wetzler report. That is the reviewer's own assessment, with no document cited. The documented use is against "enemy propaganda" in general, at a time when the Vrba–Wetzler material was in the Swiss and Allied press. Farré & Schubert (*Le Mouvement social*, 2009, pp. 65–83) are the most likely source for the archival detail; I could not open their article.

## Myths and overstatements to avoid

- **"The Red Cross canceled a visit to the family camp."** Not supported. I found no ICRC request or scheduled visit to Auschwitz. The idea traces to Kárný's reading of a German permission and to a 1997 review's "may well have." Safer: "Some historians believe the SS kept the camp ready in case of an inspection that never came."
- **"Rossel's report buried the Vrba–Wetzler report."** Overstated. The Germans used Rossel's photographs against "enemy propaganda" in general. No document I found names Vrba–Wetzler. And the report was not buried: it ran in the Swiss press from 18 June and helped bring the halt of the Hungarian deportations in July.
- **"The Red Cross visit caused the July liquidation."** An interpretation (Kárný, Kulka), not a documented fact. Attribute it.
- **"Rossel inspected Auschwitz" or "saw the family camp."** False. He reached Auschwitz I on about 27 September 1944, after the family camp was gone, and was not let in. The Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum lists "the Red Cross inspected Auschwitz and found nothing" as a denial claim ([Museum](https://www.auschwitz.org/en/stop-denial/international-red-cross-visit/)).
- **Rounded figures given as exact.** For March, give a range or name the source: the sources say 3,752, 3,791 or 3,792. Use "6,500 to 7,000" for July, not one number.
- **"No selection in the family camp."** True on arrival and in March, but not in July, when 3,500 to 3,800 were selected for labor.
