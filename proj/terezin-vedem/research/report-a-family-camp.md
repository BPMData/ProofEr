## A. The Theresienstadt "Family Camp" at Auschwitz-Birkenau (Section BIIb)

### 1. Transports from Terezín to BIIb (September 1943 – May 1944)
(a) Documented facts: Seven transports from Terezín arrived in sector BIIb across three waves, carrying 17,517 people upon departure from Terezín (Miroslav Kárný, Terezínský rodinný tábor v konečném řešení, Terezín Initiative Institute / Melantrich, 1994; Seznam transportů z Terezína, [invalid URL], 2016). (Other Terezín-to-Auschwitz transports—Dn [1,196 Białystok children] and Dn/a [53 caregivers] on 5 October 1943, Dx [45 people] on 20 March 1944, and Eh [10 people] on 1 July 1944—did not enter BIIb; the Białystok group was gassed on arrival on 7 October 1943, per Yad Vashem, Transport Dn/a, n.d..)

- Why counts differ: Departure lists from Terezín exceed Auschwitz registration totals by 43 in December 1943 (5,007 vs. 4,964) and 114 in May 1944 (7,503 vs. 7,389) because prisoners died in transit or arrived in such poor condition that the SS did not register them (Kárný, Terezínský rodinný tábor, 1994, Note 16). In summary materials, the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum (The organizational structure, n.d.) rounds the total to "about 18,000" (and in a 2004 press release wrote "about 20,000"), USHMM (The Theresienstadt (Terezín) Ghetto, 2026) cites "more than 17,000," and Beit Terezin (Dr. Margalith Shlain, 2013) cites the exact departure sum 17,517.

### 2. Non-Terezín Prisoners in BIIb and Share from Terezín
(a) Documented facts: During its existence as the family camp (September 1943 – July 1944), over 99.5% of BIIb's ~17,517 inmates came from Terezín. Note that Terezín was a transit ghetto: while the September 1943 transports were overwhelmingly Czech Jews (with 127 from Germany, 92 from Austria, and 11 from the Netherlands out of 5,007, per Kárný, Kalendárium, 1994/2011), two-thirds of the May 1944 transports were non-Czech Jews previously deported to Terezín (3,125 German, 1,276 Austrian, and 559 Dutch Jews vs. 2,543 Czech Jews, per Kárný, Terezínský rodinný tábor, 1994, Note 19, citing H. G. Adler, Theresienstadt 1941–1945, 1960, pp. 698–699).

The only documented non-Terezín inhabitants during the family camp's existence were a small, unquantified number (at most a few dozen) of prisoner functionaries transferred from other parts of Auschwitz—German criminal Lagerälteste Arno Böhm and Wilhelm ("Willy") Brachmann, initial Polish Blockälteste (later largely replaced by Terezín Jews), and a commando of German and Polish Kapos brought in on 8 March 1944 (Beit Terezin, 2013; Hermann Langbein, People in Auschwitz, 2005; Kárný, Kalendárium, 1994/2011)—plus babies born inside BIIb (Encyclopaedia GSR, citing Helena Kubica, Pregnant Women and Children Born in Auschwitz, p. 12). After the 12 July 1944 liquidation, BIIb was reused for Polish civilians from Pruszków and female prisoners from BIb (Auschwitz Museum, 2004).

### 3. The March 1944 Killing of the September 1943 Group
(a) Documented facts: On 7 March 1944, the September 1943 survivors were separated from the December arrivals and moved to quarantine sector BIIa; on the night of 8–9 March 1944, they were trucked to Crematoria II and III and gassed (Kárný, Kalendárium, 1994/2011; USHMM, Theresienstadt: Key Dates, 2026). (Some early testimonies and the 16 June 1944 BBC broadcast misdated the gassing to 7 March 1944.)
- Differing death tolls:
  - 3,792: Kárný (Terezínský rodinný tábor, 1994), [invalid URL] (Alfred (Fredy) Hirsch), and Beit Terezin (2013).
  - 3,791: Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum (Transport 263, n.d.) and Danuta Czech (Auschwitz Chronicle, 1990, p. 595).
  - Estimates ("almost 3,800" / "nearly 3,800" / "around 4,000"): USHMM (Theresienstadt: Key Dates, 2026), Auschwitz Museum (Mini Dictionary, n.d.), and Auschwitz Museum (Educational Lesson, n.d.).
- Why they differ: Of the 5,007 September arrivals, 1,140 (23%) had already died in the camp (Kárný, 1994, Note 7), and 40–70 people (medical staff, 11 pairs of twins, and artist Dina Gottliebová) were spared (Kárný, Kalendárium, 1994/2011). The 1-person gap (3,791 vs. 3,792) reflects whether underground tallies counted youth leader Fredy Hirsch, who overdosed on 8 March and whose comatose body was burned that night ([invalid URL]). The "4,000" figure is a rounded early estimate.

### 4. The July 1944 Liquidation of BIIb
(a) Documented facts:
- Dates: SS physician Josef Mengele conducted labor selections on 1–2 July 1944 (with a boys' selection around 6 July); labor transports departed 7–10 July, and the remaining prisoners were gassed over the nights of 10–11 July and 11–12 July 1944 (Yad Vashem, Prisoner No. 170421, n.d.; Toman Brod, Zamyšlení nad účelem rodinného tábora, 1994/2011; Kárný, Kalendárium, 1994/2011; USHMM, Theresienstadt: Key Dates, 2026).
- Selected for forced labor / moved elsewhere:
  - Estimate ~3,500 (Kárný, Terezínský rodinný tábor, 1994): broken down in Kárný's Kalendárium as 1,500 men (to Schwarzheide and Blechhammer), 2,000 women (to Stutthof, Christianstadt, and Neuengamme), and 80–100 boys (to Birkenau men's camp BIId; Auschwitz Museum cites ~90 boys).
  - 3,080 (Yad Vashem, Prisoner No. 170421, n.d.: including 1,000 men to Sachsenhausen, 2,000 women to Stutthof/Hamburg, and 80 youths to BIId).
  - Estimates "around 3,000" (Auschwitz Museum, Mini Dictionary, n.d.) or 3,800 (Auschwitz Museum, 2004).
- Murdered in the gas chambers: Because no exact SS gassing tally survives for July 1944 (Toman Brod, 1994/2011), historians subtract the labor selections from the estimated late-June BIIb population (~10,000–11,000), yielding estimates of ~6,500 (Kárný, 1994), ~6,700 (Beit Terezin, 2013), or ~7,000 (Auschwitz Museum, 2004; Brod, 1994/2011).
- May 1944 transports (Dz, Ea, Eb) share in the July gassings: No exact breakdown survives because December 1943 and May 1944 survivors were mixed together during the July selections (Brod, 1994/2011). Of the 7,503 May deportees, Brod estimates ~250 died before late June, leaving ~7,250 alive alongside ~3,250 December survivors (Brod, 1994/2011, Note 6). Because the May transports had high shares of elderly (3,391 over age 60) and children (511 age 14 or under), Kárný (1994) notes many of those gassed on 11–12 July were May deportees; across the whole war, 7,102 (Kárný) to 7,118 (Terezín Initiative) of the 7,503 May deportees perished.

### 5. Total War Survivors of the Family Camp
(a) Documented facts & differing counts:
- 1,167 out of 17,517 (~6.7%): 38 from Sept 1943 (Dl+Dm), 728 from Dec 1943 (Dr: 279; Ds: 449), and 401 from May 1944 (Dz: 120; Ea: 8; Eb: 273) (Kárný, Terezínský rodinný tábor, 1994, Note 33; Zeszyty Oświęcimskie 20, 1993, p. 202).
- 1,294 out of 17,517 (~7.4%): Terezín Initiative Institute (The Terezín family camp in Auschwitz-Birkenau, [invalid URL], 2011/2021).
- (The Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum, 2004 does not publish a separate BIIb survivor total, noting fewer than 4,000 survived out of all ~46,000 Jews sent from Terezín to Auschwitz.)
- Why they differ: Kárný's 1,167 relied mainly on postwar Czechoslovak repatriation lists; subsequent archival cross-referencing by the Terezín Initiative Institute across German, Austrian, Dutch, and Israeli records identified additional non-Czech survivors, raising the documented count to 1,294.

## B. Why the Family Camp Existed, and the Red Cross

### 6. Purpose of the Family Camp
- (a) Documented facts: No surviving German document states why BIIb was created (USHMM, 2026; [invalid URL], 2021). Camp records for September 1943 arrivals carried SB6 (Sonderbehandlung, six-month quarantine), prisoners wrote censored/postdated postcards under the cover address Arbeitslager Birkenau bei Neu-Berun, and ~7,500 Jews were deported from Terezín to BIIb on 15–18 May 1944 to de-crowd Terezín before the 23 June Red Cross visit (Yad Vashem, Transport Dl; Auschwitz Museum, The organizational structure; USHMM, Theresienstadt as a Tool of Nazi Propaganda, 2026). Neither former commandant Rudolf Höss nor SS-Unterscharführer Pery Broad (KL Auschwitz Seen by the SS) produced a founding directive, and Adolf Eichmann's 1961 Jerusalem testimony (after praising BIIb's children's block during a February 1944 visit) is treated cautiously by historians (H.E.A.R.T., Pery Broad excerpts, 2008; Kárný, The Genocide of the Czech Jews, 2000/2009; USHMM, Eichmann Trial, 2024).
- (b) Historians' interpretations:
  - Red Cross show-site / alibi thesis (widely accepted, debated in detail): Otto Dov Kulka ("Ghetto in an Annihilation Camp", 1984, pp. 315–330), Miroslav Kárný (Zpráva Maurice Rossela, 1996, pp. 188–195; The Genocide of the Czech Jews, 2000/2009), and Nili Keren ("The Family Camp", 1998, p. 429) link BIIb to RSHA–German Red Cross (DRK) correspondence showing Himmler approved an ICRC visit in May 1944 to Terezín and "einem jüdischen Arbeitslager" ("one Jewish labor camp"), which they identify as BIIb. However, [invalid URL] (2021) notes that some scholars question whether the SS could realistically have staged BIIb for visitors given inmates' emaciation and ~25%–33% six-month mortality.
  - Hostage / negotiation-leverage thesis (circumstantial): Yehuda Bauer (Jews for Sale?, 1994, pp. 80, 113–114, 118–119) suggests BIIb inmates may have been held as potential bargaining chips in Nazi–Allied negotiations, though direct documentary proof for BIIb is absent.
  - Anti-resistance motive: Kárný adds that after the Warsaw Ghetto Uprising, Terezín commandant Anton Burger targeted young Czech Jews in September 1943 to weaken potential resistance (Kárný, 2000/2009).

### 7. Did Anyone Formally Plan or Request a Visit to Auschwitz or BIIb in 1944?
- (a) Documented facts: From 1942–1944, the ICRC repeatedly requested access to concentration camps (Berlin delegate Roland Marti requested Terezín in autumn 1942 and November 1943) to secure Allied blockade exemptions for parcels; Berlin refused general camp access (Sébastien Farré, IRRC, Vol. 94, No. 888, 2012, pp. 1381–1408; ICRC, The ICRC in World War Two: The Holocaust, 2020). The Danish Red Cross and Danish government pressured Germany from October 1943 specifically to inspect Terezín, not Auschwitz (USHMM, 2026). Meanwhile, WJC representative Gerhart Riegner urged the ICRC to visit deportation/extermination sites (ICRC & WJC, Remembering the Shoah, 2015, pp. 18–21), and WRB representative Roswell McClelland funded parcel trials (Farré, IRRC, 2012). On 17 May 1944 (relayed 19 May), Himmler unilaterally offered ICRC/DRK access to Terezín and "one Jewish labor camp" (Kárný, 2000/2009).
- (c) Myth / poorly supported claim ("the Red Cross canceled a visit to Auschwitz"): False. The ICRC never planned, scheduled, or canceled a visit to Auschwitz or BIIb. As Kárný (2000/2009) concluded after reviewing archival and published records: "Nowhere, in any published or archival document known to me, did I find any evidence or even an indication of any initiative exerted in this direction by the International Committee." Both the ICRC (FAQ, 2024) and the Auschwitz-Birkenau State Museum (Myth No. 14, n.d.) confirm the ICRC never inspected Auschwitz II-Birkenau.

### 8. Maurice Rossel's 1944 Visits (Auschwitz, Terezín, Oranienburg/Sachsenhausen, Ravensbrück)
(a) Documented facts:
- Auschwitz (27 September 1944): On his own initiative, ICRC delegate Maurice Rossel traveled to Auschwitz on 27 September 1944 (ICRC, FAQ, 2024; Sébastien Farré & Yan Schubert, Le Mouvement Social, No. 227, 2009, pp. 65–83, para. 25; Kárný gives 29 September, and Claude Lanzmann's 1997 film misdated it to 1943, per Daniel Palmieri, ICRC Cross-Files, 2022). No other ICRC delegate visited Auschwitz in 1944. He was barred from entering the camp, never saw Birkenau, and met an SS commandant or staff officer outside the barbed-wire fence at the Auschwitz I Kommandantur, seeing only a few barracks and prisoners from a distance (ICRC, FAQ, 2024; Farré & Schubert, 2009, para. 25 & n. 45). In his report (29 November 1944), he repeated officers' assurances about parcel delivery, but also noted that detainees he questioned at nearby Teschen (Cieszyn) told him about gassing showers (douches de gazage) (Farré & Schubert, 2009, para. 25 & nn. 44, 46; Auschwitz Museum, Myth No. 14, n.d.).
- Terezín (23 June 1944): With Danish officials Frants Hvass and Dr. Eigil Juel Henningsen, Rossel toured the staged ghetto under SS/Foreign Office/DRK escort, held no private talks with inmates, took 36–38 photographs, and filed his 27 June 1944 report calling Terezín an Endlager ("final camp") (Farré & Schubert, 2009, paras. 18–21, 26, 31–33; USHMM, 2026).
- Oranienburg-Sachsenhausen & Ravensbrück (Sept–Dec 1944): On 22 September 1944, Rossel met the adjutants of Oranienburg-Sachsenhausen and Ravensbrück; in early October (report 14 October 1944), he visited Ravensbrück, filing a "damning report" on its horrors, surgical experiments, and sterilizations, and describing Ravensbrück as a "50% extermination camp" compared with Auschwitz ("90% extermination camp") (Farré & Schubert, 2009, para. 25 & nn. 44, 47–49).

### 9. Do Historians Link the June 23 Visit or Rossel's Report to the July 1944 Liquidation?
(b) Historians' interpretations & disagreements:
- Otto Dov Kulka (1984, pp. 329–330): Argues for a direct causal link—that Rossel's Endlager verdict satisfied RSHA official Ernst Möhs, making BIIb "superfluous" and triggering its July liquidation (cited in Kárný, 1996).
- Miroslav Kárný (1994, 1996, 2000): Supports the causal link but corrects Kulka's chronology. Using Yad Vashem Archives (JM 1700/4), Kárný proved DRK liaison von Heydekampf did not read Rossel's written report until 10 September 1944 (briefing Möhs on 11 September). Still, Kárný argues the SS already knew by early July—from Rossel's 23 June dinner remarks, Heydekampf's 27 June memo (BAK R 58/89), and Rossel's 1 July letter—that the deception had succeeded (Kárný, 1996; Kárný, 2000/2009).
- Nili Keren (1998, pp. 428–440): Links BIIb to the Terezín deception while emphasizing that the July labor selections (unlike March) also reflected acute German wartime labor shortages.
- Jean-Claude Favez (1988/1999) & Sébastien Farré & Yan Schubert (2009): View the ICRC's role as a broader structural failure rather than attributing the July liquidation to Rossel's text. Farré & Schubert also show ICRC official Jean-Étienne Schwarzenberg doubted Rossel's Endlager claim by 19 July 1944 and withheld the report from distribution (Farré & Schubert, 2009, paras. 20, 43–47 & n. 96).
- Other explanations: No surviving SS order proves a single trigger; December 1943 arrivals reached their six-month SB6 mark in late June 1944, Danuta Czech (1990) notes the 16 June BBC broadcast may have delayed liquidation from 20 June to July, and Aktion Höss (Hungarian deportations) plus Hitler's April 1944 Jägerprogramm labor demand shaped the July timing (Kárný, 2000/2009; USHMM, Selections of Jews Upon Arrival at Auschwitz, 2026).

## C. The Vrba–Wetzler Report and How Rossel's Report Was Used

### 10. Timeline, Publication, and Effect of the Vrba–Wetzler Report (April–November 1944)
(a) Documented facts:
- Creation (7–27 April 1944): Rudolf Vrba and Alfréd Wetzler escaped Auschwitz-Birkenau on 7 April 1944, reached Žilina, Slovakia, by 25 April, and dictated/translated their 32–40-page report with Oskar Krasniansky by 27 April 1944 (GHDI, Report by Alfred Wetzler and Rudolf Vrba, 1944; USHMM, Auschwitz: Key Dates, 2026; Frank Baron, Hungarian Cultural Studies, Vol. 16, 2023, pp. 73–74).
- Distribution to Jewish organizations, ICRC, and Allied governments:
  - Žilina / Bratislava Working Group (25 April – mid-May 1944): Received immediately; Rabbi Michael Dov Weissmandel and Gizi Fleischmann sent coded bombing appeals to Switzerland in early/mid-May (Yad Vashem, Auschwitz Protocols, p. 1; Baron, 2023, pp. 73–74, 82).
  - Budapest / Kasztner & Jewish Council (late April – early May 1944): Given to Rezső Kasztner at the end of April and to Budapest Jewish Council members in early May, though not widely publicized by them (USHMM, Rudolf (Rezső) Kasztner, 2026; Baron, 2023, pp. 76–77).
  - Hechalutz & US War Refugee Board (17 May 1944): Reached Nathan Schwalb in Geneva and Roswell McClelland (WRB), though it sat untranslated in McClelland's files for weeks (Baron, 2023, pp. 82–83).
  - WJC / Gerhart Riegner & British Foreign Office (13–14 June 1944): Czechoslovak envoy Dr. Jaromír Kopecký shared it with Gerhart Riegner (WJC) and British Legation attaché Elizabeth Wiskemann on 13 June; Wiskemann cabled London on 14 June (Baron, 2023, pp. 83–84).
  - US State Department & OSS (16–20 June 1944): Summaries reached the US State Department on 16 June (Yad Vashem, p. 1) and Bern OSS chief Allen Dulles around 20 June (Baron, 2023, p. 84).
  - Jewish Agency (19 June 1944): Richard Lichtheim (Geneva) cabled Jerusalem on 19 June (USHMM, Auschwitz Report).
  - ICRC (by 23 June 1944): The ICRC in Geneva possessed the report by 23 June 1944 (Kárný, 2000/2009).
- First press coverage & full publication:
  - BBC: Broadcast a warning about the 20 June Terezín gassing on 16 June 1944 and excerpts of the report on 18 June 1944 (Yad Vashem, p. 1).
  - Swiss press (23/24 June – 11 July 1944): Following George Mantello's 24 June press release, 383–384 articles appeared in Swiss newspapers (Baron, 2023, p. 84).
  - First full English publication (25–26 November 1944): Released by the US War Refugee Board as German Extermination Camps—Auschwitz and Birkenau (after circulation to journalists/Congress on 18 November) (FDR Presidential Library, Vrba-Wetzler Report, 2016; USHMM, 1944: Key Dates, 2026).
- Effect on Hungarian deportations (July 1944): (b) Historians' interpretation: While 434,000–437,402 provincial Hungarian Jews had already been deported since 15 May 1944, historians attribute Regent Miklós Horthy's 6–7 July 1944 halt order (sparing roughly 150,000–260,000 Budapest Jews until October) to a convergence of pressures catalyzed by the report: an internal Hungarian translation reaching church leaders and Horthy, Swiss press publicity sparking warnings from Pope Pius XII (25 June), President Roosevelt (26 June), and King Gustav V of Sweden, intercepted uncoded cables from Wiskemann and Lichtheim (26 June) naming 70 perpetrators, and the 2 July Allied bombing of Budapest (Baron, 2023, pp. 78–89).

### 11. Did the Vrba–Wetzler Report Describe BIIb or Warn of Its Liquidation?
(a) Documented facts: Yes. Sections 12–15 of the report explicitly document BIIb (GHDI, Report by Alfred Wetzler and Rudolf Vrba, 1944, Sections 12–15):
- September 1943 transports & March 1944 gassing (Sections 12–13): Records the arrival of "Family transports from Teresin (Theresienstadt), which arrived during the week following 7 September 1943," describes Fredy Hirsch's children's school and the roster titled "Specially treated Czech Jews for six months' quarantine," notes ~500 elderly died, and reports that 3,791 prisoners (plus Hirsch) were gassed in March 1944 (dated 7 March in the report), leaving 11 sets of twins.
- December 1943 transports & 20 June 1944 warning (Sections 14–15): Records 3000 Jews arriving from Terezín on 20 December 1943 under the same six-month quarantine roster, lists c. 3500 Jews in II Section, b. as of April 1944, and explicitly warns: "Their quarantine will be over on 20 June 1944."

### 12. How German Officials Used Rossel's Report — and the Vrba–Wetzler Claim
- (a) Documented facts: On 1 July 1944, Rossel wrote to Foreign Office escort Eberhard von Thadden enclosing two sets of Terezín photos (and sent two sets to RSHA's Rolf Günther), stating his report would reassure people about Terezín's satisfactory conditions (Kárný, 1996, pp. 188–195). On 26 July 1944, Thadden replied he would use the photos when foreigners raised "alleged horrors in Theresienstadt," and the Foreign Office sent a set to the Swedish Legation in Berlin, where Thadden cited the 23 June visit to Legation Counselor Otter to argue "that the Jews were not doing badly" (Kárný, 1996, n. 15). Deputy Reich Press Chief Helmut Sündermann also cited Rossel at a 19 July 1944 press conference against "enemy propaganda" (Kárný, 2000/2009). As noted above, DRK liaison von Heydekampf and RSHA's Ernst Möhs did not inspect Rossel's written report until 10–11 September 1944 (Kárný, 1996).
- (c) Myth / overstatement ("German officials used Rossel's report to cast doubt on / bury the Vrba–Wetzler report"): Overstated as stated for German officials. Surviving German Foreign Office, RSHA, and DRK records show Thadden and Sündermann used Rossel's letter and photos to rebut "alleged horrors in Theresienstadt" and general atrocity propaganda—not to refute the Vrba–Wetzler report by name (Kárný, 1996; Kárný, 2000/2009). However, (b) historians' interpretation (Kárný; Farré & Schubert, 2009) notes that within the ICRC in Geneva, Rossel's Endlager claim objectively contradicted the Vrba–Wetzler warning and contributed to the ICRC's failure to press for an inspection of BIIb before its July liquidation.
