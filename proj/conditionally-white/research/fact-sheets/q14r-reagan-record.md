# Q14-R — The Reagan side of the diptych (Ballyporeen 1984)

Research worker fact sheet, 2026-11-24. Tools note: the session's shared WebSearch budget (200/turn) and WebFetch budget (400/hour) both ran out partway through this batch; items (3b) protests, (4) genealogy, and (5b–c) Boland/Iran-Contra/El Salvador could not be checked against any source and are marked UNVERIFIED with named leads. Nothing below is reported as read unless it was actually fetched.

## Headline findings
1. **"For this great-grandson of Ireland, this is a moment of joy" is NOT from Ballyporeen.** The Reagan Library's Ireland exhibit places it in the **arrival remarks at Shannon Airport, 1 June 1984**. The Ballyporeen text (3 June 1984) has no "great-grandson"; it does have "It is like coming home after a long journey." The brief (§ Chapter 7, line 151) and the pack's PS-12 splice the two speeches into one quote. Cite them as two events.
2. **The Ballyporeen speech never says "famine"** (APP text checked: no match). Reagan's own euphemisms are in it: "My great-grandfather left here in a time of stress" and "a descendant of people who are buried here in paupers' graves." These carry the "the script never mentions the famine" beat on the primary text itself.
3. **"Bum rap" exists but not in the San Pedro Sula remarks.** It is in a **Q&A with reporters aboard Air Force One** returning from San Pedro Sula, 4 Dec 1982: "And frankly I'm inclined to believe they've been getting a bum rap." (also "he is totally dedicated to democracy in Guatemala"). The San Pedro Sula remarks contain the "great personal integrity and commitment" line only.
4. **The "32 massacres … 96% Maya Ixil … just before the handshake" figure does not check out as written.** The CEH Conclusions (¶¶108–123) contain no Ixil massacre count and no 96% figure. The nearest sourced "32 massacres" (Victoria Sanford, Yale Genocide Studies Program, from CEH *Memoria* vol. 10) is for the **Ixil and Ixcán areas of northern El Quiché under Ríos Montt, March 1982–March 1983** (1,424 victims) — i.e. the period *spanning* the handshake, not before it; the March 1981–March 1982 Lucas García year had 45 massacres (1,678 victims). No source found for "96% of victims Maya Ixil." Use CEH ¶116 instead ("between 70% and 90% of villages were razed" in the Ixil region) or the 1,771 Ixil deaths charged at the 2013 trial.
5. **CEH genocide period is 1981–1983**, not 1981–1982 (the NSArchive case page says 1981–1982; the CEH text says "between 1981 and 1983"). Use the CEH wording.
6. **The 1986 anti-apartheid override dates/counts are confirmed**: veto 26 Sept; House override 29 Sept (313–83); Senate override 2 Oct (78–21).
7. **Michael Regan's emigration date is unverified here**; if the commonly repeated c. 1851 date holds, he left *during* the famine's last years (brief defines the famine as 1845–1852), not "after" it — the brief's "after the Great Famine" wording would be wrong either way. Reagan's own "left here in a time of stress" sidesteps the date problem.
8. Protests "kept outside the village": **not verified** (no Irish Times retrospective located before budgets ran out). Do not ship this clause until a dated source is in hand.

### R2a — CEH "acts of genocide" conclusion
- CLAIM: CEH concluded "acts of genocide"; conclusions ¶¶108–123.
- FINDING: Confirmed. ¶122 (English edition of Conclusions): "In consequence, the CEH concludes that agents of the State of Guatemala, within the framework of counterinsurgency operations carried out between 1981 and 1983, committed acts of genocide against groups of Mayan people which lived in the four regions analysed." It cites Art. II(a), (b), (c) of the Genocide Convention and intent "to destroy, in whole or in part" groups identified by common ethnicity. ¶110 lists the four regions: Maya-Q'anjob'al and Maya-Chuj (North Huehuetenango), Maya-Ixil (Nebaj, Cotzal, Chajul, Quiché), Maya-K'iche' (Joyabaj, Zacualpa, Chiché, Quiché), Maya-Achi (Rabinal, Baja Verapaz). ¶123: "The CEH has information that similar acts occurred and were repeated in other regions inhabited by Mayan people." Headline figures from the same document: ¶1 "Eighty-three percent of fully identified victims were Mayan"; ¶2 dead and disappeared "reached a total of over 200,000"; ¶82 State responsibility "93%"; ¶86 "626 massacres"; ¶116 Ixil region "between 70% and 90% of villages were razed."
- SOURCES:
  - Commission for Historical Clarification, *Guatemala: Memory of Silence — Conclusions and Recommendations* (1999), English edition, ¶¶1, 2, 82, 86, 108–123; PDF hosted by the US Court of Appeals for the First Circuit library: https://www.ca1.uscourts.gov/sites/ca1/files/citations/Guatemala%20Memory%20of%20Silence%20Report%20of%20the%20Commission%20for%20Historical%20Clarification%20Conclusions%20and%20Recommendations.pdf (read; OCR renders Ixil as "lxiI" in ¶110)
  - National Security Archive, "Guatemalan Genocide Case" page, quotes CEH "acts of genocide against groups of Mayan people" (but gives the period as 1981–1982). https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/guatemalan-genocide-case (read)
- STATUS: VERIFIED
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: "…agents of the State of Guatemala, within the framework of counterinsurgency operations carried out between 1981 and 1983, committed acts of genocide against groups of Mayan people which lived in the four regions analysed." — CEH, *Memory of Silence*, Conclusions ¶122 (1999).
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: The brief's operative phrase ("what the UN-backed Historical Clarification Commission documented as genocide in the Ixil region") is fair; the exact CEH term is "acts of genocide," and the finding covers four regions, Ixil among them. Use 1981–1983.

### R2b — "32 massacres in the Ixil region, 96% of victims Maya Ixil, just before the handshake"
- CLAIM: CEH documented 32 massacres in the Ixil region March 1980–Nov 1982, 96% of victims Maya Ixil, in the period just before 4 Dec 1982.
- FINDING: Not found as stated. (a) CEH Conclusions contain no Ixil massacre count and no Ixil victim percentage (checked every "Ixil" sentence; only ¶¶110, 116, 117). (b) The only "32 massacres" located: Victoria Sanford's table for the **Ixil and Ixcán** areas (northern El Quiché), from CEH *Memoria* vol. 10: Lucas García year (March 1981–March 1982) 45 massacres / 1,678 victims; Ríos Montt year (March 1982–March 1983) 32 massacres / 1,424 victims; total 77 / 3,102. That is a different region definition and a period that straddles the handshake. (c) The 2013 trial charged Ríos Montt with 1,771 Ixil deaths in 15 massacres during his rule (NSArchive; USHMM: "formally charged with the killing of 1,771 Mayans"). (d) NISGUA's summary of the separate Lucas García–period Ixil case lists 34 massacres, 1978–23 March 1982 (prosecution figures, not CEH). (e) Prosecution expert evidence at the 2013 trial: an Ixil person was "eight times more likely to be killed by the army" than a non-Ixil person in that place and time (ASIL). The "96%" could not be traced (search budget ran out before CEH vol. III/vol. 6 Ixil chapter could be checked).
- SOURCES:
  - Victoria Sanford, "Violence and Genocide in Guatemala," Yale MacMillan Center Genocide Studies Program, citing CEH, *Memoria*, vol. 10. https://macmillan.yale.edu/gsp/violence-and-genocide-guatemala and chart page https://macmillan.yale.edu/gsp/violence-and-genocide-guatemala-chart-3 (read)
  - National Security Archive, Guatemalan Genocide Case: "fifteen massacres … resulting in the deaths of 1,771 unarmed men, women and children." https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/guatemalan-genocide-case (read)
  - USHMM Genocide Prevention Blog, "Verdict in case of former Guatemalan military ruler charged with genocide," 14 May 2013. https://www.ushmm.org/genocide-prevention/blog/verdict-in-case-of-former-guatemalan-military-ruler-charged-with-genocide (read)

- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: "This was most significant in the Ixil region, where between 70% and 90% of villages were razed." — CEH, Conclusions ¶116.

### R2c — Ríos Montt trial history
- CLAIM: convicted of genocide 10 May 2013; annulled by Constitutional Court 20 May 2013; retrial; 2018 ruling that genocide occurred; died 1 April 2018 before verdict.
- FINDING: 10 May 2013: trial court found him guilty of "genocide and war crimes under Guatemalan law" (ASIL; NSArchive says "genocide and crimes against humanity"), sentence 80 years; co-defendant Rodríguez Sánchez acquitted; 718-page written sentence released 17 May. 20 May 2013: Constitutional Court voided the verdict 3–2 on alleged due-process violations, ordering the trial restarted from its 19 April stage. Retrial "finally began in October 2017" (IJ Monitor). He died in April 2018 and "the court closed the case against him" (IJ Monitor; exact day 1 April 2018 not read this session). The retrial court found (2–1) that the State, through the army, "committed genocide and crimes against humanity against the Maya Ixil population" during his de facto government, but acquitted Rodríguez Sánchez; IJ Monitor (published 28 Sept 2018) says the ruling came "Wednesday evening," i.e. 26 Sept 2018 (date inferred from calendar).
- SOURCES:
  - Naomi Roht-Arriaza, "Genocide and War Crimes in National Courts: the Conviction of Rios Montt in Guatemala and its Aftermath," ASIL Insights vol. 17 issue 14, 23 May 2013. https://www.asil.org/insights/volume-17-issue-14/ (read)
  - Jo-Marie Burt & Paulo Estrada, "Court Finds Guatemalan Army Committed Genocide, but Acquits Military Intelligence Chief," International Justice Monitor, 28 Sept 2018. https://www.ijmonitor.org/2018/09/court-finds-guatemalan-army-committed-genocide-but-acquits-military-intelligence-chief/ (read)
  - National Security Archive, Guatemalan Genocide Case (conviction; "Ten days later … overturned the conviction on a technicality"). https://nsarchive.gwu.edu/guatemalan-genocide-case (read)
- STATUS: VERIFIED (2013 dates, annulment, retrial, 2018 finding); PROBABLE (26 Sept 2018 date, death on 1 April 2018 — month confirmed, day not read)
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: none needed (one clause). Suggested clause: "convicted of genocide in 2013, a verdict the Constitutional Court annulled ten days later; he died in 2018 during the retrial, which found that the army had committed genocide against the Maya Ixil."
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: The "annulled on a technicality" framing is NSArchive's; ASIL's neutral wording is "alleged due process violations." Use the neutral one.

### R5a — ICJ, *Nicaragua v. United States*, judgment 27 June 1986
- CLAIM: ICJ ruled against US contra support and harbour mining; US withdrew/rejected jurisdiction.
- FINDING: ICJ case page: the 27 June 1986 merits judgment rejected the US justification of collective self-defence; found the US in breach of customary international law obligations not to intervene in another State's affairs, not to use force against another State, not to violate its sovereignty, and not to interrupt peaceful maritime commerce; found breaches of the 1956 Treaty of Friendship, Commerce and Navigation; ordered the US to cease and to make reparation. The 10 May 1984 provisional-measures order required the US to stop restricting access to Nicaraguan ports, "in particular, the laying of mines." On 18 January 1985 the US announced it "intends not to participate in any further proceedings in connection with this case." Case removed from the List by Order of 26 Sept 1991 after Nicaragua discontinued. The judgment's operative paragraphs naming the contras ("training, arming, equipping, financing and supplying") and the early-1984 mining were not read this session (PDF URL below).
