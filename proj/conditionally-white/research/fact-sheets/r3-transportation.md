# R3 — Transportation (F58, with F51/F53/F54 checks)

Round-3 worker sheet, 2026-11-24. Reads with `q11-transportation.md`, `gap-q11-q12-australia.md` and
`r2-scotland-australia.md`; nothing verified there is repeated except where this round adds a source or a correction.
About 27 page reads used; stopped there under the shared budget.

## Headlines

1. **"15 years" is not a transportation term.** The National Archives of Ireland (NAI) gives the terms as "seven years
   (the most common), ten years, fourteen years or life." Use 14, not 15.
2. **NAI's own transportation page gives "some 30,000 men and 9,000 women"** sent from Ireland over 62 years. That
   is about 39,000 people, not the "about 26,500 (NAI)" in F54. F54 needs to be reconciled before it ships (see the note under F54 below).
3. **A ship did sail from Ireland to Western Australia:** the *Phoebe Dunbar* left Kingstown (now Dún Laoghaire) and
   reached Fremantle on 30 August 1853. NAI calls it "the last ship to carry convicts directly from Ireland to
   Australia." The *Hougoumont* (1868) sailed from **Portland, England**, not Ireland, with 62 or 63 Fenians aboard.
4. **Horse theft in Ireland in the late 1840s was a transportable felony** (livestock theft, seven or ten years in the
   cases seen). **Whether it was still capital in Ireland after 1832 is still not settled from a primary text.** The
   1832 Act's title and scope were read. Its extent clause, which says whether it applied to Ireland, was not
   (archive.org's full text could not be reached past the index).
5. NSW: the Order in Council of **22 May 1840** revoked the 1824 orders **from 1 August 1840**, and named Van Diemen's
   Land and Norfolk Island (plus Bermuda hard labour) as the destinations.
