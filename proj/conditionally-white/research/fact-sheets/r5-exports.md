**Access this round.** No browser. About 75 page reads, nearly all through Internet Archive's full-text search
(`archive.org/services/search/beta/page_production/?service_backend=fts&user_query=...`). It indexes the
lending-only books that R4's search-inside couldn't open (Bourke 1993, Ó Gráda's books, Kinealy 1997 and 2002). It
returns short OCR snippets (about 100–125 characters) and **no printed page numbers**. Where a sentence or table row
below was assembled from overlapping snippets, it says "joined". The in-book search endpoint (`inside.php`) still
gives a client error to WebFetch. The decisive new source is the University of Southampton (Cockton) scans of the
**actual House of Commons returns** on IA (`op12xxxxx-1001`), which are public and were read as OCR text.
I didn't use Google Books, HathiTrust or web.archive.org. Avoided as sources: Fogarty's book, the Barnes Review
(a Holocaust-denial outlet that turned up in one search), the "genocide"/"illuminati" uploads, Wikipedia.

## Headline findings
1. **The primary returns are found and read.** Livestock: *Account of the number of live cattle exported from
   Ireland to Great Britain, 1846–49* (HC 1850, vol. LII, p. 229; Kinealy cites it as "1850, 423, lii"). Grain:
   *Return of the aggregate amount of provisions imported from Ireland into England* (three years to Jan. 1847; HC 1847
   vol. LIX p. 523) and the 1851 grain returns (HC 1851 vol. LIII p. 289).
2. **Ó Gráda's quarters table (Black '47 Table 4.1) is the series Wikipedia prints**, and Ó Gráda gives its source:
   "BPP 1852 (LI)". Its 1844–46 totals match the 1847 Customs return to the quarter. **That makes the yearly grain
   series VERIFIED for 1844–46 in the primary text, and SECONDARY (Ó Gráda, sourced to BPP) for 1847–51.**
3. **Two Bourke figures for 1847 exist, and both come from Bourke.** In his 1976/1993 series, 1847 is 146,000 tons
   exported against 889,000 imported (as Ó Gráda reproduces it). In a short table elsewhere in *The Visitation of
   God?* he gives 145,000 against 836,000, the figures Kinealy uses. Bourke's own text: exports were 130–140 thousand
   tons "depending on which returns are accepted". **Show 1847 imports as roughly 840,000–890,000 tons, never as one
   exact figure.**
4. **The live-animal series exists and is official in form, but it isn't Customs data.** The return says outright that
   it is "framed from non-official documents, collected at the ports of exportation, and consisting chiefly of printed
   market and shipping reports". Cattle exports **rose** from 186,483 (1846) to 201,811 (1849). Pigs **collapsed**
   from 480,827 (1846) to 106,407 (1847).
5. **"Over 3 million live animals 1846–50" adds up from these returns:** 3,129,014 head of all kinds (cattle, calves,
   sheep, lambs and pigs) for 1846–50. That is my own sum of the two returns. The total is real, but it's mostly
   sheep and pigs, the pig trade fell by about four-fifths, and the source is the return, not Fogarty.
6. **Correction: calves.** The return gives 6,363 calves in 1846 and 9,992 in 1847. That's **+57%**, not the
   "+33%" in the derivative chain.
7. **No official butter series exists for these years.** The Jan. 1847 Customs return says of provisions other than
   corn and cattle: "this department possesses no information whatsoever." The 56,557-firkin figure is in none of the
   three Kinealy books that IA's search indexes. Its only carriers found are the New Jersey Great Irish Famine
   curriculum (Mullin, 2nd ed. 1999) and copies of it.

## What changes in the master sheet
- **F39:** replace "series as printed by Wikipedia" with **Ó Gráda, *Black '47 and Beyond* (1999), Table 4.1
  (source: BPP 1852 LI)**, with 1844–46 verified against HC 1847 vol. LIX p. 523. Add Bourke's ton series (via Ó Gráda
  1989/2006) as the second compilation. Say they are two compilers of the same official returns, not two independent
  data sources. Grain was officially recorded. For 1847, write "about 145,000 tons out; about 840,000–890,000 tons in".
- **F43:** a livestock row is now possible, VERIFIED, from HC 1850 [423] lii, with the compilation caveat. Cattle
  186,483 → 201,811 (1846–49, rising); sheep 259,257 → 324,179 (1847) → 241,061; pigs 480,827 → 106,407 (1847) → 68,053
  (1849). The 1846–50 total is about 3.13 million head (computed). Calves "+33%" is wrong; it's +57%.
- **F40:** stays secondary and tertiary. Add that no official butter series exists (HC 1847 return) and that the
  figure isn't in Kinealy's books. The safer butter line is Kinealy 2002's Liverpool butter in cwt (one chain).
- **For the chart (brief Ch. 4):** the documented "food leaving" story is **cattle and sheep holding up or rising
  while people starved, and grain exports continuing but outweighed by imports from 1847**. Pigs fell because the
  potato failed. An honest chart shows cattle rising and pigs collapsing on the same axis, with the note that the
  counts come from port reports.
