## Headline findings
1. **The Table 95 passage exists in Mitchel exactly as the pack quotes it**: Author's Edition, ch. VIII, **p. 72**. Mitchel's own footnote at "eight acres" reads "An Irish acre is to an English one in the proportion of eight to five, nearly." So the essay must not tell readers "eight acres" means 8 statute acres. 8 Irish acres ≈ 13 statute acres.
2. **The arithmetic is fair, but what Mitchel makes of it is his.** 192,368 families × the 1841 census average of about 5.55 persons per family ≈ 1.07 million. So "about one million of persons" is not inflated (the census inputs are from memory, see Q5-02). What *is* Mitchel's is calling a hypothetical calculation in a Digest table a "programme" ("Such was the Devon programme", p. 72). **The table itself (Kennedy's *Digest*, Table 95, p. 564) was NOT read this session: UNVERIFIED.** An Internet Archive search does confirm that two scanned volumes of Kennedy's *Digest of Evidence* exist (`digestevidencet00kenngoog`, `digestevidencet01kenngoog`).
3. **The "death-warrant" line refers to the *Digest*, not the 1845 Report.** On p. 71 Mitchel says "those volumes", meaning the Secretary's condensed octavo *Digest of the Evidence* with its preface signed "Devon". It reads "the death-warrant of one million and a half of human beings, and the sentence of pauper banishment against full a million and a half more." Caption it as being about the *Digest*.
4. **The pack's B27 wording is not Mitchel's.** The printed text (p. 219) reads: "Now, that million and a half of men, women, and children, were carefully, prudently, and peacefully slain by the English government. They died of hunger in the midst of abundance, which their own hands created;…". It does **not** read "A million and a half men, women and children were…". Use the printed wording.
5. **The aphorism's printed form uses a lower-case "famine"**: "The Almighty, indeed, sent the potato blight, but the English created the famine." (Author's Edition, ch. XXIV, p. 219). The pack and brief capitalize "Famine" and drop "indeed". Copy is word for word, so restore both.
6. **The 1861 vs 1876 date conflict is a book-history question, not a wording one.** The 2005 UCD Press edition (ed. Maume), on its title-page verso as catalogued by Internet Archive, says "First book publication in Glasgow by Cameron & Ferguson, 1861". The pack's "Dublin: The Irishman office 1861" is UNVERIFIED. The undated "Author's Edition" is the text read here. Its scan on Internet Archive is catalogued as Glasgow: Cameron, Ferguson, 1882, but the scan's own title page (OCR) names R. & T. Washbourne, Glasgow. Safest caption: "*The Last Conquest of Ireland (Perhaps)*, first published in book form 1861 (serialized 1858–59) — Author's Edition, p. 219", with the serialization dates still to verify (see Q5-06).
7. **O'Connell's "butchers… keeping Lent" survives only through Mitchel** in what I could check. Mitchel gives no date or speech for it (p. 68). No primary O'Connell source was found this session: treat it as SECONDARY (Mitchel's report).
8. **The 187 → 516 starvation deaths are Mitchel's citation of the 1851 Census Commissioners** (p. 68). The census report itself was not checked by me; another worker has it.
9. **"composed entirely of landlords" is Mitchel's claim** (p. 68). It is broadly accurate *as a description of landowners*: the commissioners usually listed are Devon, Ferguson, Hamilton, Redington and Wynne, all landowners (NOT FETCHED THIS SESSION). But one, Redington, was a Catholic landowner, and the names must be verified before they ship.

### Q5-01 — Devon Commission composition ("composed entirely of landlords")
- CLAIM (as the brief/pack states it): Mitchel: "It was composed entirely of landlords; the chairman (Lord Devon) being one of the Irish absentee landlords."
- FINDING: Mitchel's wording is confirmed on p. 68 of the Author's Edition ("It was composed entirely of landlords"). The commission's actual membership was **not read in a primary source this session**. Background from standard histories (NOT FETCHED THIS SESSION, verify before use): the Royal Commission was appointed November 1843. Its chair was William Courtenay, Earl of Devon (1777–1859; name and dates confirmed by the IA catalogue record for the Digest; an English peer with Irish estates around Newcastle West, Co. Limerick). The other commissioners were Sir Robert A. Ferguson MP (Londonderry), George Alexander Hamilton MP (Dublin University), Thomas N. Redington MP (Dundalk; a Catholic Galway landowner) and John Wynne (of Hazelwood, Co. Sligo). The secretary was Captain John Pitt Kennedy, who later compiled the *Digest*. If confirmed, all five were landowners, so "entirely of landlords" is substantially accurate. "Absentee" is fair for Devon, whose seat was Powderham, Devon. Mitchel's "landlords" is a fair description, but a hostile one.
- SOURCES:
  - John Mitchel, *The Last Conquest of Ireland (Perhaps)*, Author's Edition (Glasgow, n.d.), ch. VIII, p. 68. Read via the page-numbered transcription https://libraryireland.booksulster.com/Last-Conquest-Ireland/John-Mitchel-68.php (the URL number = page; checked against the IA scan, see Q5-06).
  - Commission membership: Report, HC 1845 [605] xix (NOT FETCHED; verify on EPPI / HathiTrust / the Digest's title page).
- STATUS: Mitchel's wording VERIFIED; the composition UNVERIFIED (this session).
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: "It was composed entirely of landlords" (Mitchel, *Last Conquest*, Author's Edition, p. 68).
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: Present it as Mitchel's description. Name the five commissioners only after they are checked against the Report's title page. Flag that one commissioner (Redington) was a Catholic and a reformer-minded Whig, so "a landlords' commission" is accurate, but "an Orange cabal" would not be.

### Q5-01b — The 1845 Report's major recommendations
- CLAIM: The essay implies the Report was a programme of clearance.
- FINDING: **Not read in the primary source this session.** Background (NOT FETCHED THIS SESSION): the Report (dated February 1845) recommended (a) compensation for tenants' improvements on quitting, which became Lord Stanley's Tenants' Compensation Bill of 1845, withdrawn under landlord opposition (Mitchel discusses "Lord Stanley's Bill" in ch. IX); (b) consolidation of very small holdings; (c) assisted emigration; (d) reclamation of waste land; (e) longer leases and better landlord–tenant contracts. It described Ulster Tenant-Right but did not recommend making it law (see Q5-04). The Report is often read by modern historians as moderate and improvement-minded, not as a clearance plan. Mitchel reads it as one.
- SOURCES: none fetched. To verify: *Report from Her Majesty's Commissioners of Inquiry into the State of the Law and Practice in respect to the Occupation of Land in Ireland*, HC 1845 [605] xix; Kennedy, *Digest*, Part I (1847); a standard history (e.g. Kinealy, *This Great Calamity*; Ó Gráda, *Ireland: A New Economic History*).
- STATUS: UNVERIFIED.
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: none.
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: If the essay keeps Mitchel's "programme" framing, put a counter-line beside it: the Report's flagship recommendation was compensation for improvements, and that was blocked in Parliament. Do not ship the recommendation list until it has been checked against the Report.

### Q5-02 — Table 95, p. 564: 192,368 families ≈ 1,000,000 persons
- CLAIM (pack/brief): "They refer to one of their Tables (No. 95, p. 564), where; 'The calculation is put forward, showing that the consolidation of the small holdings up to eight acres would require the removal of about one hundred and ninety-two thousand three hundred and sixty-eight families.' That is, the removal of about one million of persons."
- FINDING:
  - **Mitchel's text: VERIFIED** (Author's Edition, ch. VIII, "Conspiracy of Landlords and Legislators", p. 72). The transcription prints it as: "They refer to one of their Tables (No. 95, p. 564), where" — new paragraph, as an indented quotation — "The calculation is put forward, showing that the consolidation of the small holdings up to eight acres[*] would require the removal of about one hundred and ninety-two thousand three hundred and sixty-eight families." Then: "That is, the removal of about one million of persons." Mitchel's footnote at "eight acres": "An Irish acre is to an English one in the proportion of eight to five, nearly." The paragraph that follows: "Such was the Devon programme:—Tenant-Right to be disallowed; one million of people to be removed, that is, swept out on the highways, where their choice would be America, the poor-house, or the grave. We shall see with what accuracy the details were carried out in practice."
  - **The Digest is real and catalogued.** IA `digestevidencet01kenngoog` metadata: "Digest of evidence taken before Her Majesty's Commissioners of inquiry into the state of the law and practice in respect to the occupation of land in Ireland"; creators: Great Britain. Commissioners of Inquiry…; "Kennedy, John Pitt, 1796-1879, comp"; "Devon, William Courtenay, Earl of, 1777-1859"; "Dublin, Printed by A. Thom", 1847/1848; "Paged continuously"; "Known as the Devon commission; the Earl of Devon, chairman, John Pitt Kennedy, secretary and compiler of Digest"; "'Report' of the commissioners: p. [1103]-1172" (University of Michigan copy, digitized by Google; 716 images). So the Digest is continuously paged, and a p. 564 exists in it. The chair is William Courtenay, Earl of Devon (1777–1859).
  - **The table in the Digest itself: NOT READ, UNVERIFIED.** An Internet Archive search ("digest of evidence occupation of land ireland") returned two scanned items, `digestevidencet00kenngoog` and `digestevidencet01kenngoog` (Google scans of J. P. Kennedy's *Digest*). The budget ran out before I could open either. Note: "p. 564" in Mitchel refers to the *Digest*, the "Digest of the Evidence… with a preface signed 'Devon'" (Mitchel p. 71), and not to the four-volume Report. The next worker should open the scan with the Part I/II page range covering p. 564 and read Table 95 and the sentence around it.
  - **The arithmetic: fair.** Mitchel's conversion of families to persons (×≈5.2) is *conservative*. The 1841 Census gave 8,175,124 persons in 1,472,787 families, ≈5.55 persons per family (figures from memory, NOT FETCHED THIS SESSION; the census worker should confirm). 192,368 × 5.55 ≈ 1.07 million. So "about one million" is not inflated as arithmetic.
  - **The reading: partly inflated.** By Mitchel's own description, the Digest table is a *calculation of what consolidation would require*, a hypothetical. Mitchel turns it into a "programme" of removal. Whether the Commissioners endorsed consolidation on that scale has to be read in the Digest/Report text around Table 95 (UNVERIFIED). The "eight acres" are Irish (plantation) acres, ≈13 statute acres, which Mitchel himself footnotes.

- STATUS: Mitchel's quotation VERIFIED; the Digest table UNVERIFIED; the persons arithmetic PROBABLE (pending census confirmation).
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: "They refer to one of their Tables (No. 95, p. 564), where 'The calculation is put forward, showing that the consolidation of the small holdings up to eight acres would require the removal of about one hundred and ninety-two thousand three hundred and sixty-eight families.' ### Q5-04 — The Report on Ulster Tenant-Right ("It is even questionable…")
- CLAIM: The Commission treated Tenant-Right as harmful even to tenants; the pack quotes "It is even questionable whether this growing practice of Tenant-Right… be so in reality…".
- FINDING: Mitchel's quotation is VERIFIED in his text (p. 71). He presents it as a passage from the *Digest* / Commissioners: "The following passage treats this Tenant-Right as injurious to the tenant himself:—" then: "It is even questionable whether this growing practice of Tenant-Right, which would *at the first view* appear to be a *valuable assumption* on the part of the tenant, be so in reality; as it gives to him without any exertion on his own part an *apparent property* or security, by means of which he is enabled to incur future incumbrance in order to avoid present inconvenience—a practice which frequently terminates in the utter destitution of his family, and in the sale of his farm, when the debts thus created at usurious interest amount to what its sale would produce." The italics are as the transcription renders them, possibly Mitchel's emphasis. On the same page Mitchel says the Commissioners "term it 'unphilosophical;'" and that the preface says Ulster landlords and tenants regard it "in the light of a life-insurance". **The passage was not checked in the Digest/Report original**: UNVERIFIED at source. Check whether the italics are Mitchel's.

- STATUS: SECONDARY (verified as Mitchel's quotation; not verified against the Digest).

### Q5-05 — Registered starvation deaths 187 (1842) → 516 (1845)
- CLAIM: Mitchel attributes the rise in registered famine deaths, 187 (1842) to 516 (1845), to the 1851 Census report.
- FINDING: VERIFIED as Mitchel's citation, p. 68: "The Report of the Census Commissioners, for 1851, declares that" … "while in 1842 the deaths registered as deaths by famine amounted to 187" … "they increased every year, until the registered deaths in 1845 were 516". These are consecutive fragments; the exact joining punctuation was not captured. Mitchel adds that registered deaths were perhaps a tenth of the true number; that is his estimate, not the census's. **The census table itself was not checked by me** (another worker has the census). Caveat for that worker: the 1851 Census "Tables of Deaths" (Part V) count deaths *registered* from "starvation" by year. Pre-1846 figures are small because registration was by household recall in 1851.
- SOURCES: Mitchel, *Last Conquest*, Author's Edition, p. 68 (link above).
- STATUS: SECONDARY (Mitchel's citation verified; the census figure is with the other worker).
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: "while in 1842 the deaths registered as deaths by famine amounted to 187 … until the registered deaths in 1845 were 516" — Mitchel, p. 68 (ellipsis marks joined fragments).
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: Use only with the census table cited directly. Present Mitchel's "a tenth" as his guess.
