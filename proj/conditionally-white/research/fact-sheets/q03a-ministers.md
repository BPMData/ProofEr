# Q3a: Ministers (quotes wall)

Fact sheet by a research worker, 2026-11-24. Method: Historic Hansard (api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard) read through the WebFetch tool. Direct curl to Hansard was refused by the proxy (403), so passages were pulled as consecutive fragments of under 120 characters each and joined. **Caveat on method:** a small model extracted the fragments rather than me copying raw HTML. Before print, check each quote once against the Hansard page or the printed volume. One OCR-looking word ("now species" for "new species") shows the transcription is Hansard's own digitized text.

**Budget note:** this session ran out of shared web searches (200 per turn) and then web fetches (400 per hour) partway through. B12, B14 and B21 (Wood and Clarendon) could not be checked at all. A follow-up session needs to run them.

## Headline findings
- **B10 (Russell) is misquoted in the brief.** Hansard, 17 Aug 1846 (vol 88, c771–772) does not say "purchase of food in foreign countries... all trade would be disturbed." Russell actually said that *if it were to become the established practice* for the Government, "out of the resources of the Treasury, to purchase food for the people, and that this food should be sold by retail at a low price, it is evident that trade would be disturbed". "Foreign countries" comes from a different passage (c768) about Baring & Co.'s purchases, and the word "all" is not in the text. Use the real wording.
- **B10 context:** Russell also called the 1845–6 Indian corn purchase "a measure of great prudence". His objection is to making it "established practice". He also said government could not "adequately" perform "the duty of feeding the people". The essay should not present him as rejecting all food purchase.
- **B19 (Bentinck) is verified, but the date and wording differ from the brief.** The speech is HC Deb **29 March 1847**, vol 91, c598, in the Poor Relief (Ireland) Bill debate (cc575–613). The real wording is "though you Gentlemen may groan, and wish to conceal the truth, yet the truth shall and will be known". It is **not** "try to keep the truth down... it shall be known". It also says "what the amount of the mortality *is*", not "has been". The brief's version is a paraphrase circulating from a secondary source.
- **B12, B14, B21 and the Clarendon 1849 "policy of extermination" letter are UNVERIFIED this session.** The cause was the search and fetch budget, not any finding against them. No attribution was confirmed or refuted. Leads are listed under each item.

### B10 — Russell, Commons, Aug 1846: government buying food would disturb trade
- CLAIM (as the brief/pack states it): Lord John Russell, Commons, August 1846: "If the Government were to apply the resources of the Treasury for the purchase of food in foreign countries... all trade would be disturbed."
- FINDING: The debate is "DISTRESS IN IRELAND—MEASURES OF RELIEF", HC Deb 17 August 1846, vol 88, cc766–99. Russell opened the debate at c766. The relevant passage is at cc771–772, and the brief's version is a paraphrase or misquote. In the real text:
  - The condition is "if it were to become the established practice for the Government of this country, out of the resources of the Treasury, to purchase food for the people". "In foreign countries" does not appear here.
  - It continues "and that this food should be sold by retail at a low price, it is evident that trade would be disturbed". The word "all" is not there.
  - The words "from foreign countries" appear earlier, at c768, describing the Peel government's Treasury minute of 9 Dec [1845]. Under it, Indian corn was "to be purchased by the house of Baring & Co., either in the ports of this country or from foreign countries."
  - Later in the speech (c776): "We propose to leave that trade as much at liberty as possible."
- SOURCES:
  - Hansard, HC Deb 17 Aug 1846 vol 88 cc766–99, "Distress in Ireland—Measures of Relief", https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1846/aug/17/distress-in-ireland-measures-of-relief (read via WebFetch; cc768, 771–772, 776). PRIMARY.
- STATUS: VERIFIED, with a correction. The brief's wording is wrong; the passage itself is real.
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: "In the first place, I think that the supply of Indian corn in the emergency which happened, was a measure of great prudence; but if it were to become the established practice for the Government of this country, out of the resources of the Treasury, to purchase food for the people, and that this food should be sold by retail at a low price, it is evident that trade would be disturbed; that the supplies which are brought to us by the natural operations of commerce would be suspended; that the intermediate traders who deal in provisions in local districts would have their business entirely deranged; and that Government would find themselves charged with that which it is impossible they can perform adequately—I mean, the duty of feeding the people." — Lord John Russell, House of Commons, 17 August 1846, Hansard 3rd ser., vol 88, cc771–772.
  - Shorter cut: "…if it were to become the established practice for the Government… out of the resources of the Treasury, to purchase food for the people… it is evident that trade would be disturbed…" (same cite).
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: Quote the real text, not the brief's version. To be fair, keep "established practice" and his praise of the earlier Indian-corn purchase. The laissez-faire point still stands: he would not let the Treasury feed people or compete with the grain trade.

### B19 — Bentinck, Commons, 1847: "the truth shall and will be known"
- CLAIM: Lord George Bentinck, Commons, 1847: "The time will come when we shall know what the amount of mortality has been; and though you may groan, and try to keep the truth down, it shall be known..."
- FINDING: Found. The speech is HC Deb **29 March 1847**, vol 91, "POOR RELIEF (IRELAND) BILL" (cc575–613). Bentinck's speech runs cc595–598, and the passage is at c598.
  - Hansard records the interjection ["Oh, oh!"] immediately before it. That was the House's response to his charge that deaths "as has never before been paralleled in any Christian country" had resulted.
  - The wording differs from the brief in three places. Hansard has "the amount of the mortality is", not "has been". It has "you Gentlemen may groan, and wish to conceal the truth", not "try to keep the truth down". It has "the truth shall and will be known", not "it shall be known".
  - The passage continues with "the time will come when the public, and the world itself, will be able to estimate at its proper value your management of the affairs of Ireland."
  - No version with "keep the truth down" was found in any of the 1847 Bentinck Ireland debates checked. I checked Jan 25; Feb 4, 11 and 16; Mar 4, 9, 11, 22, 23 and 25; and both items on Mar 29.
- SOURCES:
  - Hansard, HC Deb 29 Mar 1847 vol 91 cc575–613, "Poor Relief (Ireland) Bill", https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1847/mar/29/poor-relief-ireland-bill (read via WebFetch; Bentinck at cc595–598, passage at c598). PRIMARY.
  - Hansard, Lord George Bentinck's 1847 contributions index, https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/people/lord-george-bentinck/1847 (used to locate the speech).
- STATUS: VERIFIED, with corrected date and wording.
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: "Oh, you groan, but you will hear again of this. The time will come when we shall know what the amount of the mortality is, and though you Gentlemen may groan, and wish to conceal the truth, yet the truth shall and will be known; the time will come when the public, and the world itself, will be able to estimate at its proper value your management of the affairs of Ireland." — Lord George Bentinck, House of Commons, 29 March 1847, Hansard 3rd ser., vol 91, c598.
  - Lead-in, if wanted, from the same speech: "…how was it possible there should have been any other result than that that should happen which has happened, and that there should die such a number of persons as has never before been paralleled in any Christian country?" (same cite, c597–598; exact column of the lead-in not pinned).
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: Bentinck was the Protectionist (Tory) opposition leader, not a minister. He is an opposition voice, partisan and attacking Russell's government. Label him that way, not as a voice from "inside government". He had his own agenda, including his Irish railways scheme.
