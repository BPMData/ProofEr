## Headlines

1. **F96 is now mostly settled, from a scholarly history.** Edward Sweetman, *Constitutional Development of
   Victoria, 1851–6* (1920), ch. XI "The Convicts Prevention Act" (pp. 146–165), gives the title: "An Act to
   facilitate the apprehension and prevent the introduction into the Colony of Victoria of offenders illegally at
   large" (Vict. Statutes, Act XIII). La Trobe assented on **23 September 1852**. The Duke of Newcastle's despatch of
   **30 April 1853** said "the Act had been disallowed" as "a practical interference with the Queen's prerogative of
   pardon." J. V. Barry, *The Life and Death of John Price* (1964), independently gives the number: "Convicts
   Prevention Act (16 Victoria No. 13)." So the number, date and disallowance have two sources each, or one
   scholarly source plus a matching index line.
2. **The Act was passed again and again in defiance of London.** Sweetman: the 1853 Amendment Bill passed its
   second reading 34 to 7, but La Trobe "did not assent". It passed "for the third time" on 15 Nov 1854, and Hotham
   assented the next day ("the Act was for one year only"). It passed "for the fourth time", with Macarthur's
   assent "on February 5th" (1856, by context). Sweetman likens it to "a kind of Defence of the Realm Act".
3. **Correction to R4:** the name "Influx of Criminals Prevention" was **used in Victoria in 1854**, not only in
   NSW in 1894. Hotham, 18 Nov 1854: "had I refused to affix my signature to the Influx of Criminals Prevention
   Bill, the people would have taken the law into their own hands" (Sweetman p. 154). It is still wrong as the name
   of the 1852 Act.
4. **F94: the reviews disagree on McCalman's number.** Alan Atkinson (*ABR*, Dec 2021, no. 438): "at least thirty
   thousand more, possibly half of all the men and women sent to Van Diemen's Land, crossed from the south."
   Quartly (R4) gave "more than 40,000". Neither page of the book was found (no archive.org copy; NLA and Google
   Books blocked). Print **"30,000 to 40,000 or more"** or **"tens of thousands"**, never "40,000" alone. A second,
   independent scholarly count for the 1840s: A. G. L. Shaw (*eMelbourne*): time-expired prisoners from VDL and NSW
   "numbered over 3000 in 1846 (about 15% of the male population)".
5. **F58 is still unverified.** No text read says whether 2 & 3 Will. IV c. 62 extended to Ireland. One lead
   points to yes (a 1862 legal annotator lists the Irish horse-stealing section and c. 62 s. 1 together), but it is
   a bare citation line. **F63:** no page found in Malcolm & Hall. The Dillon "white men" remark now has a second
   scholarly source, Bruce Nelson (2012), who cites Lyons's 1968 biography of Dillon. The year 1889 is still Marley's.
