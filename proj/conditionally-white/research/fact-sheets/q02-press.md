## Headline findings

1. **B3 (The Times) is real but the circulating wording is altered.** The text reproduced from Peter Gray's *The Irish
   Famine* (1995) reads "When the Celts once cease to be **potatophagi**, they must become carnivorous" — not "potato
   eaters". The "potato eaters" version (IrishCentral, Evergreen course site, the brief's B3) is a modernised
   paraphrase and must not be presented as the Times' words. Two education compilations date it **22 September 1846**;
   the brief's "September 1846" is consistent but less precise.
2. **B3 opening words differ between secondary sources:** "For our parts" (Singer handout crediting Gray 1995) vs.
   "For our own part" (NY State Education Dept. curriculum Document J). The original issue (Times Digital Archive) was
   not reachable; page/column UNVERIFIED. Use the Gray wording with a SECONDARY note, or get the issue image.
3. **B25 (ILN) is VERIFIED in the primary: *Illustrated London News*, 3 April 1852 (vol. 20, no. 552), probably p. 265**
   — the brief's "April 1852" is right; exact wording confirmed from the Internet Archive OCR (see entry).
4. **B13, B23, B26 have no scholarly or primary anchor found beyond the O'Dowd/IrishCentral compilation** (and a
   course website, Evergreen State College "Ireland 16-17", that prints the same list with month-only dates and no
   sources). None was traced to an issue/page in this pass. Status UNVERIFIED for all three.
5. **B23 (Newry Telegraph): the brief's ellipsis hides a clause that changes the meaning.** IrishCentral's text runs
   "It is true that the potato has failed in Connaught and Munster; **but it has failed just as much in Ulster;**" — the
   paper's point is that Ulster had the same blight but (it claims) coped through industry. Cutting "but it has failed
   just as much in Ulster" makes the quote say something the source doesn't. If used at all, keep that clause.
6. **B26: the "a hundred million" clause was not seen in any source checked.** The IrishCentral excerpt read here
   shows only "If this [exodus] goes on, as it is likely to go on…the United States will become very Irish..." and
   "We must gird our loins to encounter the Nemesis of seven centuries' misgovernment." Neither the Times 1860 issue nor
   *The Nation* May 1860 issue was located. Both links of the chain UNVERIFIED.
7. **Probable upstream anthology for B13/B23 (hypothesis, not verified):** John Killen (ed.), *The Famine Decade:
   Contemporary Accounts 1841–1851* (Belfast: Blackstaff Press, 1995) — a newspaper-extract anthology; a copy exists on
   Internet Archive (identifier `faminedecadecont0000unse`, lending copy). Searching inside it was refused. Checking it

### B3 — *The Times*: "we regard the potato blight as a blessing"
- CLAIM (as the brief/pack states it): "For our parts, we regard the potato blight as a blessing. When the Celts once
  cease to be potato eaters, they must become carnivorous..." — *The Times*, September 1846.
- FINDING:
  - The passage is reproduced in a longer extract (labelled Section C/D) in an Alan Singer teaching handout that
    credits "Peter Gray, The Irish Famine, (1995)" and dates the editorial *The London Times*, September 22, 1846.
    Wording there (read in the PDF, exact):
    - "Hence the universal prostration of self-complacent poverty and unrepining discontent."
    - "For our parts, we regard the potato blight as a blessing."
    - "When the Celts once cease to be potatophagi, they must become carnivorous."
    - "With the taste of meats will grow the appetite for them; with the appetite, the readiness to earn them."
  - The NY State Education Department Great Irish Famine curriculum ("Was The Great Irish Famine an Act of Nature?",
    Part Two, Document J, "Excerpt From an Editorial in The London Times, 1846") also dates it **September 22, 1846**,
    but prints "For our own part, we regard the potato blight as a blessing." Other sentences from the same extract in
    that document: "The Government was required to ward off starvation, not to pamper indolence (laziness)." [the
    parenthetical gloss is the curriculum's] and "The Irish peasant has tasted of famine and found that it was good..."
  - So: two independent-ish education compilations agree on 22 Sept 1846; both ultimately look to a document
    collection (Gray 1995 credited by Singer; NYSED gives no secondary). They disagree on "For our parts" vs "For our
    own part". "Potatophagi" appears in the Gray-derived text; "potato eaters" appears only in IrishCentral/Evergreen.
  - Original Times issue: NOT reached (Times Digital Archive paywalled; no free scan found). Page/column UNVERIFIED.
  - Scholarly works asked for (Gray, *Famine, Land and Politics* 1999; Kinealy; Woodham-Smith; Ó Gráda; Kerby
    Miller): could not open the relevant pages; no page citation obtained. Search for a Gray FL&P footnote returned no
    usable hit.
  - Reprints in other 1846 papers: NOT checked to completion (Papers Past bot wall; Chronicling America and HathiTrust
    search errors; Google Books quota exhausted).
  - Context worth knowing: RTÉ's "Their own wickedness" (Niamh Gallagher, 2020) cites other Times famine editorials of
    **15 September 1846**, **19 August 1846** and **12 October 1847** ("a case of permanent and inveterate national
    degradation") — confirms the Times' editorial line that autumn, but does not quote the 22 Sept piece.
- SOURCES:
  - *The Times* (London), editorial, 22 September 1846 — NOT SEEN (paywalled Times Digital Archive).
  - Peter Gray, *The Irish Famine* (London: Thames & Hudson, 1995) — credited source of the extract; NOT SEEN directly;
    page unknown.
  - Alan Singer, "13.8 British Response to the Great Irish Famine" (teaching handout), https://alansinger.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/13.8-british-response-to-gif.pdf — prints extract, credits Gray 1995, dates 22 Sept 1846.
  - Alan Singer, "AIM QUESTION: Why study about the Great Irish Famine, 1845-1852?", https://alansinger.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/10th_120.pdf — same "potatophagi" sentence, dated 22 Sept 1846, no page.
  - NY State Education Dept., Great Irish Famine curriculum, "Was The Great Irish Famine an Act of Nature?" Part 2, Document J, https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/gifcfamnature2.pdf — dated 22 Sept 1846; "For our own part".
  - Niall O'Dowd, IrishCentral (first pub. 2019, updated 19 April 2023), https://www.irishcentral.com/roots/genocide-what-british-ruling-class-said-irish-famine — "potato eaters" version, "September 1846", no source. NOT a source.
  - Niamh Gallagher, "Their own wickedness: how the British press reported the Famine", RTÉ Brainstorm/History, 17 Sept 2020, https://www.rte.ie/history/famine-ireland/2020/0917/1165825-their-own-wickedness-how-the-british-press-reported-the-famine — context only.
- STATUS: SECONDARY (date 22 Sept 1846 agreed by two education compilations; wording from Gray 1995 via Singer;
  original issue unseen). The "potato eaters" wording: UNUSABLE as a quotation.
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: "For our parts, we regard the potato blight as a blessing. When the Celts once cease to be
  potatophagi, they must become carnivorous. With the taste of meats will grow the appetite for them; with the
  appetite, the readiness to earn them." — *The Times*, editorial, 22 September 1846, as reproduced in Peter Gray, *The
  Irish Famine* (1995) [SECONDARY; via Singer handout; page not seen]. Note NYSED prints "For our own part".
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: Safe to use with footnote "as reproduced in Gray, *The Irish Famine* (1995)" and date 22 Sept
  1846; keep "potatophagi" (gloss it in the caption: "potato-eaters", in mock-Greek). Before shipping, one person with
  Times Digital Archive access (many public/university libraries) should confirm page/column and "our parts"/"our own
  part".

### B13 — *Waterford Freeman*, August 1846: "less sacred than the interests of the 'men in trade'"
- CLAIM (as the brief/pack states it): "The government does not propose to buy up provisions this year as was done by
  them last year, lest it would interfere with men in trade. The lives of the people are to be looked upon as things
  less sacred than the interests of the 'men in trade.'" — *Waterford Freeman*, August 1846.
- FINDING: Found only in (a) O'Dowd/IrishCentral (month-only date, no source) and (b) the Evergreen State College
  "Ireland 16-17" course site, which prints the same sentence plus three other *Waterford Freeman* items (Nov 1845,
  Oct 1846, Jan 1847), month-only, no page or book. The Evergreen list mirrors the IrishCentral list closely; it is not
  independent. No issue/page located. No scholarly citation found in this pass. Probable upstream: Killen, *The Famine
  Decade* (1995) (hypothesis; not checked — see Headline 7).
- SOURCES:
  - *Waterford Freeman*, August 1846 — NOT SEEN.
  - O'Dowd, IrishCentral (2019/2023), URL above — NOT a source.
  - Evergreen State College course site, "Eyewitness Reports of the Famine", https://sites.evergreen.edu/ireland1617/?p=175 — compilation, no source.
- STATUS: UNVERIFIED.
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: none.
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: Hold off the wall unless the issue is found (Killen 1995; Irish Newspaper Archives/BNA hold
  Waterford papers). The policy it describes is documentable directly: Russell's Commons statement of August 1846 on not
  buying food abroad (Hansard; B10/Q1 territory) is the stronger primary substitute. If used, caption as an Irish
  newspaper's editorial voice, not government.

### B23 — *Newry Telegraph*, March 1849: Ulster "painstaking, industrious, laborious people"
- CLAIM (as the brief/pack states it): "It is true that the potato has failed in Connaught and Munster... [Ulster's
  blessing because] we are a painstaking, industrious, laborious people..." — *Newry Telegraph*, March 1849.
- FINDING: Found only in O'Dowd/IrishCentral and the Evergreen course site, both month-only, no source. IrishCentral's
  opening reads: "It is true that the potato has failed in Connaught and Munster; but it has failed just as much in
  Ulster;" — the pack's ellipsis drops "but it has failed just as much in Ulster", which is the hinge of the argument
  (same blight, different outcome credited to Ulster character). The full "painstaking, industrious, laborious"
  sentence was not seen verbatim in any page read here (WebFetch excerpting limit). No issue/page located.
- SOURCES:

### B26 — *The Times*, quoted in *The Nation*, May 1860: "Nemesis of seven centuries' misgovernment"
- CLAIM (as the brief/pack states it): "If this [exodus] goes on... the United States will become very Irish... We must
  gird our loins to encounter the Nemesis of seven centuries' misgovernment. To the end of time a hundred million...
  will remember that their forefathers paid tithe to the Protestant clergy, rent to absentee landlords..." — *The
  Times*, quoted in *The Nation*, May 1860.
- FINDING: IrishCentral prints: "If this [exodus] goes on, as it is likely to go on…the United States will become very
  Irish..." and "We must gird our loins to encounter the Nemesis of seven centuries' misgovernment." attributed "The
  Times, quoted in The Nation, May 1860". Evergreen repeats the Nemesis sentence with the same attribution. The "hundred
  million" sentence was not seen in either page as read (WebFetch excerpt). Neither the Times 1860 original nor *The
  Nation* (Dublin) May 1860 issue was located. Search surfaced Goldwin Smith, *Irish History and the Irish Question*
  (1905) and Thomas Fleming, "The Green Flag in America", *American Heritage* 30:4 (June/July 1979) for the phrase, but
  in the portions read (Smith via Gutenberg mirror, ~134k chars, partial; Fleming pages 1, 2, 4) the phrase did not
  appear. Neither confirmed as an anchor.
- SOURCES:
  - *The Times* (London), 1860 — NOT SEEN; exact date unknown.
  - *The Nation* (Dublin), May 1860 — NOT SEEN.
  - O'Dowd, IrishCentral, URL above — NOT a source.
  - Evergreen course site, URLs above — compilation, no source.
- STATUS: UNVERIFIED (both links).
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT: none.
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: Do not use as a Times quote. The chain is doubly secondary (an Irish nationalist paper quoting
  the Times), and the "hundred million" clause has no source seen at all. Next step: *The Nation* 1860 is on Irish
  Newspaper Archives; the Times passage, if real, should be findable in the Times Digital Archive by "Nemesis".

### B25 — *Illustrated London News*, April 1852: "The potato failure is thus working a mighty revolution"
- CLAIM (as the brief/pack states it): "The greater the numbers who emigrate in any one year, the larger the amount of
  funds received in Ireland in the next... The potato failure is thus working a mighty revolution." — ILN, April 1852.
- FINDING: **Found in the primary.** *Illustrated London News*, vol. 20, no. 552, **3 April 1852**, unheaded leader-page
  paragraph on Irish and Highland emigration, which opens (OCR) "What has been called the 'Exodus of the Celtic races'
  continues." In the archive.org OCR the passage sits just before the running head "266 THE ILLUSTRATED LONDON NEWS
  — April 3, 1852", so it is most likely on **p. 265** (confirm from the page image; OCR order is not proof). The
  brief's ellipsis "in the next..." stands for "to enable friends and relatives to follow to the land of plenty and
  independence." — nothing else intervenes before "The potato failure...". The next clauses continue the "revolution"
  list (landlords' property changing hands, "ten millions of money" spent by England on relief).
  The other April issues were also checked: 10 April (no. 553) and 17 April (no. 554) no hit; 24 April (no. 555) no hit
  in truncated text.
- SOURCES:
  - *Illustrated London News*, vol. 20, no. 552, 3 April 1852, p. 265 (probable; text precedes the p. 266 running head), Internet Archive scan `sim_illustrated-london-news_1852-04-03_20_552`, OCR text https://archive.org/stream/sim_illustrated-london-news_1852-04-03_20_552/sim_illustrated-london-news_1852-04-03_20_552_djvu.txt — PRIMARY, read via OCR.
  - O'Dowd, IrishCentral (2019/2023) and the Evergreen course site — carry the same wording, "April 1852"; not sources.
- STATUS: VERIFIED (primary, via OCR text; page number PROBABLE — check the page image for p. 265 vs 266).
- QUOTE-READY EXCERPT (OCR corrected only for obvious scan errors: "efllux" → "efflux"; "Wuar" → "What"; check
  against the page image before setting):
  "Year after year the efflux continues. Strong men that are the very life-blood of a nation, and that will become so
  to that great kindred nation of America, which is destined in due time to overshadow the world with its power and
  glory, leave our shores in countless multitudes. The greater the numbers who emigrate in any one year, the larger the
  amount of funds received in Ireland in the next, to enable friends and relatives to follow to the land of plenty and
  independence. The potato failure is thus working a mighty revolution." — *Illustrated London News*, 3 April 1852,
  vol. 20, p. 265 [page probable].
- NOTES FOR THE ESSAY: Safe for the wall with the exact date (3 April 1852). It is the London illustrated press's
  voice, broadly approving of the "revolution" (the same paragraph lists landlords ruined and relief spent), so caption
  it as commentary, not policy. The "ten millions" figure in the following clause is the paper's claim; don't reuse it
  as a figure without its own check.
