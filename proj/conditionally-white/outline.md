# Conditionally White: chapter outline

Status: DRAFT for Bryan's review, 2026-11-24 · built on `research/master-fact-sheet.md` (F-IDs) and
`research/quote-verification.md` (quote IDs) · prose comes later, via Gemini

The thesis, plot, tone and visual system stay as the brief sets them, with one calibration the research forces
(below). This outline keeps every beat the research supports, changes the ones it doesn't, and says why.
"Footnotes" lists the rows each chapter cites. The last section lists every brief claim the research contradicts
or can't yet support. Research is partial: a shared page-read limit stopped round 2, so many quote-wall items are
still open (see the quote sheet).

**The calibration.** The brief argues "policy premised on racialized contempt". The mainstream historians the
essay leans on (Ó Gráda, Gray) put doctrine first and contempt second, and Ó Gráda explicitly rejects "the
deliberate outcome of anti-Irish racism" (G1, F85, F86). Suggested thesis wording: **a famine made far deadlier by
policy, built on economic doctrine and hardened by a contempt that spoke the language of race.** "Racialized" stays,
but as the essay's argument, tested in Ch5, not as settled fact.

## Chapter 6 · The Bargain (paper, gold)
- **America side:** Draft Riots as "at least 105 dead, about 119 documented" (F47); the Colored Orphan Asylum's 233
  children all got out (F48); no percentage of Irish rioters exists, so use Bernstein's sentence (I1, F49). Police,
  machine and Union-army instruments are unverified (F50): keep the gauge's tick labels general until checked.
- **Australia side, the horse thief, rebuilt on what the records say:**
  - The menu had **three doors, not two**: Irish male transportation to Australia was **suspended from 1846 for about
    two years**, and men sentenced then often went to the **Bermuda hulks** instead (F51). From 1848, Van Diemen's Land
    was the main destination until 1853 (F53). Sentences nearly quadrupled, from under 545 a year to about 1,900 in
    1848 (F52), and peers in the Lords blamed "distress" and named sheep and cattle stealing (F52).
  - **Committing a crime to be transported is documented, but the documented cases are women** (the Kelleher sisters,
    Bantry 1848; Margaret Byrne, Carlow 1849) (F55). The government also recorded transportation as dreaded (F56). So
    the deliberate theft is plausible in kind and the essay says exactly that: legend, not record.
  - **What the record does and doesn't rule out, said plainly in the copy:** a man sentenced for theft around 1850
    most likely went **Bermuda hulks → Van Diemen's Land**, or straight to Van Diemen's Land from 1848 (F51, F53).
    Western Australia is unlikely for a famine-era Irish sentence, but its dates are still unchecked (F58). From 1848 the
    registers confirm the sentence, not the voyage, so "transported" rests on the family's word until a name is
    checked against the National Archives of Ireland's Ireland–Australia transportation index.
  - **The brief's "NSW closed 1840 / VDL 1853 / WA 1850–68 → most likely bound for the far side of the continent"
    is unverified and probably backwards** for a famine-era Irish sentence: VDL is where the Irish went (F53, F58).
  - Earl Grey orphans: 4,114 girls on 20 ships, probable (F57).
  - **Second generation:** Lambing Flat stays, but **the rioters can't be called Irish**: official sources say
    "European miners"; the documented Irish link is publican James Torpy and the Tipperary Gully meeting (F60).
    Eureka adds the Irish-heavy death list (F61). **The Stolen Generations "staffed by people who had fled caste"
    line is dropped**: no source (F62). White Australia items are unverified (F63).
  - **Castle Hill 1804:** "Death or Liberty" was a slogan; no flag exists (F59).
