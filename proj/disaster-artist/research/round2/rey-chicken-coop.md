# The chicken coop: provenance of the Portrait of Dr Félix Rey (research prompt 3, round 2)

## Verdicts
- **Not documented fact. It is an oral tradition with no named first teller I could find.** The earliest printed version I could actually read (Max Braumann, *Kunst und Künstler*, 1928) says something different: the portrait went into the garret and "took the place of a broken windowpane". It says nothing about a chicken coop.
- The chicken coop / hen house appears in the Pushkin Museum's own web text, as "the painting was first banished to the attic and later covered a hole in the chicken coop". The text is unsigned and has no source, and the museum's other page says it is a "tradition" (see Q3). Wikipedia and French/Russian popular versions repeat it, also without citation.
- Tellers vary: Rey's mother (DailyArt, artnet-style popular pieces, ar.culture.ru "the mother disliked it"), "his family" (French Wikipedia, and Feilchenfeldt-derived text), "the doctor's father" is a character in the Parinaud article. Uses vary: coop hole, windowpane in the garret, attic only.
- Not reached: Feilchenfeldt (2013, p. 306), the *Bulletin des amis du vieil Arles* no. 135 and the original *Kunst und Künstler* page. These are the likeliest places for an earlier telling. **Weakest point: I could not read any of the three, so "first in print" is unresolved.**
- Safest wording: "According to a tradition repeated by the Pushkin Museum, the portrait was kept in an attic and later used to cover a hole in a chicken coop." Do not attribute it to Rey himself.

## Q1. Who first told the story in print, when, in what words?
**Not found: no earliest telling identified.** What I did read, oldest first:

1. **Max Braumann (not "Hans"), "Mit Van Goghs Freunden in Arles", *Kunst und Künstler*, 1928.** I read only the English translation (Johanna van Gogh-Bonger, ed. Robert Harrison) on webexhibits.org, headed "From Kunst und Künstler (Art and Artists), 1928 (1926 Issue, p. 451)" (the page's own, inconsistent, dating). Braumann reports a visit to Rey with Prof. Julius Seyler. The portrait passage is introduced as coming from "Serret" (I could not tell who Serret is from the page), not from Rey. Quoted from that page: "He painted Doctor Rey's portrait, and made him a present of it." / "However, the doctor thought it so lacking in beauty that he put it in the garret." / "It is said that there it took the place of a broken windowpane, serving the purpose of keeping out the drafts." / "Afterward a friend who discovered it in the garret got hold of it..." No chicken coop. Note that the page shows the passage breaks off there.
2. **The Rey interview text itself** (vggallery.com, "Dr. Félix Rey, interviewed by Max Braumann (1928)", from *Kunst und Künstler*, "With Van Gogh's Friends in Arles"): Rey says of the portrait that Van Gogh "outlined [my head] entirely in green", painted hair and moustache "in a blazing red on a biting green background", and that he "was simply horrified." The fetched text had no chicken coop and did not name Camoin. (Another page on the same site, vggallery.com/photos/rey.htm, says Rey "never really liked the painting" and used it for years to repair a chicken coop. That is a gallery-site caption with no source, so it is a lead only.)
3. **Vollard / Camoin tellings:** not found. I searched for the story in Vollard's memoirs and in Camoin's accounts and found nothing I could read.
4. **André Parinaud, "Van Gogh au poulailler"** (INHA-hosted scan, no date or periodical on the page; signed "A. P."). French: "Elle avait quitté le grenier pour le poulailler, où elle servait à boucher une ouverture dans le grillage." Attic first, then the coop, where it plugged a hole in the wire fencing; Rey "se trouve « affreux »"; Rey's family mocked it. It adds a Vollard visit "to the Rey family" where the portrait was found in the henhouse and scraped in front of him, Vollard offering 50 francs, and Félix asking 150. This conflicts with the Camoin-first chain, and it also gets Rey's own life wrong in places (page says 1838 for 1888, OCR noise), so it is an embellished retelling, not evidence.
5. **French Wikipedia "Portrait du docteur Rey"** (lead): "his family" found it ridiculous and kept it more than ten years in a "poulailler" to plug a hole, then probably an attic; Camoin, a soldier at Arles in April 1901, bought it and placed it with Marseille dealer Molinard, then Vollard sold it for 150 francs. Footnote cited is *AVA – Bulletin des amis du vieil Arles*, no. 135 (no author or page given). This is the closest I got to a primary citation and I could not read it.
6. **English Wikipedia** (lead): Rey's family "found the portrait unrealistic", used it "more than ten years" to plug a coop hole. The coop sentence has no footnote on the page.

## Q2. Versions
| Who used it | For what | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Rey himself (put in garret) | Garret, windowpane "it is said" | Braumann 1928 (via "Serret"), webexhibits translation |
| Rey's mother | Patched a coop hole | DailyArt Magazine (Zuzanna Stańska, 2017, no sources); Open Forum Infectious Diseases article (Khoshbin & Katz, 2015) via davidfmarks.net blog; ar.culture.ru ("the mother disliked it") |
| "His family" | Coop hole, 10+ years, then attic | French and English Wikipedia (citation needed); e-vesti.ru (2025) says mother, coop, then attic until discarded April 1901 |
| Attic then coop (no one named) | Hole in coop | Pushkin Museum site (artmaecenas.pushkinmuseum.art) |
| Rey's family, then Vollard scraping it clean | Henhouse wire hole | Parinaud, "Van Gogh au poulailler" (undated) |
| Rabbit hutch, kept face to the wall | Not found in any source I read | none |

Camoin versions also differ: DailyArt says Camoin found it in Rey's backyard; ar.culture.ru says Rey sold it to Vollard on Camoin's advice; Pushkin's artmaecenas page says Camoin followed Van Gogh's tracks to Arles in 1900 and found it in Rey's yard; French Wikipedia says Camoin bought it in April 1901.

## Q3. Does the Pushkin Museum itself tell it?
- **Yes, on its own Russian-language project site, unsigned, no source.** artmaecenas.pushkinmuseum.art (footer: © State Pushkin Museum of Fine Arts, 2010–2016): "Картина сначала была сослана на чердак, а позже прикрывала дыру в курятнике." The same page: "В 1900 году художник Шарль Камоэн, друг Анри Матисса, поехал по следам ван Гога в Арль..." (spelled Камоэн). Provenance lines: owned by Dr Rey, Arles to 1900; 1900 Vollard galleries, Paris, and Cassirer, Berlin; 1908 bought by Shchukin from Druet for 4,600 francs; from 1948 GMII. The page names no mother and no family.
- **ar.culture.ru** (Artefact, Russian Ministry of Culture project, not the museum): «Существует предание, что картина была сначала сослана на чердак в доме Рея, а затем прикрывала дыру в курятнике.» ("There is a tradition that...") and «Известно, что она не понравилась матери доктора, но и сам доктор Рей не оценил подарка.»
- Not read: the museum's English "Portrait of Doctor Rey" collection page (pushkinmuseum.art/.../zh_3272) and the old printed catalogues. The site turned me away (robots disallowed), so I did not retrieve it. Curator statements: not found. Note the museum's dates (Camoin 1900, Vollard 1900) conflict with the 1901 dating in French Wikipedia.

## Q4. Physical evidence
- The Pushkin conservation site's X-ray page ("X-ray examinations of French impressionists' paintings") discusses this portrait and says the X-ray shows "chaotically scattered white dots: the traces of the primer steeping through the holes in the canvas", which it presents as hasty priming, not as damage. It mentions no patches, tears or repairs.
- A conservation report consistent with a coop patch (weather staining, paint loss, scraping): **not found.** The coop story is not supported or contradicted by anything I could read.

## Q5. Verdict
Family tradition or later embellishment, with no named first teller; not documented fact. The earliest telling I read, Braumann 1928, is a garret and windowpane story told second-hand ("It is said"). Honest one-sentence attribution: "A tradition, repeated by the Pushkin Museum without a source, says the portrait was first put in an attic and later used to cover a hole in a chicken coop." Avoid "Rey's mother did it" unless Feilchenfeldt or the AVA bulletin supports it.
