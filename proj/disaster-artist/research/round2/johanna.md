# Round 2 research: Johanna van Gogh-Bonger, the 1914 letters edition and her second marriage

Prompt: no. 2 in `gemini-prompts.md`. Researched 2026-11-24 with web search and page fetches.
Access note: vangoghletters.org and vangoghmuseum.nl returned only a "JavaScript seems to be disabled" notice (tried once each). DBNL's pages for the 1914 volumes are partly blocked by robots rules (not worked around). Quotes below are only from pages that actually loaded; where a fetch tool summarised a page, I say so.

## Verdicts

- **The draft claim ("she sanded the friction out of the brothers' relationship and made Theo a saintly caretaker") is not supported by any source I could reach.** No scholar I found says she cut money, Sien or Gauguin passages. No scholar calls her editing a deliberate softening of Theo. Verdict on "any scholar supports / partly supports / contradicts": none supports, none partly supports, none directly contradicts. The claim is untested, not refuted.
- **What is documented is narrower.** Van Gogh Museum scholars say her introduction "contains various errors and omissions"; the 1996 Journal says all older editions are "reworked and/or abridged"; one letter (416 / CL 347, Dec 1883) was left out in part (two enclosed sheets). That is all that is named.
- **One piece of evidence cuts against the claim.** The 1914 text on DBNL (letters 167-182, Jan-Mar 1882, as fetched) still prints Vincent's money complaints to Theo and the Tersteeg reproaches. See Q2. (Single source, read through a fetch summary, and I could not read the later Sien letters.)
- **Second marriage confirmed:** Johan Cohen Gosschalk, 21 August 1901 (announced then; one genealogy says Bussum), he died 18 May 1912 in Amsterdam. No later marriage found.
- **Biggest gap:** Luijten's biography and the 2009 edition's notes were not readable, so the essay should not state what she cut or why until someone reads them.

## Part A: the 1914 edition

### Q1. What did she leave out or change?

Documented so far (little):
- **Letter 416 / CL 347 (mid-December 1883): partial omission.** Van Gogh Museum Journal 1997-98 ("The Van Gogh letters project: new findings and old"; the fetched page names no author), says: "For completeness' sake it should be noted that Jo van Gogh-Bonger did not include either of them with the first part in her 1914 edition of the letters." The "either of them" are the second and third sheets that the 1990 edition printed as enclosed parts. Fetched page: dbnl.org/tekst/_van012199701_01/_van012199701_01_0006.php. This matches what the first pass found.
- **General: "various errors and omissions."** "Paper endures: documentary research into the life and work of Vincent van Gogh" (Leo Jansen, Hans Luijten, Fieke Pabst, Van Gogh Museum Journal 2002): the fetch summary says Jo's introduction "contains various errors and omissions." The summary also says V. W. van Gogh's 1952-54 edition restored passages "previously omitted for one reason or another." Note the "errors and omissions" phrase, as summarised, refers to her biographical introduction, not clearly to the letters. Fetched page: dbnl.org/tekst/_van012200201_01/_van012200201_01_0003.php. I did not read the full text myself, only the tool's extract, so check the exact sentence before quoting.
- **General: older editions abridged.** "'As it came into my pen': a new edition of the correspondence of Vincent van Gogh," Van Gogh Museum Journal 1996, pp. 88-101: "all existing editions contain more or less 'reworked' and/or abridged - sometimes drastically so - versions of the texts." The editors were "struck by the liberties that previous editors had taken." It does not name Jo (fetched page: dbnl.org/tekst/_van012199601_01/_van012199601_01_0007.php). This covers all editions before 2009, not hers alone.
- **French letters differ from the manuscripts.** 19th-Century Art Worldwide review (autumn 2010) of the 2009 edition: "Artemis Karagheusian had alerted van Gogh scholars to the differences that existed between the manuscripts and printed versions of van Gogh's French letters." It also says the 2009 edition re-dated letters "if necessary." Not specific to Jo.
- **Dates and order.** Jo's own preface (Amsterdam, January 1914, as reprinted at vggallery.com/misc/archives/jo_preface.htm): "Much time was necessary to decipher the letters and to arrange them," and because "often the dates failed," placing each letter took "much careful thought." So misdating and reordering were a known difficulty she herself named. No list of specific misdated letters found.
- **Names replaced by initials.** In the 1914 text the woman Vincent loved in 1881 appears as "K." (letters 153, 155 as numbered in 1914; fetched DBNL section 0010). I could not check the 1914 practice for Sien (see Q2), so I cannot say which names were initialled, nor who decided.
- **Whole letters by 2009 number:** not found. No source I could reach gives a list of letters wholly left out.

Where sources agree: the three Journal pieces and the review agree that older editions, including hers, were not complete. Confidence: moderate that omissions exist; low on what they were.

### Q2. Did she cut or soften money, Sien, Gauguin or quarrel passages?

**Not found.** No source I read names any such passage.
- Evidence against wholesale softening of money friction, from DBNL's text of the 1914 volume 1, section covering letters 167-182 (January to early March 1882, as numbered in 1914; the fetch tool's summary, not my own reading): letter 168, "Tot heden ontving ik niets, zelfs geen schrijven van U" ("Until now I received nothing, not even a letter from you"); letter 176, Vincent is "nog al erg om geld verlegen" (rather hard up); letter 179, Vincent is hurt by Tersteeg, who gave him f 10 "with reproaches"; letter 180, he would rather lose Tersteeg's friendship than concede; letter 181, he has repaid the f 10. Page: dbnl.org/tekst/gogh006brie01_01/gogh006brie01_01_0011.php. So money tension with Theo's circle is in her edition.
- Sien: the name did not appear in that section (Jan-Mar 1882) of the 1914 text, but Vincent's letters naming Sien start later (spring 1882), and the later DBNL sections (0012, 0013) were blocked by robots rules. **So whether the 1914 text mentions Sien, or how, is not found.**
- Gauguin: sections for Arles (1888) not read. Not found.
- Quarrels between Vincent and Theo: not found in any scholarly source. Letter 416 / CL 347 is the only named cut, and nothing I read says what the omitted sheets contain.

Confidence: low. This is an absence of evidence from a partial search, not proof that no cuts exist.

### Q3. What did she say about her editing?

- Preface, Amsterdam, January 1914 (vggallery.com reprint): she describes only the labour of deciphering and arranging ("Much time was necessary to decipher the letters and to arrange them"; "often the dates failed"). The fetched page says she says nothing there about selection or omissions.
- On the delay (as quoted in the 19th-Century Art Worldwide review): it "would have been a disservice to the deceased [Vincent]" to arouse interest in him before his work was known (the same sentiment appears in the 2002 Journal summary). Fetched in summary form; wording is the review's.
- Her diaries ("Mijn Dagboek", over 500 pages in four unpublished diaries, to 1897, ed. Hans Luijten; Doppiozero, Mariella Guzzoni, 27 January 2020): nothing found on her editing. Her own 1914 memoir of Vincent (the introduction to the edition) was not read. The webexhibits page I read is her son's memoir of her; it has nothing on her editing choices.
- Her own words on cuts: not found.

### Q4. Why were Theo's letters to Vincent not in 1914, and when first published?

- Few survive. Patrick Grant, *The Letters of Vincent van Gogh: A Critical Study* (Athabasca University Press, 2014), Introduction, "Assessing the Documents," p. 8: "Only 39 letters from Theo to Vincent survive, mainly because Vincent did not preserve his correspondence." Wikipedia (lead only) says 84 letters to Vincent survive, 39 from Theo, citing the Van Gogh Museum. A vangoghstudio.com post says "Vincent lost many of them" (weak source).
- So the plain reason for 1914 is that Jo had Vincent's letters to Theo (found in Theo's papers) and Theo's side mostly did not exist. Nobody I read states this as her stated reason; it is inference from survival, and the 2002 Journal says "Of course, Jo did not have all the correspondence to hand."
- First publication of Theo's letters to Vincent: the 2002 Journal ("Between two editions") says "1932 saw the publication of Theo's letters to Vincent." The Van Gogh Museum library catalogue confirms an item: *Théo van Gogh: lettres à son frère Vincent*, Amsterdam: Maatschappij tot verspreiding van goede en goedkoope lectuur, 1932, 127 pp., authors listed as Theo van Gogh and Vincent Willem van Gogh (library.vangoghmuseum.nl/Details/fullCatalogue/300023021). Both agree on 1932. Confidence: high on the date; the catalogue title is French and I did not see its contents.

### Q5. How do the 2009 editors and Luijten judge her editing?

- 2009 editors (Jansen, Luijten, Bakker): the sources I could read do not judge her editing in so many words. They criticise all earlier editions as "reworked and/or abridged" (1996 Journal) and Luijten et al. list "various errors and omissions" in her introduction (2002 Journal).
- Luijten's biography: no review I could reach quotes a judgment of her editing. Reviews: The Art Newspaper (2022) says only that she "published Vincent's letters to Theo in three volumes" and had "translated two-thirds" into English; De Groene (Dutch) calls it "de uitgave van de briefwisseling die zij zou verzorgen" without criticism; Zout Magazine says she "spande zich bijzonder in" for the edition; none mentions cuts. The 2021 editors, per John Banville in the NYRB (13 May 2021), say she "made a huge contribution to the recognition of Van Gogh's work."
- Not found: any Luijten quote on how she handled sensitive material. Needs the book itself (Dutch: *Alles voor Vincent*, 2019; English 2022).

## Part B: her life after Theo

### Q6. Second marriage

- To **Johan (Henri Gustaaf) Cohen Gosschalk**, born 3 November 1873, Zwolle; died **18 May 1912**, Amsterdam (UvA Album Academicum, albumacademicum.uva.nl/en/id/id021047). He was a law doctor (1896) and an art critic; the Bussum local history article says he had been a boarder at her pension and died "aged 38", buried at Zorgvlied.
- Marriage date: Bussums Historisch Tijdschrift 35/3 (2019) 40-46: "The wedding announcement appeared in the Algemeen Handelsblad on 21 August 1901." DutchJewry genealogy (duparc): "Married 21 Aug 1901 Bussum." English Wikipedia (lead): "August 1901", citing Luijten 2022 pp. 193-196. Place Bussum is from one genealogy only. Whether 21 August is the wedding or the announcement is not settled.
- Her son Vincent Willem's memoir (webexhibits.org/vangogh/memoir/nephew/5.html): "In 1901 she married Johan Cohen Gosschalk, a painter and writer on art, who was a good deal younger than she." The Art Newspaper (2022) calls him "the neurotic Johan Gosschalk" and the Zout Magazine review says the marriage "proved difficult." Note the sources disagree on whether he was a "painter" (memoir, Art Newspaper 2022) or a jurist and critic (Bussum journal, UvA).
- Later marriage: none found. Wikipedia: "widowed again in 1912 and never remarried" ([citation needed]). Bussum journal: she was buried beside Johan at Zorgvlied (died 2 September 1925, Laren).

### Q7. After Theo died: family, places, dates

- Theo died 25 January 1891 (Bussum journal). Doppiozero says she "returned to Holland as a young widow in late November of that same year" (the page is ambiguous about the year); the Bussum local history journal says she settled in Bussum in May 1891. Dates conflict, unresolved.
- Bussum: the Bussum journal says she began a boarding house at Villa Helma (Koningslaan 4, built 1888, rent f 600 a year) "on the advice of Anna Dirks" (wife of Jan Veth). Allesoverboekenenschrijvers.nl (review of Luijten) says "op aandringen van een goede vriendin Anna Veth-Dirks" she moved to Bussum.
- **Did her family urge her home to Amsterdam? Not found.** The only person named as urging the move is Anna Veth-Dirks, a friend, and the move was to Bussum. No source I read mentions parents or her brother Andries urging a return.
- Amsterdam: her son's memoir says "In 1903 we went to live in Amsterdam," where they lived 23 years in the same flat; the Bussum journal says they gave up the Villa Helma lease, had a house built at Regentesselaan 39 and moved to Amsterdam in 1904. Dates differ by a year.
- New York: memoir: "In 1915 she moved to New York, where she started translating Vincent's letters into English," back in Holland by 1919. Wikipedia (lead): Upper West Side and Queens 1915-1919, citing Shorto 2021 (NYT Magazine).

### Q8. Ages

- Jo was born 4 October 1862 (Amsterdam; DutchJewry genealogy and Wikipedia agree), so she was **28** when Theo died on 25 January 1891.
- Vincent Willem was born 31 January 1890 (Bussum journal). He was one week short of 1 year at Theo's death, so **11 months old**.
