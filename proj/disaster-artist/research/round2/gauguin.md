# Round 2 research: Gauguin in Arles (prompt 1)

Researched 2026-11-24. Method: WebSearch and WebFetch only. vangoghletters.org and the Van Gogh Museum letter pages returned only a JavaScript notice, so Vincent's letters were read on webexhibits.org (the 1958 Complete Letters translation, old numbering). Where a 2009-edition number is given it comes from a catalogue or page that states it; any mapping I inferred is marked "inferred". Page summaries came through a fetch tool that paraphrases; text in quotation marks is what the tool returned as quoted from the page.

## Verdicts

- **"Unsalable" / "invendable": NOT FOUND** in any Gauguin letter I could reach. Treat the wording as unsupported; use "incompatibility of temper" (Vincent's editors' paraphrase of Gauguin's letter to Theo).
- **Razor:** the open-razor scene appears only in Gauguin's late memoir (Avant et après, 1903; the 1888-89 letters I could reach do not mention a razor). Scholars differ: Van Tilborgh (Van Gogh Museum) says "there are only the words of Gauguin"; Kaufmann and Wildegans reject it and blame Gauguin's rapier; Bailey notes the account may be embellished.
- **Who paid the fare and debts: NOT FOUND.** The 150 F per month for a painting each month is confirmed by Vincent's own letter (c. 17 Sept 1888, old no. 538).
- **Christ in the Garden of Olives:** a self-portrait, per Wikipedia leads, the Van Gogh Museum Journal 2001 and Lhote. The red hair is stated by Country Life; no catalogue or letter found. The "purple Christ with red hair" in the letters is Bernard's painting, as described by Theo.
- **Weakest points:** no original Gauguin text read for Q1, Q2, Q4; Pahura and Vaeoho ages not verified; the Norton's own entry was not reached.

## 1. Gauguin's December 1888 letter about leaving; "unsalable"

- Vincent to Theo, 2009 edition no. 724 (the vangoghletters.org title reads "724 (731, 565)", dated "on or about Tuesday, 11 December 1888"; seen only as a search-result title, text unreadable). The old-edition version of this letter is webexhibits no. 565, which the 1958 edition dates 23 December. Date conflict: the 2009 edition has redated it earlier.
- The 1958 editor's note (webexhibits 565) says: "Gauguin had written to Theo that Vincent and he could not go on living together" "in consequence of incompatibility of temper." The note says the quarrel was made up and Gauguin wrote a second letter calling the first a bad dream. The page gives no date for Gauguin's own letter.
- Vincent in the same letter: "Gauguin is very powerful, strongly creative, but just because of that he must have peace." and "either he will definitely go, or else definitely stay."
- W. N. Arnold, "The Ear-Cutting Affair" (1992), chapter abstract: Gauguin's letters to Theo expressed incompatibility and asked for money to return to Paris; later Gauguin asked Theo to regard his earlier letter "as a bad dream."
- Gauguin's own wording in French or English translation (Merlhès edition): NOT FOUND, never read the letter itself.
- Another Gauguin letter (January 1889, to "Cher Monsieur", probably Albert Dauprat per Victor Merlhès) says a painter friend "went raving mad" and he feared "a fatal and tragic accident". Source: The Art Newspaper, 7 June 2019 (Martin Bailey).
- "Unsalable"/"invendable" in Gauguin letters from Arles: NOT FOUND (see list at the end).
- Confidence: medium that "incompatibility" is the right word (two sources); low on exact date; high that "unsalable" is unsupported.

## 2. Fare, debts, Theo's arrangement

- Vincent to Theo, c. 17 September 1888 (webexhibits old no. 538): "I do not think it would be wise to offer Bernard straight off 150 francs for a picture every month, as you did Gauguin." So Theo's arrangement was 150 F a month in exchange for a picture a month. When it began: not stated in what I read. Vincent also writes of Gauguin possibly leaving Pont-Aven "without paying his debts".
- Who paid the train fare and settled the Pont-Aven debts: NOT FOUND in any source I could read. The first pass also did not find it.
- The Art Institute of Chicago page that gives arrival on 23 October 1888 was not re-read.
- Confidence: medium on 150 F (one primary source), none on fare/debts.

## 3. The self-portrait exchange

- Van Gogh Museum catalogue (Joost van der Hoeven, March 2023, Contemporaries of Van Gogh 1, cat. 57), as relayed by fetch: about 11 September 1888 Vincent asked for portraits of each other (letter 680); Gauguin and Bernard instead painted self-portraits with the other's likeness in the background; Gauguin wrote on 1 October 1888 (letter 692) "We've satisfied your desire; in a different way, it's true." Gauguin's painting: September 1888 (Art Newspaper 2023; Van Gogh Museum entry "1888").
- Vincent's self-portrait: October 1888 (Art Newspaper, 16 June 2023). Letter to Gauguin dated 3 October 1888 (webexhibits old 544a; the page notes Dutch number 553a), says "I've a portrait of myself, all ash grey" and "I aimed rather for the character of a bonze, a simple worshipper of the eternal Buddha." Colours: "The ashen colour, which has been obtained by mixing malachite green with orange lead, on pale malachite background."
- Letter to Theo (webexhibits old no. 545, 7 October 1888; the catalogue gives 2009 no. 697 for 4 or 5 October, so 545 = 697 is inferred): the portrait described as "the portrait of a bonze, a simple worshiper of the eternal Buddha", palette "ashen gray against pale veronese (no yellow)", and "Gauguin looks ill and tormented in his portrait!!"
- Gauguin's description of his own: letter 692, 1 October 1888, Jean Valjean "The mask of a thief, badly dressed and powerful like Jean Valjean, who has his nobility and inner gentleness" (catalogue quote). Van Gogh Museum collection page: "my individual image, as well as a portrait of us all, poor victims of society".
- Answer to "before Gauguin arrived": yes. Both portraits were painted and exchanged in September to early October 1888, three weeks before 23 October.
- The Harvard attribution of the dedicated portrait (Fogg) was not checked on harvardartmuseums.org.
- Confidence: high on dates, medium on letter numbers (2009 numbers only partly confirmed).

## 4. The night of 23 December 1888: razor accounts

Accounts found:
- Gauguin, Avant et après (written 1903, published 1918 per standard references, not verified here): "I turned about on the instant as Vincent rushed toward me, an open razor in his hand." (quoted on The Marginalian, 2017, from "Paul Gauguin's Intimate Journals").
- Gauguin, January 1889 letter (see Q1): no razor; "that friend went raving mad", feared "a fatal and tragic accident".
- Gauguin's "1901 memoirs" repeat Bernard's letter for the "cut through" ear description (Art Newspaper, 17 April 2020, Bailey). The Bernard to Aurier letter of 1 January 1889 itself was NOT read and a razor mention there is NOT confirmed.
- Differences: Gauguin 1889 gives no razor and no street scene; the 1903 memoir adds the razor chase. Bailey says the later account "may have been embellished".
- Scholars: Murphy says Gauguin "left two accounts, both slightly different" (PBS Secrets of the Dead blog) and that his description fits Vincent cutting off most of the ear; her razor reading comes from Dr Rey's drawing. Kaufmann and Wildegans (Van Gogh's Ear / Van Goghs Ohr): Gauguin attacked Vincent with a rapier and his account was self-protecting (NPR, 10 May 2009). Louis van Tilborgh, Van Gogh Museum: "an interesting opinion", "so far I have not found proof", "There are only the words of Gauguin". Van Gogh Museum letters site now says the whole ear was cut off (per Bailey). Arnold (1992): Gauguin's account "not fully reliable".
- Confidence: medium.

## 5. Departure: 24 or 25 December; with Theo?

- Webexhibits 565 editor's note: a telegram on 24 December.
- Martin Bailey, The Art Newspaper (25 Oct 2024): Theo arrived from Paris on Christmas Day; "Theo and Gauguin left that evening, arriving back in the capital on 26 December." Bailey cites no source for the departure.
- Answer: Gauguin left on 25 December (evening) with Theo, per one secondary source. The first pass said 23 Oct arrival; no second independent source for the departure date found. Confidence: medium-low.

## 6. Christ in the Garden of Olives (Norton)

- Wikipedia lead: Norton Museum of Art, 1889, painted at Le Pouldu in November, a self-portrait; cites Malingue, Gauguin letter to Bernard, Nov 1889, pp. 173-174; Jaworska, Artibus et Historiae 19 no. 37 (1998) 77-102; Buser, Art Journal 27 no. 4 (1968). Not read.
- Van Gogh Museum Journal 2001, "A modern Gethsemane: Vincent van Gogh's Olive grove" (author not shown on page): says the work was "a self-portrait" with a scene of Judas's betrayal.
- Lhote (Getty page): "The Christ resembles Gauguin, it's not at all like my sketch." No hair colour.
- Country Life (article id 206620): self-portrait with "striking red hair" and a mask-like face; credit to the Norton, gift of Elizabeth C. Norton. This is a magazine, not a catalogue; no scholar or museum text confirming red hair found.
- Vincent's letters: in the Museum Journal article, Theo described Bernard's painting as "a purple Christ with red hair, with a yellow angel". Vincent answered Bernard (letter c. 20 Nov 1889, webexhibits B21) calling it "that nightmare of a Christ in the garden of olives". So the red-haired Christ in the letters is Bernard's painting, not Gauguin's. Theo's original letter not read (vangoghletters 817 etc. blocked).
- Confidence: self-portrait high (3 sources); red hair on Gauguin's painting medium-low (one magazine plus the visible painting).

## 7. Later life

- Teha'amana (also Tehamana): Noa Noa describes her as "child of about thirteen" (Bailey, Art Newspaper, 7 Oct 2019); Studio International review of National Gallery "Gauguin Portraits" calls her his "13-year-old wife"; DailyArtMagazine says 14, no source, unreliable. Danielsson's 1950s research gave a death date of 9 December 1918 with no birth year; Childs doubts she was a single real person; Noa Noa is semi-fictional.
- Pau'ura (Pahura): "In 1896, his neighbours offered him another girl, Pau'ura, then 14" (Michael Prodger, New Statesman, 2024, reviewing Sue Prideaux's Wild Thing). Prodger's own source not seen.
- Vaeoho: NOT FOUND in any page I could read; her age is unverified. David Sweetman: NOT FOUND, never reached.
- Death: 8 May 1903, Atuona, Hiva Oa, Marquesas (Britannica; Daily Art Magazine for date). Syphilis: assumed, not proven. Met Museum essay: "He was suffering from syphilis by this time." Hyperallergic: "long been believed". Artnet: teeth tests found no mercury traces (suggesting he did not have syphilis, but untreated syphilis is possible); teeth found 2000 at his hut, reported by Caroline Boyle-Turner in Van Gogh Studies no. 1 (Art Newspaper, 2007).
