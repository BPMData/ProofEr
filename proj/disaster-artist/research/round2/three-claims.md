# Three claims that may be AI hallucinations (prompt 4)

## Verdicts
1. "January 1890, Boussod, Valadon & Cie held a show of about 20 of Vincent's works": **FALSE as worded; GARBLED from the real January 1890 Les XX display (6 paintings, Brussels, 18 Jan to 23 Feb) and the March 1890 Indépendants (10 paintings, Paris, 20 Mar to 27 Apr).** No source found for any Boussod, Valadon show of Vincent's work.
2. "From 1905 the Stedelijk began buying Van Gogh's work": **FALSE / unsupported. GARBLED from the real 1905 Stedelijk exhibition (474 works shown, loaned) and later long-term loans (from about 1930) by Vincent Willem van Gogh.** No purchase found.
3. "Portrait of Dr Félix Rey entered the Pushkin in 1960": **FALSE. GARBLED: it entered the Pushkin in 1948**, when the State Museum of New Western Art (GMNZI) was liquidated (decree of 6 March 1948). The "1960" has no source.

Overall confidence: claim 3 medium-high (one Russian museum-project provenance line, consistent with dated background); claims 1 and 2 medium (absence of evidence plus positive evidence of what really happened; the letters site could not be read).

## Claim 1: Boussod, Valadon show, January 1890

**What I could confirm happened in Jan to Mar 1890**
- **Les XX, Brussels.** Vincent showed six paintings: two Sunflowers, Ivy, Flowering Orchard (Arles), Wheat Field Sunrise (Saint-Rémy), Red Vineyard. The page says the exhibition "opened January 18, running till February 23" and cites Vincent's letter of 20 Nov 1889 (letter 614b) listing the six (Wikipedia, "Vincent van Gogh's display at Les XX, 1890", read; a lead only, its citations are the letters). The Vincent van Gogh chronology page (Wikipedia, read) gives the same dates and adds that one painting was sold to Anna Boch.
- **Société des Artistes Indépendants, Paris.** Same Les XX page: Vincent "finally showed ten paintings in their 6th annual exhibition, March 20 through April 27, 1890". Chronology page: "Van Gogh contributes 10 paintings, 5 of which have already been shown at Les XX in Brussels."
- **Boussod, Valadon.** The Van Gogh Museum Journal 1999 article (dbnl.org page `_van012199901_01_0007`) states Theo held 12 exhibitions at the gallery, "two date from before Vincent's departure for the south" (Gauguin, Guillaumin, Pissarro in Dec 1887; Degas and Gauguin in Jan 1888). It names no show of Vincent's work and says "we do not know if, or in what way, Theo's hangings differed". It says of the 1891 memorial show that "There was no other choice but to hold the show in his own apartment" (the fetch gave this phrase without context; I did not verify the date it refers to).
- The Van Gogh Museum Journal 2000 article (dbnl.org `_van012200001_01_0005`) mentions "In May-June 1890 Theo staged a Jean-François Raffaëlli show". A vangoghletters.org note (letter 858, note 9) is titled "From 25 February to 15 March 1890 Theo organized an exhibition of recent work by ..." but the page body would not load (JavaScript notice), so I only have that truncated search-result title and cannot say whose work it was. The artist's name is cut off in what I saw, so whether it was Vincent's work is unconfirmed (it is the one lead worth checking in the letters edition itself).
- The Van Gogh Museum catalogue essay "Works Collected by Theo and Vincent van Gogh" (catalogues.vangoghmuseum.com) says only that in 1890 Theo, for Boussod, Valadon, promoted Monticelli (twenty lithographs). It does not say Vincent was exhibited there.

**Verdict: FALSE as worded; GARBLED.** Two real 1890 shows (Les XX in January, with six paintings, and the Indépendants in March, with ten) are the likely source of the confusion; neither was at Boussod, Valadon and neither had about 20 works. Caveat: I cannot rule out informal hanging of Vincent's work at Theo's gallery or flat (Theo did show his brother's work to visitors), but I found no source that calls it a show, and none with a count of about 20.

## Claim 2: Stedelijk "began buying" Van Gogh from 1905

**Evidence**
- 1905 was an exhibition, not a purchase. Van Gogh Museum Journal 1995 (dbnl.org `_van012199501_01_0002`): "In 1905, both took an active part in organising the large Van Gogh exhibition at the Stedelijk Museum". Journal 1999 (dbnl.org `_van012199901_01_0007`): "No fewer than 474 pictures were on view" and it marked Van Gogh's breakthrough with the public. Neither says anything was bought.
- Loans, not purchases: Journal 1995 (dbnl.org `_van012199501_01_0003`) says Vincent Willem van Gogh "transferred the bulk of his collection on loan to the Stedelijk Museum" after the 1930 retrospective; that Johanna loaned parts of the collection to the Rijksmuseum in 1909 and 1917 to 1919; and that in 1962 the collection went to the Vincent van Gogh Foundation for 18,470,000 guilders. The state, not the Stedelijk, was the buyer. The 1962 Dutch news item (dbnl.org `_nee003196201_01_0260`) says many paintings "worden reeds meer dan 30 jaren tentoongesteld in het Stedelijk Museum" (displayed for 30+ years), without saying owned or borrowed.
- The Stedelijk's own history page (stedelijk.nl/en/museum/history) says it was known for "its paintings by Vincent van Gogh (many of them later transferred to the Van Gogh Museum upon its creation)". It gives no purchase record.
- I found no Stedelijk or city-of-Amsterdam purchase of a Van Gogh painting in or after 1905.

**Verdict: FALSE / unsupported.** What is real: the July to August 1905 exhibition, and from about 1930 long-term loans from the family. Caveat: I did not find the Stedelijk's own acquisition ledger, so "no purchase exists" is a negative finding; a small purchase of a drawing or print cannot be excluded.

## Claim 3: Portrait of Dr Félix Rey entered the Pushkin in 1960

**Evidence**
- Provenance line from newestmuseum.ru (the online project of the Museum of Modern Western Art, a Pushkin-linked project; page `/data/authors/g/gogh_vincent_van/portrait_of_doctor_rey.php`, Russian version, read): "1889–1900 — собственность доктора Рея, Арль; с 1900 — в галерее А. Воллара, Париж; в 1908 приобретена С.И. Щукиным в галерее Дрюэ за 4600 франков. До 1918 — в собрании С.И. Щукина, Москва; затем 1-й Музей новой западной живописи; с 1923 — ГМНЗИ; с 1948 — ГМИИ." Inventory numbers on the page: Ж-3272 (Pushkin), GMNZI 33. The English version gives "1918, from the collection of S. I. Shchukin" and the same inventory numbers.
- The liquidation date: newestmuseum.ru history page (`/history/gmnzi/?lang=en`): the museum "was liquidated by the decree of the Board of Ministers of the USSR #672 dated March 6, 1948." Wikipedia (State Museum of Modern Western Art) gives the same date, citing Natalia Semenova, "Operation Liquidation", Snob, 21 May 2013 (lead only).
- Independent support that the 1948 liquidation moved GMNZI works into the Pushkin: The Art Newspaper (article on The Red Vineyard, 2025) says "In 1948, The Red Vineyard was among the works transferred to the Pushkin Museum" and The Art Newspaper's older piece "Hermitage and Pushkin in war of words" says the museum "was shut down by Stalin in 1948". These cover the museum and a sister painting, not the Rey portrait itself.
- Early provenance: Wikipedia (lead only) lists Camoin buying it at Arles in April 1901, then Vollard (150 francs), Cassirer and Druet in 1908, then Shchukin (4600 francs); it cites letter 571 and the Pushkin collection page and *AVA, Bulletin des amis du vieil Arles* no. 135. I did not read those originals. Note the discrepancy: newestmuseum.ru says Rey sold to Vollard in 1900 and does not name Camoin; Wikipedia says Camoin 1901. I did not resolve this.
- "1960": not found in any source I read.

**Verdict: FALSE; GARBLED. 1948 is the entry year to the Pushkin** (via the GMNZI liquidation). Weakness: the only Rey-specific source for "1948" is the newestmuseum.ru project page; I could not open pushkinmuseum.art (robots disallow), Feilchenfeldt 2013 or Russian Wikipedia, so there is no second Rey-specific source for the year.
