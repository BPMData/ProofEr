## 1. Lifetime sales
**The Red Vineyard.**
- Buyer Anna Boch: Martin Bailey, "What were the first 12 Van Gogh paintings ever sold?", The Art Newspaper, 23 Sept 2022, https://theartnewspaper.com/2022/09/23/what-were-the-first-12-van-gogh-paintings-ever-sold . Price 400 francs ("then £16"). Bailey calls it "the only painting that Van Gogh is certain to have sold in his lifetime." Painted Nov 1888.
- Second source: Bailey, "How did the only painting sold by Van Gogh in his lifetime end up in Russia?", The Art Newspaper, 4 Feb 2022 (and 15 Aug 2025 reprint), https://theartnewspaper.com/2022/02/04/how-did-the-only-painting-sold-by-van-gogh-in-his-lifetime-end-up-in-russia . Boch, 400 francs. The Conversation, "Van Gogh Museum at 50: Vincent van Gogh and the art market", 2023, https://theconversation.com/van-gogh-museum-at-50-vincent-van-gogh-and-the-art-market-a-brief-history-197361 : Boch bought it "at the 1890 Les XX exhibition for 400 francs".
- DISAGREEMENT on date: Bailey's text gives the show as "March 1890" in one place and the Les XX show (January 1890) in another. Les XX actually ran 18 January to 23 February 1890 (Wikipedia lead, citing Octave Maus/Rewald; not primary). Safe wording: "sold in 1890, around the Les XX show in Brussels". The exact sale day is not documented in what I could read. No source I opened gives the sale price as anything but 400 francs; the 400 F figure comes from Bailey (same author both times, so those two are not independent). The Conversation agrees but gives no source. Confidence MEDIUM.
- Onward path (same Bailey sources): Boch kept it until about 1907 (Bailey 2022: "around 1907"; Bailey 2022b: "until 1907"); sold to Ivan Morozov for 30,000 francs; nationalised 1918; to the Pushkin in 1948 (see 8).

**Other documented lifetime sales, trades and gifts.**
- Art Journey Curator Substack (a lead, not a source; sources named in its bibliography include Martin Bailey, Apollo 1996, and the VGM FAQ): says Theo sold a Vincent self-portrait to British dealers (invoice), and that Vincent traded paintings for supplies. I could not verify these in a primary source. LOW CONFIDENCE. I did NOT find a documentable "painting sold via Tanguy or a London dealer" during his life in anything I could open.
- The 1882 C.M. van Gogh drawings commission and any sale of drawings: NOT verified (no source opened).
- Bailey's list shows the next documented sales were in 1891, after Vincent's death (11 paintings in 1891 through Theo/Jo/Tanguy at roughly 400 F each; Mirbeau 300 F for Three Sunflowers/Irises; Boch 350 F for Peach Trees in Blossom, June 1891; Gallimard 400 F, June 1891; six to "Willy Gretor" for 2,200 F, Nov 1891). These are posthumous, so they do not count toward "sold in life".

**Honest range for "number sold in his lifetime":**
- One painting, The Red Vineyard, is "certain" (Bailey, Art Newspaper, 2022).
- The Conversation (2023): "allegedly made only one documented sale of a painting".
- The Wikipedia lead says "at least a few others" (cites VGM FAQ; I could not open the FAQ). So the defensible phrasing is: "one sale is documented; a few other transactions or trades are plausible but undocumented." The draft should not say "sold exactly one painting" without "documented" or "certain".

Three sources opened do not mention it: the Posthumous fame article, the Theo van Gogh Wikipedia article (Theo exhibited Monet, Pissarro, Degas), and the Van Gogh Museum Journal 2000 piece (dbnl) which says nothing of Vincent shown at Boussod Valadon. ## 4. Johanna van Gogh-Bonger
- Born 4 Oct 1862 Amsterdam; married Theo 17 April 1889; son Vincent Willem born 31 Jan 1890; Theo died Jan 1891; remarried 1901: Wikipedia lead (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johanna_van_Gogh-Bonger , which lists Luijten 2022 and Shorto, NYT Magazine 2021 among its sources). Smithsonian (https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/new-biography-spotlights-jo-bonger-sister-law-who-rescued-van-gogh-obscurity-180973183/): born October 1862, married 1889, son 1890, Theo died 1891 aged 33, she died 1925 aged 62.
- Theo's death: 25 Jan 1891 at the Willem Arntz hospital in Den Dolder (province of Utrecht), NOT in the city of Utrecht; first buried Utrecht; exhumed and reburied beside Vincent at Auvers in 1914 at Jo's wish (Wikipedia, Theodorus van Gogh, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodorus_van_Gogh, lead). The VGM page "Vincent's life, after his death" has an image caption about "the final resting place of Vincent and Theo in Auvers-sur-Oise" (metadata only). 1914 reburial: MEDIUM (one lead plus VGM caption). Draft "died in Utrecht": ADJUST to "near Utrecht (Den Dolder)".
- REMARRIAGE: she DID remarry. Artsy ("Vincent van Gogh's market was tirelessly built by his sister-in-law Jo", https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-vincent-van-goghs-market-tirelessly-built-sister-in-law-jo): "In 1901, she married for a second time, again to an artist." Second husband Johan Cohen Gosschalk. Van Gogh Museum Journal 1995, dbnl, https://dbnl.org/tekst/_van012199501_01/_van012199501_01_0002.php : "In 1901, Jo married the painter and critic Johan Cohen Gosschalk"; after his death in 1912 she "had reassumed her first husband's name". Two independent sources plus Wikipedia agree. HIGH. The draft's "never remarried" is WRONG.
- Bussum: Wikipedia: she opened a boarding house there after Theo's death. Artnet "Art Bites" (https://news.artnet.com/art-world/art-bites-who-is-jo-van-gogh-bonger-2551340): she took about 400 canvases plus hundreds of drawings to "a Dutch village" and opened a boarding house. VGM Journal 2002 (dbnl _van012200201_01_0003): she "had settled in Bussum". Smithsonian: moved to Bussum, organised exhibitions. HIGH.
- Bernard's advice: Artnet says Émile Bernard told her to keep the paintings in Paris and interest could be revived; she ignored it. This is the only "advice" item found; no source about advice to discard the work. Can't verify "advice to discard".
- Exhibitions and loans 1890s: Smithsonian says about 20 shows across Holland by 1900. TAN (2024): she "quickly agreed to lend 102 paintings" to the 1896 Groningen exhibition (https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/12/06/send-us-everything-how-six-students-brought-together-more-than-100-van-gogh-works-just-a-few-years-after-his-death). thehistoryofart.org (a Zwikker-based summary): about fifteen exhibitions with Paul Cassirer before WWI. "Twenty shows across Holland" is a single secondary summary, LOW.
- 1905 Stedelijk: July and August 1905 (catalogue title, 60 pages: Internet Archive scan via Commons). Count: Smithsonian "nearly 500"; Artnet "nearly 500"; thehistoryofart "more than 480". The "484" figure I had in mind is NOT confirmed in anything opened. Say "nearly 500 paintings and drawings, in July and August 1905". Visitors: about 4,500 in two months (TAN, 2024). MEDIUM-HIGH for "nearly 500". Organised with Cohen Gosschalk (VGM Journal 1995 dbnl).
- Stedelijk buying Van Goghs "from 1905 onward" (draft): NO support found. VGM Journal 2002 says only that in 1930 Vincent Willem agreed to lend most works to the Stedelijk. Can't verify; likely wrong.
- Sales strategy: Artsy: she released works slowly, offered dealers 10 to 15 percent commission, set asking prices by customer (Luijten, quoted secondhand). Her total sales: Artsy "at least 190 paintings and 55 drawings"; Smithsonian "around 250 original works"; Dbnl 1995 says only "few exceptions, nothing more was sold until after Jo died in 1925" (this speaks to the paintings she held back). thehistoryofart says "nearly two hundred artworks sold between 1891 and 1925". DISAGREEMENT between ~200 and ~250 (differences of paintings versus all works).
- Paintings kept: TAN 2020 (https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2020/12/11/why-the-van-gogh-museum-might-never-have-existednew-research-reveals-how-the-family-collection-was-nearly-sold-off): in 1911 the family held "around 200 Van Gogh paintings and 600 drawings"; she did not seriously consider the 1911 offer (via the dealer de Bois, probably Anton Kröller). Second: dbnl 1995 ("with few exceptions, nothing more was sold until after Jo died in 1925").
- 1914 "Brieven aan zijn broeder": three volumes, edited by Jo (Wikipedia; NYRB review quotes Banville: "a three-volume edition ... published in 1914, edited by ... Jo van Gogh-Bonger"). VGM Journal 2002 says the 1952 to 1954 edition restored passages "previously omitted for one reason or another". What she omitted: NOT found in any source I could open (Luijten not accessible). thehistoryofart says she financed the 1914 edition and delayed publication so the paintings could gain recognition first. Cannot give specifics.
- Early buyers: Kröller-Müller: first purchase 1908, Edge of a Wood (1883), 110 guilders (Kröller-Müller Museum, https://krollermuller.nl/en/van-gogh-collection); the couple acquired 88 paintings and 172 works on paper over the next twenty years (same page; the Conversation says "91 paintings and more than 180 works on paper", a DISAGREEMENT; the museum's own figure is the better one). None bought directly from Jo (TAN 2020). German buyers: Osthaus (Folkwang) bought Wheatfield behind Saint Paul's Hospital with a Reaper through Cassirer in 1902 and First Steps from Jo in 1905 (thehistoryofart, secondary); German holdings by 1914: the Conversation says about 120 pictures; thehistoryofart (citing Städel research) says about 150 works. DISAGREEMENT, say "well over a hundred".

## 6. Van Gogh Museum
- Foundation: dbnl VGM Journal 1995: the Vincent van Gogh Foundation was established 10 July 1960; "In 1962, with the consent of the Dutch parliament, the State purchased the entire collection on behalf of the Foundation"; "The Rijksmuseum Vincent van Gogh was opened officially on 2 June 1973." The draft's "Foundation 1962" is an ADJUST (founded 1960; the agreement and state purchase 1962). Opening date 2 June 1973: one primary-ish source (a VGM publication) plus the Wikipedia/VGM "opened 1973" corroboration (TAN 2020, The Conversation). MEDIUM-HIGH.
- Collection size: 1911 about 200 paintings and 600 drawings (TAN 2020); the Art Newspaper (Bailey) also says Vincent Willem was paid only a small part of the collection's market value. The museum's present total ("200 paintings, 500 drawings, 750 letters") was not verified; do not use.

Christian Science Monitor, 8 April 1987 (https://csmonitor.com/1987/0408/fvan.html): £22.5m ($36.5m) before premium, almost £25m ($40.5m) with the 10 percent premium, buyer then anonymous. DISAGREEMENT: £24.75m is the arithmetic of £22.5m plus 10 percent, which CS Monitor rounds to "almost £25m" and TAN rounds to £24m. Say "about £24.75m with premium, about $40m". TAN 2023 (Tokyo/Nazi persecution article): Yasuda bought it for its museum on the 42nd floor of its Tokyo headquarters. MEDIUM.
- Irises: Sotheby's New York, 11 Nov 1987, $53.9m, Alan Bond (TAN ten most expensive: "$54m"; Artnet obituary of Bond: $53.9m, Sotheby's lent Bond about $27m, more than half the price, with the loan emerging two years later, 1990 Getty purchase, brokered by Sotheby's, price undisclosed). Christian Science Monitor, 30 Nov 1987: $53.9m. HIGH. (TAN 2022 says Getty paid "around $50m" in a different article; undisclosed officially, treat as rumour.)
- Dr Gachet, first version: Christie's New York, 15 May 1990, $82.5m including premium ($75m hammer), buyer Ryoei Saito (Artnet "real record" article, https://news.artnet.com/market/picasso-painting-broke-real-record-300470 ; TAN ten most expensive: $83m; CBS News 2004: $82.5m "including the auction house premium" to "a Japanese billionaire"). HIGH.
- Surpassed: Picasso, Garçon à la pipe, Sotheby's New York, Wednesday 5 May 2004, $104m incl. commission ($93m hammer) (CBS News, https://www.cbsnews.com/news/picasso-painting-fetches-104m ; Al Jazeera, https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2004/5/6/pablo-picasso-breaks-auction-record). CBS names Gachet as the previous record; Al Jazeera says "a Van Gogh ... 1990, $82.5m". HIGH. (Inflation-adjusted, Gachet's record was only beaten by Picasso's Les Femmes d'Alger in 2015, per Artnet; mention only if the piece uses real terms.)
- Where is Gachet: TAN, 15 Nov 2019 (https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/11/15/where-is-the-portrait-of-dr-gachet-the-mysterious-disappearance-of-van-goghs-most-expensive-painting): believed held privately, probably in Switzerland, after a sale (reportedly to Wolfgang Flöttl); Saito said he wanted it burned with him, which did not happen when he died in 1996. Artnet 2015: "It's not clear where the Van Gogh ended up." Say "whereabouts unknown, believed private". HIGH for "unknown", LOW for any named holder.

## 9. Julien "Père" Tanguy
- Source read: Émile Bernard, "Julien Tanguy, dit le Père Tanguy", Mercure de France, 16 Dec 1908 (PDF, https://leautaud.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/1908-12-16-le-pere-tanguy-mc.pdf). In 1871 he joined the Commune's federated battalions (thirty sous a day); arrested near rue Saint-Vincent, taken to Versailles, deported ("déporté"), endured the PONTOONS (hulks); a friend, Jobbé-Duval, got him pardoned in 1873 after two years; kept out of Paris about two more years; then Saint-Brieuc. The pontoons point to Brest (hulks in the roadstead). French Wikipedia (lead, citing Bernard 1908, Duret 1916 and Vassor): taken to Satory camp, council of war, deported to Brest after Satory, and says archives show he served his whole sentence (the Jobbé-Duval and Rouart "intervention" versions are contested). English Wikipedia: "released at the instigation of friends".
- VERDICT on "deported to New Caledonia, amnestied 1880": WRONG. No source puts him in New Caledonia; two sources (Bernard 1908, fr.wikipedia) put him at Brest on hulks. The general amnesty of 1880 is irrelevant to him. Release: 1873 (Bernard), or after serving the full sentence per the archives (French Wikipedia); the exact sentence is not given in what I read. Confidence on "Brest hulks, not New Caledonia": MEDIUM-HIGH.
- Shop: 14 rue Clauzel (Bernard; English Wikipedia: opened 1873; Rodin page: "ran a small paint supplies shop, on the Rue Clauzel"). Earlier base rue Cortot 12. Barter: Bernard says he took still lifes and landscapes from Cézanne in return for colours; Musée Rodin: "often accepted paintings in exchange for the goods he sold." HIGH (two sources).
- Wife: Renée-Julienne Briend, married 1855 at Saint-Brieuc (English Wikipedia). Bernard: she was the one who spoke openly of their poverty to visitors. Her "attitude" beyond that was not found. Substack (lead) says Vincent sold a portrait of Tanguy's wife for 20 F by his own letter; unverified.
- Death and stock sale: English Wikipedia: died 6 Feb 1894 (draft/other sources say 1 Feb; DISAGREEMENT, not resolved); Mirbeau wrote a tribute in L'Écho de Paris, 13 Feb 1894; the auction at the Hôtel Drouot on 2 June 1894 was of works donated by painter friends (Bernard: "many artists donated works"; raised about 15,000 F, about 10,000 F after costs, for his widow). Note: this was a charity sale of donated works at Mirbeau's suggestion, not simply a sale of Tanguy's own stock. The widow lived in a small room on rue Cortot on the proceeds (Bernard). MEDIUM-HIGH for the sale, LOW for the death date.
