Points that matter for the essay.
- "250 F/month in Arles" is supported by Vincent's own words (Aug 1888). It was not constant: Oct 1888 shows 150 F per person as the proposed standard, and the 250 F figure is Vincent's own supposition ("if I take it that…").
- "Paint and canvas paid separately" is not confirmed by any page I opened. Letter 201 does say paint and paper cost extra on top of living costs, but that is Vincent's budget, not a statement of how Theo paid. UNVERIFIED.
- Family-wide support. Van Gogh Museum catalogue (Contemporaries of Van Gogh 1, introductory essay): Theo took over the family support from his father from early 1881, and sent money to their mother after their father died in 1885. VERIFIED.
- The 150 F that Theo paid Vincent in Nuenen 1884 is confirmed (letter 440 note and letter 527). The Hague rate of 150 F (1883) was not confirmed in any page I opened. LOW CONFIDENCE.

## Q2. Scholarly estimate of the total, and my year table

Published scholarly estimate. I found no published franc total for the decade.
- Van Gogh Museum catalogue, Contemporaries of Van Gogh 1, introductory essay (catalogues.vangoghmuseum.com/contemporaries-of-van-gogh-1/introductory-essay). VERIFIED: Theo's income was "between 11,000 and 14,000 francs a year", made up of a monthly salary plus a yearly commission of 7.5 per cent of his branch's profit. "On average, 14.5 per cent of Theo's total income went to Vincent." The allowance "represented a considerable drain on Theo's income."
- Cross-check, my arithmetic: 14.5% of 11,000 to 14,000 F is about 1,600 to 2,030 F a year. Over 10 years that is about 16,000 to 20,300 F. This supports a total of roughly 18,000 F, but only as a rough check. The method (which years, which income series) behind the 14.5% is not given on the page I read.
- The primary source for the real total is Stolwijk & Veenenbos, The Account Book of Theo van Gogh and Jo van Gogh-Bonger (2002). I could not open it (only catalogue entries). The 14.5% almost certainly comes from it or the museum's own work. Needs checking against the book.

My year-by-year table. These are my estimates from the letters above, not published figures. Assumptions: 100 F/month Dec 1880 to 1882, 150 F/month 1883 to Feb 1886, nothing in cash in Paris, 250 F/month Arles to Saint-Rémy. High adds ad hoc sums such as the March 1884 pattern and Arles extras.

| Year | Low F | High F | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1880 | 100 | 500 | small sums, Theo's help began about this time (UNVERIFIED) |
| 1881 | 600 | 1,200 | 100 F/month from about mid-year (letter 164) |
| 1882 | 1,200 | 1,800 | 100 to 150 F/month (letter 201) |

The draft's "about 18,000 F" is inside this range and consistent with the museum's 14.5% share. "150 to 250 F most months" holds for the months Theo paid cash (Nuenen/Arles); it does not describe Paris 1886 to 88 (no cash) or the early years (100 F).

Guilder/franc. In letter 527 Vincent's own conversion is 125 guilders = 250 francs (about 2 F per guilder). I could not verify the official rate; the 2:1 figure is Vincent's rough working rate.

## Q3. Converting 1890 francs

- Gold parity. The French franc held 0.290322 g of fine gold (Germinal franc, 1803 law), and the US dollar after the 1834/1837 acts held 23.22 grains of fine gold (about 1.5046 g). 1.5046 / 0.29032 gives 5.18 F per dollar. The French figure and the 23.22 grain figure are from my background knowledge. UNVERIFIED: I opened the Econlib coinage-laws page, which gave only 25.8 grains standard weight for the 1873 dollar (25.8 × 0.9 = 23.22 fine). Needs a citation (e.g. Historical Statistics of the US).
- 400 F at that parity is about $77.
- US CPI step. officialdata.org (BLS CPI-U, spliced pre-1913 with Sahr/Oregon State data): $100 in 1890 is $3,708.91 in 2026 (CPI 337.511 / 9.1). VERIFIED. Multiplier about 37.1.
- Results.
  - 400 F: about $77 in 1890, about $2,860 in 2026 dollars.
  - 18,000 F decade total: about $3,475 in 1890, about $129,000 in 2026 dollars (range from my estimates 13,100 to 19,100 F: about $94,000 to $137,000).
- Caveats. French prices and wages are not US ones, so this converts francs to dollars first and then inflates dollars. A pre-1913 CPI is a rough splice and a poor fit for a one-person budget. Wage comparison (Q4) is the better frame for "what 150 F a month meant". Not done: INSEE franc-euro converter (INSEE's converter only covers back to 1901, so it cannot be used directly for 1890 without extrapolation; UNVERIFIED claim from memory).

## Q4. Comparison wages

- Industrial worker, VERIFIED: coal miners (Journal de la Société de statistique de Paris, vol. 32, 1891, pp. 20-21, numdam.org/item/JSFS_1891__32__20_0.pdf). 1880-84 average 3 F 80 daily and 1,079.06 F annual; 1885-88 average 3 F 72 daily and 1,060.93 F annual. This is miners, not "the average industrial worker"; no all-industry average found. Theo's 1,800 F a year to Vincent is therefore about 1.7 times a miner's annual pay.
- Primary schoolteacher: NOT FOUND. The Senate/education.gouv pages I opened reproduce the 30 Oct 1886 Goblet law, which only refers to a future salary law (Law of 19 July 1889). No salary figures in the pages opened. My recollection that a beginning instituteur earned roughly 1,000 to 1,500 F is unverified. UNVERIFIED; do not use until sourced (try Prost, Histoire de l'enseignement en France; Ozouf; Journal officiel 1889).

## Q5. Employer and dismissal

- Name change. "Goupil & Cie became Boussod, Valadon & Cie in 1884." Van Gogh Museum catalogue essay (VERIFIED). Same date in vangoghroute.com (a lead only).
- Theo's role. The catalogue essay refers to Theo's "branch" and to "19, Boulevard Montmartre" (Journal 2000 text, p. 48). Whether he was formally "manager" is not stated in what I opened. Salary: no monthly salary figure found. Total income 11,000 to 14,000 F including 7.5% commission (VERIFIED, catalogue essay).
- Dismissal date. Vincent's employment ended 1 April 1876 (vangoghroute.com, lead) and the letters 065 notes: Vincent wrote on 4 January 1876 that he would have to resign as of 1 April. Letter 065 notes VERIFIED.
- Who. Letter 065 notes: after a meeting with Boussod, Vincent believed he had "no choice but to resign". The 12 January 1876 letter from Rev. van Gogh to Theo (webexhibits.org/vangogh/letter/4/etc-fam-1876.htm) says Vincent "had been especially blamed for going home at the end of the year" and that after the encounter with "M. Boussod" he "feels no other way open" than to leave on the first of April. So the first-hand account has Vincent giving notice, not being dismissed. The Art Newspaper (Martin Bailey) says "the owner of Goupil dismissed" him and that Charles Obach, London branch manager, had reported on poor performance.
- Versions.
  - Christmas version: Vincent went home to Etten at Christmas 1875 without leave and the firm resented it. Source: letter 065 notes (editors, "very much resented"), the father's letter of 12 January 1876, and vangoghroute.com (lead). This is the best-documented version.
  - Customers version: poor with customers, tactless. Told by Martin Bailey, The Art Newspaper, 19 October 2018 ("awkward employee, lacking tact"). The "Bible reading and proselytizing to customers" version is NOT supported by any source I opened. UNVERIFIED. I did not reach Naifeh and Smith (2011) or Vincent's own letter that tells it. Do not use until traced.
