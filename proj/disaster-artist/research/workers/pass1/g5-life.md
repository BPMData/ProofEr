## Q1. Careers before art

### Goupil & Cie start (July 1869, age 16, via uncle Cent)
- Wikipedia "Vincent van Gogh chronology" (LEAD): apprenticeship at Goupil & Cie, The Hague, 30 July 1869. Wikipedia "Vincent van Gogh" (LEAD): "In July 1869, his uncle Cent obtained a position for him at Goupil & Cie in The Hague" (cites its own note 38; article text does not state age). Born 30 March 1853 (VGM, vangoghmuseum.nl, page metadata, opened), so 16 on 30 July 1869 (arithmetic, not a stated source fact).
- Uncle Cent = Vincent van Gogh (1820-1888), art dealer, brother of Theodorus; identified in the notes to letter 065 (vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let065/notes.html, opened).
- That Cent was a "partner" in Goupil: not confirmed in anything I opened. Bonger Memoir (webexhibits.org/vangogh/memoir/sisterinlaw/4.html, opened) speaks of "Uncle Vincent" and "the House of Goupil" but the summary I got does not use "partner". CAN'T VERIFY the word "partner" here. LOW CONFIDENCE (single LEAD source for the start date).
- Hague-era testimonial: Bonger Memoir: "When leaving The Hague, he got a splendid testimonial from Mr. Tersteeg."

### London (1873) and Paris
- Chronology (Wikipedia, LEAD): London branch about a week after 12 May 1873; Paris from November 1873 at Uncle Cent's request (as extracted). The Art Newspaper (Martin Bailey, "Adventures with Van Gogh", 19 Oct 2018, theartnewspaper.com/2018/10/19/...): Vincent, aged 20, joined the London gallery as an assistant in 1873; manager Charles Obach (b. Stuttgart 1841).
- Bonger Memoir: "In 1873 the latter [Vincent] had been transferred to the firm in London"; "In October 1874, 'Uncle Vincent did indeed effect a short removal to the firm in Paris'"; then "transferred permanently to Paris and assigned especially to the picture gallery." Note: the Chronology summary says Paris Nov 1873, the Memoir says Oct 1874 for the short Paris stay. DISAGREEMENT on the first Paris move (Nov 1873 vs Oct 1874). Memoir is the older source; the "Nov 1873" reading looks suspect (Wikipedia LEAD only). Treat the first Paris stay as Oct 1874 (Memoir) until the vangoghletters biography can be read. Permanent Paris move: 1875 (Wikipedia main page LEAD: transfer to Paris "in 1875").

### Dismissal (notice, effective 1 April 1876): what is documented
- Letter 065 (CL50), Vincent to Theo, Paris, Monday 10 January 1876 (vangoghletters.org/vg/letters/let065/print.html, opened; year corrected from "1875" by note 1). Vincent reports telling Boussod he would leave "on 1 April, thanking the gentlemen for anything I might have learned in their firm"; "His Hon. took the words out of my mouth, so to speak"; "I've certainly done things that were in some way very wrong."
- Notes to letter 065 (opened): Vincent had spent December 1875 with his parents; on 3 January 1876 he returned to Paris and met Boussod, who received him unfavourably ("the meeting turned very unpleasant" per his father); his father wrote Vincent believed "he had no choice but to resign as from April 1st"; another family letter said Pa found Vincent "lacks the strength to get ahead on his own"; "His dismissal was not rescinded, however." Cited separately by the summariser: going home at the end of the year "had been very much resented." (Page-level summary; wording to be re-verified.)
- Bonger Memoir (opened): "that ended in his dismissal as from April 1"; "Uncle Vincent was deeply disappointed in his namesake and had washed his hands of him."
- The Art Newspaper (Bailey, 2018): "awkward employee, lacking tact," poor with customers; Obach twice arranged temporary transfers to Paris; the owner dismissed him after Obach reported disappointing performance. Atlas Obscura piece (secondary): fired January 1876 "after his boss gave him a harsh evaluation".
- Disagreement on the act: Vincent's own letter frames it as him giving notice to leave on 1 April (resignation, in a conversation Boussod steered); Memoir and Bailey say dismissal; Wikipedia chronology (LEAD) says "resigned after talks with Léon Boussod". Last working day 30 March 1876 (chronology LEAD); letter 073 (28 March 1876, opened) says he expects to leave Friday evening (31 March) for Etten.
- DRAFT TEST: "fired 1876 for reading the Bible at the counter and proselytizing the customers". I found NO opened source for Bible-reading at the counter. Documented reasons: unauthorised/resented Christmas absence, general unsuitability for customer work (Bailey), a strained meeting with Boussod, and Vincent's own religious turn is a plausible background but is not stated as the cause in anything I opened. Verdict: ADJUST / CAN'T VERIFY the Bible-at-the-counter claim. "Fired" is defensible but note he was allowed to frame it as leaving on 1 April.

### Ramsgate and Isleworth 1876
- Letter 074 (CL59), Etten, 4 April 1876 (opened): a Ramsgate teacher offered a one-month unpaid trial; teacher was William Port Stokes, school at 6 Royal Road, Ramsgate. Editors cite Nottingham 1974 p.13 and London 1992 p.12.
- Chronology (Wikipedia LEAD): arrived Ramsgate 16 April 1876; Stokes's school moved to Isleworth June/July 1876; Vincent moved to the Rev. Slade-Jones's school 3 July 1876. Bonger Memoir: Stokes's school "moved in July to Isleworth"; he then took a position at the school of Mr Jones, "a Methodist preacher"; not re-hired after Christmas. Minor DISAGREEMENT on the move (June vs July) between the two. Holds in outline.

### Dordrecht (1877)
- Bonger Memoir: Uncle Vincent "procured a place for him in the bookshop of Blussé and Van Braam in Dordrecht"; accepted "without great enthusiasm." Wikipedia main (LEAD): returned home at Christmas 1876, bookshop afterwards. Exact start (January 1877) is in neither opened source with a date; LOW CONFIDENCE on the month.

### Amsterdam theology preparation 1877-78
- Bonger Memoir: "That period in Amsterdam, from May, 1877, to 1878, was one long tale of woe"; teacher advised him to give up his studies; he later called it "the worst time of my life." Wikipedia main (LEAD): lived with uncle Johannes Stricker; left July 1878.
- Mendes da Costa and Latin/Greek: NOT in any source I could open. CAN'T VERIFY (known from general literature; needs vangoghletters letters from 1877-78 or Naifeh).

### Brussels evangelist school (1878)
- Bonger Memoir: "Toward the end of August he arrived at the school in Brussels which had been opened only recently"; "when the three months had elapsed, he did not get his nomination." Holds, single opened source (plus Wikipedia chronology not giving it). LOW CONFIDENCE on the school's formal name and on any single cause.

### Borinage 1879: why not renewed
- Bonger Memoir (opened): Vincent went to the Borinage at his own risk (Pâturages, 30 francs a month), in January 1879 received a six-month temporary post at Wasmes; gave away belongings, lived in a poor hut; "So much zeal was too much for the Committee, and a person who neglected himself so could not be an example to others"; "he was not appointed again, but was given three months to look for another position." The Church Council at Wasmes warned him.
- Wikipedia "Abraham van der Waeyen Pieterszen" (LEAD): the evangelisation committee headed by Pierre Péron (minister of Dour) refused to extend his contract in 1879. Wikipedia "Vincent van Gogh" (LEAD): dismissed for "undermining the dignity of the priesthood" (cites its note 53).
- DISAGREEMENT in the characterisation: Memoir says non-renewal after probation because of excess zeal and self-neglect; Wikipedia says dismissed. The vangoghletters notes (letters 149-153) would settle this; I could not open them.
- DRAFT TEST: "dismissed 1879 for living like the miners and giving away his clothes". The core (self-neglect, gave away belongings, committee judged it unsuitable) is supported by the Memoir. Wording ADJUST: the post was a six-month probationary appointment that the committee did not renew, not a mid-term sacking; "giving away his clothes" is supported by "gave away his belongings", exact "clothes" not confirmed in what I opened.

### Decision to be an artist (1880)
- Bonger Memoir: summer 1880 in Cuesmes he returned to drawing: "I will take up my pencil... I will go on with my drawing. From that moment everything has seemed trans-formed for me." Same Memoir: Theo had earlier suggested he become a painter. Wikipedia chronology (LEAD): 1 August 1880 set out on his first walk to find work; main page: began drawing in August 1880. Brussels move October 1880 (Memoir; chronology LEAD). VGM's own summary line (meta description, opened): he worked as dealer, teacher, bookseller and lay preacher "before he decided to become an artist". Holds: decision in summer 1880, in Cuesmes; letter (Brieven numbering) is the Theo letter of about 24 September 1880 (not opened; number not verified).

## Q2. The beloveds

### London landlady's daughter: Eugénie vs Ursula Loyer
- Facts (Martin Bailey, The Art Newspaper, "Mystery of Van Gogh's love affair in London", 29 March 2019, theartnewspaper.com/2019/03/29/mystery-of-van-goghs-love-affair-in-london): the Loyer household at 87 Hackford Road, Brixton; Ursula (Sarah Ursula Loyer, b. 1815 per Bailey, who says she understated her age) ran a small school with her daughter Eugénie, about 19 at her engagement; Eugénie married Samuel Plowman in 1878. Source of the "Ursula" story: Jo Bonger's 1913 Memoir. Bailey argues the object of affection "must therefore surely have been" Eugénie, though Vincent never named her. (Summary says census 1871 listed Sarah U. Loyer aged 46, which conflicts with b. 1815; flag for checking.)
- Bonger Memoir (opened): Vincent lodged with Mrs Loyer; "Ursula" made "a deep impression upon him"; she was already engaged; "He tried everything to make her break this engagement, but he did not succeed." So the Memoir calls the young woman "Ursula", i.e. the name Bonger used for the daughter was the mother's name. Van Gogh Museum Journal 1996, "In love: Vincent van Gogh's first true love" (dbnl.org/tekst/_van012199601_01/_van012199601_01_0009.php; author not shown on the page I opened) retells the Memoir as: lodged with widow Ursula Loyer and daughter Eugénie from August 1875 [sic; Vincent lodged there from 1873-74]; no proposal appears in the accounts; the story turns on her refusal to break the engagement.
- That Journal article's rebuttal: Anna van Gogh to Theo, 6 January 1874 (Vincent and Eugénie agree to be "brother and sister"); Anna to Theo, 24 February 1874 (Eugénie has a fiancé, "a good natured youth", Vincent "always in good spirits"); Jan Hulsker (1985) found no evidence of intensified love and called the episode "highly dramatised and exaggerated". It argues Vincent's real youthful love was Caroline Haanebeek (letter to Theo 12 Nov 1881, 181/157). So scholarship disagrees on whether there was any love affair with a Loyer at all.
- Bailey's piece (same Art Newspaper article) also reports an Anna letter of 6 January 1874 naming "Ursula" and a later letter naming Eugénie with a note from Vincent that she was engaged. The two summaries describe the 6 Jan 1874 letter differently. DISAGREEMENT between the two secondary accounts of the same letter; the letter itself (vangoghletters, to Theo, Anna's letter) not opened.
- Who refused him: nothing opened documents a proposal or a refusal. The only "refusal" is the Memoir/Journal retelling that she would not break her engagement. 
- DRAFT TEST: "Ursula Loyer, a real person; the verdict was delivered by her mother". Ursula Loyer was a real person but she was the mother (Bailey), not the beloved; the Memoir's "Ursula" is the daughter in the story, and scholars (Bailey, Hulsker, VGM Journal 1996) dispute or reject it. "Verdict delivered by her mother" appears in none of the opened sources. Verdict: WRONG / CAN'T VERIFY. Safe wording: "a London landlady's daughter (traditionally named Ursula, probably Eugénie Loyer; whether he ever proposed is undocumented)."

### Kee Vos-Stricker (1881)
- Wikipedia "Early works of Vincent van Gogh" (LEAD): Kee Vos-Stricker, recently widowed cousin, summer 1881 at Etten; she refused decisively ("No, never, never"); cites its own note "Letter 153, Vincent to Theo, 3 November 1881".
- CL157 (Vincent to Theo, Etten, 12 Nov 1881; webexhibits.org/vangogh/letter/10/157.htm, opened): the phrase appears as "never, no, never"; Vincent says he will "use all my powers to thaw that 'never, no, never'"; asks Theo for money for "the trip to Amsterdam".
- CL164 (Etten, c. 21 Dec 1881; webexhibits.org/vangogh/letter/10/164.htm, opened): in Amsterdam Kee avoided him; her family said she had left the house; Uncle (J.P. Stricker) read a letter asking him to stop corresponding; Vincent refused; "she and no other".
- Translation DISAGREEMENT: older CL rendering (and Wikipedia): "No, nay, never"; the webexhibits CL157 text as summarised gives "never, no, never". The Dutch is "nooit, neen, nimmer". The draft's "no, never, never" follows the Wikipedia/Erickson rendering; fine as a paraphrase, but cite the translation used. Kee herself is not named in CL157.
- Vincent left Etten at Christmas 1881 after a quarrel with his father (letter 195, 30 Dec 1881, opened: left Etten "because of constant quarrels with his father, including over church attendance").

### Sien (Clasina Maria) Hoornik 1882-83
- Met late January 1882 (Wikipedia Early works, LEAD, citing Callow/Hulsker). VGM-sourced? not opened. Son Willem born 2 July 1882 (same LEAD). They separated autumn 1883 (LEAD). The Art Newspaper puts the parting about 18 months after meeting (artnet, same source).
- Pressure to leave her: Bailey (The Art Newspaper, "Exclusive: Van Gogh's prostitute lover committed suicide just as he predicted", 25 April 2019, theartnewspaper.com/2019/04/25/...): "partly because Theo opposed a relationship with a former prostitute, they never married." Artnet (news.artnet.com/art-world/van-gogh-girlfriend-1526860, same reporting, not independent) repeats it and quotes Vincent: he did not want her to feel "abandoned and alone" and feared "hardship will make her take the same road that ends in the abyss." Wikipedia (LEAD): Mauve went cold, Vincent supposed Mauve disapproved. "The family vetoed it": Theo documented; wider family not documented in what I opened. Father/uncle pressure unverified.
- Her death, stated factually: Sien Hoornik drowned herself on the night of 22 November 1904 in Rotterdam; her body was found early that morning in the Provenierssingel canal, north of Rotterdam Centraal; the death record is in the Rotterdam archives (found by Martin Bailey; reported in Rotterdamsch Nieuwsblad); her husband Arnoldus van Wijk (married 1901) identified her clothing and ring a week later. (The Art Newspaper 25 April 2019, one source reporting archival documents; artnet repeats the date 22 Nov 1904 and the Rotterdam archive; not independent.) Jan Hulsker earlier recorded that she "drowned herself" with the wrong date.
- Scheldt: Wikipedia "Vincent van Gogh" (LEAD) still says "drowned herself in the Scheldt in 1904". Bailey's archival find puts it in a canal in Rotterdam (Provenierssingel), not the Scheldt. DISAGREEMENT, Bailey's archive-based account preferred.
- artnet slip: says Van Gogh's death was "21 years" before Hoornik's; 1890 to 1904 is 14. Don't copy that.
- DRAFT TEST: "Sien Hoornik, the family vetoed it; the Scheldt, 1904". Year 1904 HOLDS. "The Scheldt" is WRONG (Provenierssingel canal, Rotterdam, per Bailey's find; the Scheldt claim is an older or loose attribution). "The family vetoed it": ADJUST (Theo is documented as opposing; the sources I opened do not document a family veto).

## Q3. Training: the "self-taught" claim

- Brussels, 1880-81: Bonger Memoir: moved to Brussels in October 1880, small hotel at Bd du Midi 72; befriended Van Rappard. Wikipedia main (LEAD): registered at the Académie Royale des Beaux-Arts in November 1880. Wikipedia "Early works" (LEAD) says he "decided to study on his own, rather than at the art academy", conflicting. Letter 195 (30 Dec 1881, to Rappard, opened) disparages the Brussels academy teachers Stallaert and Severdonck and says he expects little technical benefit. DISAGREEMENT whether he formally enrolled; I could not read the registration evidence. Verdict: can't verify his enrolment; the dismissive 1881 letter supports "brief or informal".
- Mauve, The Hague: Wikipedia chronology (LEAD): arrived in The Hague 27 Nov or 4 Dec 1881 to stay with Mauve. Wikipedia "Early works" (LEAD): January 1882 settled in the Hague; Mauve taught oil and watercolour and lent money; they fell out within about a month (possibly over drawing from plaster casts); Mauve cold by March 1882 (cites letters 196, 219). VGM page (opened, meta description only): he "took painting and drawing lessons with his uncle [sic] Anton Mauve in The Hague". Relationship: Mauve was the husband of Ariëtte Carbentus, Vincent's first cousin (Bonger Memoir and general literature; wording "cousin by marriage" not checked). So lessons lasted from about December 1881/January 1882 to a falling out within weeks: a matter of weeks to a few months, not a long apprenticeship. LOW CONFIDENCE on exact dates.
- Antwerp, Royal Academy: KMSKA (Royal Museum of Fine Arts Antwerp, kmska.be/en/vincent-van-gogh-in-antwerp, opened): arrived 24 November 1885; academy enrolment January 1886; drawing from plaster casts with Frans Vinck, then Eugène Siberdt; tried painting lessons with Charles Verlat; left for Paris 28 February 1886; "eventually referred to a lower grade" after he had left. Wikipedia (LEAD): classes began 18 January 1886; on 31 March 1886 the teachers decided 17 students, including him, must repeat a year; the story that Siberdt expelled him is "unfounded". Agreement between KMSKA and Wikipedia on January start and later demotion. Duration at the academy about six weeks (18 Jan to 28 Feb 1886); about three months in Antwerp altogether.
- DRAFT TEST: "a winter at Antwerp's academy". Winter yes, but short: about six weeks (Jan-Feb 1886). ADJUST wording, e.g. "six weeks at Antwerp's academy".
- Cormon, Paris: VGM "Artist in Paris" (vangoghmuseum.nl/en/stories/artist-in-paris, opened): "soon after arriving in Paris" he began drawing lessons there; arrived Paris 1886, stayed two years; dates and duration not given. Wikipedia main (LEAD): worked at Cormon's studio in April and May 1886; chronology (LEAD): March-June 1886, dates uncertain. DISAGREEMENT/uncertainty on duration (about 2 to 4 months). LOW CONFIDENCE; Cormon's studio dates need Naifeh or the vangoghletters notes to letter 569 (not opened).

## Q4. Japan

### How many prints
- Axel Rüger (VGM director), reported by The Art Newspaper, 7 June 2018, "Revealed: Van Gogh's failed attempt at art dealing" (theartnewspaper.com/2018/06/07/...): Van Gogh had 660 prints; bought "over 600" in early 1887 from Siegfried Bing; paid about 15 centimes each, hoped to sell for 25; showed them at Agostina Segatori's bar; "not for pleasure but to deal in them"; the museum holds 511 of the prints. Apollo magazine (apollo-magazine.com/how-van-gogh-imagined-japan/): bought 660 woodcut prints from Bing soon after arriving in Paris in 1886; debt of 90 francs to Bing unpaid at his death. The Art Newspaper, 18 April 2025: bought more than 600 in Paris 1886-88, most survive at the VGM including 78 by Hiroshige.
- DISAGREEMENT: purchase year (1886 vs early 1887); 660 is the VGM figure as reported by the press; I did not find the number on a VGM page (the VGM "Japanese prints" about-page text did not load; its meta line says only "hundreds"). 511 vs "most survive": reported by press, VGM page not opened. LOW CONFIDENCE until the VGM page is read; 660 is attributed to Rüger in two outlets.

### Flowering Plum Orchard (after Hiroshige), 1887
- VGM collection page s0115V1962 (opened): copy of Hiroshige's "Plum Garden in Kameido" (print n0077V1962); one of three Japanese prints he copied from his own collection; Vincent added two orange borders with Japanese characters "for a decorative and exotic effect"; the page does not say what the characters mean or whether he could read them.
- Google Arts & Culture record (artsandculture.google.com, opened, the VGM's own loan record): characters derived from Japanese examples; the text "has no coherent meaning", decorative.
- The Art Newspaper, 18 April 2025: two vertical strips of characters on the sides come "from other prints and have nothing to do with the garden scene"; Van Gogh traced the print, squared the tracing and enlarged it; British Museum scientist Capucine Korenberg matched a pencil line on his copy to the cut part of the tracing.
- Wikiwand "Japonaiserie (Van Gogh)" (LEAD): "borrowed from other prints".
- Whether he could read them: no opened source says; the "no coherent meaning" description implies he copied shapes. CAN'T VERIFY. Say "copied from other prints; the strip has no coherent meaning" and avoid claiming he could not read Japanese.
- Companion: Courtesan (after Eisen), 1887: copied via the May 1886 Paris Illustré cover (Japan issue, text by Tadamasa Hayashi) of a Keisai Eisen print (VGM s0116v1962, opened; Wikiwand LEAD).

### Siegfried Bing, Meiji exports, Expositions
- Bing supplied the prints (Art Newspaper 2018 and 2025; Apollo). Letter CL510 (Arles, 15 July 1888, to Theo, webexhibits.org/vangogh/letter/18/510.htm, opened): "There is an attic in Bing's house with thousands of prints piled up"; "In a way all my work is founded on Japanese art"; "Japanese art, decadent in its own country, takes root again among the French impressionist artists."
- Expositions: nippon.com ("When Japonism Bewitched Europe", nippon.com/en/column/g00284/) says Japan had extensive exhibits at the 1867 Paris Exposition which boosted enthusiasm, and that Paris hosted another in 1878; says nothing on Meiji state export policy. Meiji state-led export of art, the Vienna 1873 fair and the 1878 contribution: NOT VERIFIED in anything opened. CAN'T VERIFY the Meiji-state wording of the draft; needs a second source (e.g., a Japonisme scholarly source).

### What Japan meant to him: the South as his Japan
- CL500 (Arles, 5 June 1888, to Theo; webexhibits.org/vangogh/letter/18/500.htm, opened): "then why not go to Japan, that is to say to the equivalent of Japan, the South?"; staying in the South might give "an eye more Japanese"; "The Japanese draw quickly, very quickly, like a lightning flash."
- CL538 (c. 17 Sept 1888): "If there were less mistral, this place would really be as lovely as Japan." CL542 (24 Sept 1888): "I envy the Japanese the extreme clarity of everything in their work." Art Newspaper 2025: his July 1888 drawing inscription: "It does NOT look Japanese, and it's actually the most Japanese thing that I've done." Agreement across letters.

## Q5. The death

### Fact base
- Shot himself (on the traditional account) on Sunday 27 July 1890 in/near Auvers-sur-Oise; died 29 July 1890 at about 1:30 a.m. (Wikipedia chronology, LEAD; The Art Newspaper 2019: shot 27 July, died two days later). Auberge Ravoux: the innkeeper Arthur Ravoux (Art Newspaper 2019); the inn name itself not printed in that article. Age 37 on 29 July 1890 (born 30 March 1853; arithmetic). Dr Gachet wrote to Theo that Vincent "has wounded himself" (Art Newspaper 2019). Theo's account: last words "The sadness will last forever" (Wikipedia LEAD; letter not opened).

### The traditional account (suicide) and the case for it
- Martin Bailey, "Van Gogh's suicide: ten reasons why the murder story is a myth", The Art Newspaper (2019; updated 20 June 2025; theartnewspaper.com/2019/09/06/...): Gachet's note and later use of "suicide"; Theo's belief and the words he recorded; Bernard and Gauguin accounts; the police took no action on Adeline Ravoux's report of Vincent's words; church refused a funeral service; previous attempt July 1889 (paints and turpentine); distress over Theo's marriage and the Goupil troubles; Secrétan did not claim to have fired the shot; gun found abandoned in a field.
- Van Tilborgh & Meedendorp, "The life and death of Vincent van Gogh", The Burlington Magazine, July 2013 (not opened; reported in The Art Newspaper 7 Sept 2018 and DutchNews 11 Aug 2013): the Van Gogh Museum researchers examined the wound, the relationship between Vincent and Theo in his final weeks, and the letter found in his pocket; concluded the murder claim "does not add up" and that the chain of events leading to suicide is "eminently defensible, both psychologically and historically" (DutchNews). I did not read their paper and could not verify specific points on the wound angle in it.
- VGM position in 2011: curator Leo Jansen said "We cannot yet agree with their conclusions because we do not think there is enough evidence yet"; noted no independent evidence for Vincent's dying statement that he shot himself (NPR 17 Oct 2011); museum thought it "too early to change the cause of death from suicide" (DutchNews 2011).

### The accidental-shooting argument
- Steven Naifeh & Gregory White Smith, Van Gogh: The Life (2011), appendix "A Note on Vincent's Fatal Wounding" (per The Art Newspaper 2018): the shot came from a 16-year-old René Secrétan in Auvers on 27 July 1890; they say he did not specify murder or manslaughter, and Vincent "welcomed death" and said he shot himself to protect the boys (Art Newspaper 2019 summary of the argument).
- Evidence as reported by NPR (17 Oct 2011): the bullet's unusual angle in the abdomen (a doctor noticed), the missing easel, brushes and gun, no suicide note, Secrétan's behaviour. The Art Newspaper 2019: Secrétan's only public comments are in the March 1957 Aesculape interview (aged 82); he said Vincent obtained a gun that had been in Secrétan's possession; he did not claim to have fired it; he died months later. (An unopened claim that Vincent placed a large paint order days before also appears in the artnet piece.)
- Forensic commentary: Dr Vincent Di Maio (Fox News, 19 Nov 2014): wound "not self-inflicted"; muzzle "more than a foot or two away (closer to two rather than one)"; an abrasion ring is normal and the purple halo "means nothing"; close-range soot, tattooing and searing of skin would be expected and aren't described in forensic accounts. This is a commentary on the described wound, not on an autopsy report. I did not open the "Full Van Gogh Forensic Report" PDF on vangoghbiography.com (a site of unknown standing; do not rely on it).
- Wound-angle disagreement: The Art Newspaper 2019 notes the only descriptions of the wound come from Gachet's son after Gachet's death in 1909, are brief, and "the angle of the shot" has recently been interpreted differently. No autopsy or contemporaneous medical report is cited.
- Each side's strongest points (no verdict): for suicide: Theo and Gachet's contemporary statements, the 1889 attempt, distress, the unchallenged dying statement; for accidental shooting: wound angle and distance interpretations, missing gun and kit, no note, the 1957 Secrétan interview. Open question: no independent evidence either way beyond testimony.

### The revolver
- Auctioned 19 June 2019 at Hôtel Drouot, Paris, Salle 6, by AuctionArt Rémy Le Fur & Associés, €162,500 (estimate €40,000-60,000); Drouot's own news item calls it "alleged" and doesn't confirm provenance (drouot.com, opened). The Art Newspaper (2019): a corroded Lefaucheux revolver, found by a farmer about 1960 in a field where the wound is believed to have occurred; sold for €162,500. Agreement on date, price, venue. Authenticity unproven.
