## 3. Arles petition, February 1889
- Date: "late February 1889". Source: Bernadette Murphy, Van Gogh's Ear (2016), illustration list nos. 28-29, captions "The Arles petition, late February 1889 (© Archives Communales d'Arles)" and "signature of Joseph Gion, Arles Petition, late February 1889". Opened via the e-bookshelf XHTML copy of the book. This confirms the date and that the document is credited to the Archives Communales d'Arles (the Arles municipal archives).
- Number of names: 30. Source: Cosmopolis, "Van Gogh on the Verge of Insanity" (https://cosmopolis.ch/van-gogh-on-the-verge-of-insanity/), which reports "30 citizens of Arles", dated shortly before 27 February 1889, and says it draws on the Amsterdam exhibition catalogue's municipal-archive documents. Wikipedia "Hospital in Arles" and "Saint-Paul Asylum..." also say 30 but date it "March 1889", which conflicts with Murphy's caption; treat their date as wrong. The count is SINGLE-ISH (one non-Wikipedia source) so LOW CONFIDENCE. I could not open the letter notes at vangoghletters.org (JS shell).
- What it asked: Cosmopolis: residents wanted Van Gogh detained and kept from returning to his house; he "is not in full possession of his mental faculties" and "over-indulges in drink". Wikipedia (lead): called him "fou roux" and said he was "unfit to live freely"; police then closed the house. Van Gogh's own mention: letter 24 March 1889 to Theo, "municipal electors petitioning the Mayor" (webexhibits.org/vangogh/letter/19/581.htm, opened).
- Murphy's findings on signatories: NOT FOUND. Only the Joseph Gion signature caption is confirmed. Her chapter text needs a full-text copy of the book.
- Where held: Archives Communales d'Arles (Murphy's credit line). The Cosmopolis page does not name the archive.

## 4. Portrait of Dr Félix Rey
- Pushkin Museum record: the Pushkin "Art Patrons of Modern Art" site lists "Portrait of Doctor Rey, 64x53, oil on canvas" (https://artmaecenas.pushkinmuseum.art/data/authors/g/gogh_vincent_van/portrait_of_doctor_rey.php?lang=en) but I found no acquisition year or provenance text on the pages that load. NOT CONFIRMED: "Shchukin to State Museum of New Western Art to Pushkin 1948".
- Route (Wikipedia "Portrait of Doctor Rey", lead only, mostly unreferenced): painted ~7-17 Jan 1889; given to Rey; April 1901 Charles Camoin buys it and sends it to Marseille dealer Molinard; then Vollard (150 F); 1908 Cassirer/Druet; Druet sold it to Shchukin in 1908 for 4,600 F; seized by the Soviet state after 1917; de la Faille identified the sitter in 1924 (cited to AVA - Bulletin des amis du vieil Arles no. 135). Wikipedia's own cited sources: letter 571 (17 Jan 1889) and the Pushkin online entry. LOW CONFIDENCE for all sale details.
- Another Wikipedia page ("Dr. Felix Rey") cites Feilchenfeldt 2013 p. 306 for "1900-1901 Rey gave six Van Goghs to a dealer, possibly Lucien Molinard", an Open Forum Infectious Diseases article (Khoshbin & Katz 2015, "Van Gogh's Physician") for Vollard by 1903, and an interview of Rey by Max Braumann dated 1928 (reference [8]). The PMC article itself could not be opened (bot check).
- Chicken coop story, earliest teller: UNTRACEABLE so far. The Wikipedia text has no citation for it, and Daily Art Magazine, Joy of Museums and the Art Journey Substack repeat it without a source. Two secondary pages say Rey wrote that he was "simply horrified" (Joy of Museums) or give a "letter from 1930" (Substack); neither names the document. Best leads: the Braumann 1928 interview, Feilchenfeldt 2013, and the Khoshbin & Katz 2015 article's references. Irving Stone and 1920s-30s journalists were not checked.
- Discrepancy to flag: Substack says the portrait hung in the coop "twelve years"; Wikipedia says "over ten years". Neither is sourced.

## 5. Johanna's 1914 "Brieven aan zijn broeder"
- What she omitted: specifics NOT FOUND. Confirmed by opened sources:
  - Van Gogh Museum Journal 2002, "Paper endures: documentary research into the life and work of Vincent van Gogh" (author not shown in the extract; https://www.dbnl.org/tekst/_van012200201_01/_van012200201_01_0003.php): the 1914 edition was the basis for the 1952-54 Verzamelde brieven, in which V.W. van Gogh restored passages "that had previously been omitted for one reason or another"; Jo's biographical introduction "contains various errors and omissions". (A slightly different quote reading: "we now know it contains various errors and omissions".)
  - Van Gogh Museum Journal 1997-98 (https://dbnl.org/tekst/_van012199701_01/_van012199701_01_0006.php): Jo left out the second and third parts of letter 416/347 (printed only the first).
  - Nothing opened names Sien, Gauguin or money as the omitted topics. Leads: Luijten, Vincent van Gogh: The Full Story / the 2009 Letters edition introduction; vangoghletters.org "2.1 Around 1890" and "publications_3"; Jansen, Luijten & Bakker eds. (2009).
- Did it include Theo's letters? The 1914 edition contained Vincent's letters to Theo (VGM Journal 2002, same article); Theo's letters to Vincent are mentioned only as published in 1932. Wikipedia "The Letters of Vincent van Gogh" (lead): only 84 letters Vincent received survive, 39 from Theo. The first volume came out in spring 1914 (webexhibits Jo/Memoir: "The first volume of the Dutch edition was published in the spring of 1914"); Wikipedia says three volumes in January 1914 (conflict; unresolved).
- Theo's reburial at Auvers, 1914: two sources.
  1. The Art Newspaper, "Where was Van Gogh originally buried? We still don't know" (10 Sept 2021): Theo's remains moved to Auvers in 1914, months before WWI; Jo's decision; headstone "Theodore", both read "Ici repose". No exact day.
  2. Artnet News "Art Bites: Who is Jo van Gogh-Bonger" (https://news.artnet.com/art-world/art-bites-who-is-jo-van-gogh-bonger-2551340): in 1914 at Johanna's direction Theo's remains moved from Utrecht to Auvers.
  Wikipedia "Johanna van Gogh-Bonger" cites Luijten 2022 p. 293. Confidence: MEDIUM-HIGH.

## 6. French instituteur pay and franc parity
- Instituteur pay c. 1885-90: NOT FOUND. Opened and unhelpful: Sénat 1886 law PDF (refers to a separate pay law, no numbers); isidore.science 1890 arrêté (title only); a Lyon-3 memoir (no figures); encyclopedia.com "Teachers" (only 1833 minimum of 200 F, mid-century 450-500 F, and that the Ferry laws put pay on the state). The 1889 law's scale (800-2,000 F from pass-1 memory) is UNVERIFIED. Leads: Journal officiel 20-21 July 1889 on Gallica; Revue pédagogique 1889; Weber, Peasants into Frenchmen.
- Related, indirect: NYRB review "Van Gogh letters: his own worst enemy" (13 May 2021) says Theo's monthly allowance was twice a high school teacher's salary (reviewer's claim, no figures). LOW CONFIDENCE.
- Franc gold parity: 0.290322 g fine gold per franc. Source: Wikipedia "Latin Monetary Union" (lead) states one LMU franc = 4.5 g fine silver or 0.290322 g fine gold. Cross-checked against the Monetary Convention of 6 Nov 1885 (transcription of US Senate Ex. Doc., 49th Cong. 1st Sess., hosted at https://www.gold.org/sites/default/files/documents/1885nov6.pdf): a 20-franc gold piece weighs about 6.45 g at 900 fineness, which gives 5.81 g fine gold per 20 F, about 0.2903 g per franc (this is my arithmetic from the treaty table; the treaty does not state the per-franc figure). MEDIUM.
- US dollar: Bank of Canada (https://www.bankofcanada.ca/wp-content/uploads/2010/07/1854-1914.pdf) states the US returned to gold in 1879 at 23.22 grains. A banking text on chestofbooks.com ("Par of Exchange") gives the mint par of the franc as 19.3 US cents, which equals 5.18 F per dollar (1/0.193 = 5.181). That text's title and author are not shown, so LOW CONFIDENCE. Historical Statistics of the US (hsus.cambridge.org) appeared in search but was not opened.

## 7. Paintings Johanna sold; holdings at 1925 and 1962
- Total sold over her life: NO PUBLISHED TOTAL found. The Art Newspaper (2022, Jo Bonger) gives no number. Artnet says she chose which could be sold. VGM Journal 1995 (dbnl _van012199501_01_0002, "Paper endures"? actually the 1995 volume; opened): "with few exceptions, nothing more was sold until after Jo died in 1925."
- First sales: The Art Newspaper, "What were the first 12 Van Gogh paintings ever sold" (23 Sept 2022): 12 sold by one year after his death, 1 in his lifetime (The Red Vineyard, 400 F, 1890, Anna Boch); most 1891 sales were handled by Tanguy on Jo's behalf.
- Holdings: conflicting. Art Newspaper (Dec 2020, "Why the Van Gogh Museum might never have existed") says that in 1911 Jo owned around 200 paintings and 600 drawings; Art Newspaper (June 2020, Villa Eikenhof) says around 300 paintings and 500 drawings at the Bussum villa; Artnet says about 400 canvases after Theo died (disagrees with the rest and with Wikipedia's cited "about 200 paintings" from V.W. van Gogh's Memoir); Wikipedia (citing Cascone, Artnet 2025) says about 400 paintings at her death. VGM Journal 2002: Jo was left with "hundreds of paintings, drawings, prints and letters". I cannot give one exact figure; use "roughly 200 paintings at the start and about 200-300 at the end of her life, per sources that disagree".
- 1962: The State bought the collection for the Vincent van Gogh Foundation for 18,470,000 guilders (VGM Journal 2002). The Art Newspaper (Feb 2023, Choosing Vincent) says the family's decisions kept about a quarter of his paintings, half his drawings and nearly all his letters in one museum.
