## Chapter 1 · The Machine (paper): output vs. sales
- **Thesis beat:** as the brief: a production line whose two lines never meet.
- **Prequel strip, 1869–1880, five ✗ stamps, corrected:**
  1. Goupil clerk, July 1869, age 16, via his uncle Vincent (F30); London 1873, Paris 1875 (F31). Out on 1 April
     1876 after the firm resented an unauthorised Christmas trip home and a bad meeting with Boussod; he was poor
     with customers (F32). **"Fired for reading the Bible at the counter" has no source; cut it**, or keep it only
     as a stamped myth-check.
  2. Schoolmaster, Ramsgate then Isleworth, 1876 (F33).
  3. Bookshop clerk, Dordrecht, 1877 (F34).
  4. Theology prep, Amsterdam, May 1877–July 1878 (F35). The Latin-and-Greek tutor detail is unverified.
  5. Lay preacher, Borinage, 1879: a six-month trial post the committee **did not renew**, for excess zeal and
     self-neglect (F36). Not "dismissed for giving away his clothes".
  - End card 1880 as the brief (F07).
- **Sticky scene, changed:** the brief's paintings-per-year bars can't be sourced (F14: only a hobby site gives
  yearly tallies, and they contradict the brief's numbers in 8 of 10 years). **Proposal: bars by period, where
  each bar's width is the period's length and its height is paintings per month, so each bar's area is the number
  of paintings** (honest by construction, and it shows the acceleration better than years do):
  Nuenen ~200 in 24 months (F09) → Paris 200+ in 23 (F10) → Arles ~200 in 15 (F11) → Saint-Rémy ~150 in 12 (F12) →
  Auvers ~70 in 70 days (F13). The flat "lifetime sales" line with one gold dot at 1890 stays (F70).
  Caption bands: "about a painting every four days" (Nuenen) → "Arles: nearly 200 in fifteen months" →
  "Auvers: one a day" → "the market's answer, the whole way".
- **Footnotes:** F01, F07–F13, F30–F36, F70.

## Chapter 2 · The Subsidy (ochre field)
- **Thesis beat:** as the brief.
- **Stipend calculator:** slider default 150 F (the best-documented monthly rate, F21), range 100–250 F. Outputs:
  decade total, and comparisons in a coal miner's annual wage (about 1,070 F, F28) rather than a schoolmaster's
  (no source found yet). Dollar figures hedged: 1890 gold parity (F26) and a rough present-day figure (F27).
- **Text beats:** about 100 F a month early, 150 F in Nuenen, about 250 F in Arles by Vincent's own reckoning
  (F20–F22); Theo earned 11,000–14,000 F a year and about 14.5% of it went to Vincent (F24). The Goupil irony
  holds with a tweak: the firm that let Vincent go in 1876, renamed Boussod, Valadon & Cie in 1884, paid Theo's
  salary (F29, F32).
- **Footnotes:** F20–F29.

## Interlude · The Shopkeeper (paper, full-bleed plate)
- **As the brief**, with one fix: Tanguy was **not deported to New Caledonia**. He was arrested as a Communard in
  1871, held on the prison hulks at Brest and freed in 1873 (F93). His shop was at 14 rue Clauzel; he took
  paintings for paint (F93). After his death in 1894 his stock was sold at Drouot for his widow, about 10,000 F
  (F94). "Cupboard-adjacent stacks that turned out to be the family fortune" overstates it; the sale was a charity
  sale that raised a modest sum.
- **Barter-loop figure:** as the brief.
- **Footnotes:** F93–F94.

## Chapter 3 · The Beloveds (indigo field, star specks)
- **Thesis beat and the testability scale:** as the brief, with fixes and one suggested addition:
  1. **1873–74, Eugénie Loyer**, his London landlady's daughter. Jo's Memoir called her "Ursula", which was the
     mother's name; some scholars doubt he ever proposed (F37). The brief's "verdict delivered by her mother" can't
     be sourced.
  2. *(Suggested addition)* **1881, Kee Vos**, his widowed cousin: "never, no, never" (F38). She is the clearest
     documented refusal, and the scale reads better with one person who said no out loud.
  3. **1882–83, Sien Hoornik:** Theo opposed it (a wider family veto isn't documented). The bill arrived in 1904:
     she drowned on 22 November 1904 in a Rotterdam canal, the Provenierssingel, **not the Scheldt** (F39). One sober
     sentence, as the brief says.
  4. **1888, Japan:** "the equivalent of Japan, the South" (F43); about 660 prints bought (F41). **The "Meiji state
     catfishing back" line can't be sourced (F44)**; keep Japan as a country he invented rather than one that lied
     to him.
  5. **1888, the Studio of the South** (Ch4 sets it up).
  6. **1880–90, Theo.**
- **Japonaiserie plate:** the border characters were copied from other prints and make no coherent sense (F42).
  Don't say he couldn't read them; no source says so. The Hiroshige original beside the copy stays a strong plate.
- **Footnotes:** F37–F43.

## Chapter 4 · The Roommate (lamplight stage)
- **Strain gauge, re-ordered to the dates:** the portrait exchange happened **before** Gauguin arrived (Gauguin
  sent "Les Misérables" with letter 692 on 1 Oct; Vincent's bonze portrait, letter 695, 3 Oct), so it becomes the
  gauge's starting point, not its second event.
  1. ~1 Oct 1888: the portrait exchange (F59–F60).
  2. 23 Oct: Gauguin arrives; Theo pays him 150 F a month for paintings (F51–F52).
  3. Early Nov: Vincent paints *The Red Vineyard*, the one painting that will ever sell (F62).
  4. c. 11–12 Dec: Gauguin writes Theo that he must go back to Paris because of the "incompatibility" of their
     characters (F53). **The brief's "the work is unsalable" is not in the letter.**
  5. 23 Dec (red): the argument; the ear, all or nearly all of it (F55); given to Gabrielle Berlatier, a maid at the
     brothel the press called "Rachel" (F56). Gauguin's razor story comes from a book he wrote in 1903 (F54).
- **Coda:** Theo arrives on 25 Dec and leaves that evening with Gauguin (F57). The brief's "Studio of the South's
  total sales: zero" becomes a better line: its total sales were exactly one, *The Red Vineyard*, and it sold
  fifteen months later in Brussels (F62, F70). Gauguin's gyre as the brief, stated plainly: Tahiti 1891, the
  Marquesas 1901, dead at Atuona on 8 May 1903; by his own account he took a 13-year-old "wife" in Tahiti (F63).
- **Compare plate (monk vs. messiah):** as the brief; the swatch script measures the colours. Vincent's own words
  for his portrait are an ash-grey head, a reddish-brown jacket and a pale veronese green ground (F59), so the
  caption should come from the measured swatches, not from "orange robe". The third panel (*Christ in the Garden
  of Olives*, F61) stays, but "red hair as the tell" has no text source; keep it only if the swatches show it.
- **Footnotes:** F50–F63.

## Chapter 5 · Test the Legend (paper; verdict stamps)
1. "He sold only one painting." → **BURNISHED** holds: one documented sale, plus trades, gifts and a drawings
   commission (F70–F71).
2. "He cut off his ear." → **CONFIRMED** (changed from BURNISHED). Dr Rey's sketch shows the whole ear cut off, and
   the Van Gogh Museum now says the entire ear; Bailey says most of it (F55). The burnished version is the old
   "just the lobe" story.
3. "Nobody noticed him until after he died." → **BURNISHED** holds: Aurier's article (Jan 1890), Les XX and the
   Indépendants in 1890 (F72, F74).
4. "He was self-taught." → **BURNISHED** holds, with the numbers corrected: lessons from Mauve, about six weeks at
   the Antwerp academy (not a winter), a few months at Cormon's studio (F40).
5. "He shot himself in a wheat field." → **CONTESTED** holds: Naifeh & Smith (2011) vs. Van Tilborgh &
   Meedendorp (2013), Di Maio's forensic comment, the 2019 revolver sale (F95–F99).
- **Vignettes:** the petition holds (about 30 names, February 1889, asking that he be locked up or sent home;
  F58). The chicken coop is attributed to "the story goes": the Pushkin's own pages don't tell it, and the brief's
  "entered the Pushkin in 1960" has no support (F91–F92).
- **Footnotes:** F40, F55, F58, F70–F74, F91–F92, F95–F99.

## Chapter 6 · The Fix (paper)
- **Counterfactual:** the brief's calibration holds and the research supports it: Aurier, Les XX, the Indépendants
  and Anna Boch all came before his death (F70, F72, F74), and over 100 paintings were already in German
  collections by 1914 (F83). Keep it as a reasoned range, as the brief says.
- **Waffle:** ~860–900 squares, about 200 gold for what the family kept (F81); a second shade for the roughly
  190–250 works Johanna sold over her life (F80).
- **Price curve (log scale, nominal US dollars, converted at the period's gold parity):** 1890 *Red Vineyard*
  400 F ≈ $77 (F70) → 1901 Bernheim-Jeune, 16–31 March, 71 paintings by one source (F84) → 1905 Stedelijk show, nearly 500 works (F78) →
  **1907 Morozov buys *The Red Vineyard* for 30,000 F, 75 times its 1890 price** (F75; replaces the brief's
  unsupported "1960 Pushkin" point) → 1908 Kröller-Müller's first purchase (F82) → 1914 letters (F85) → 1973 museum
  opens (F87) → 1987 *Sunflowers* £24.75m and *Irises* $53.9m (F88–F89) → 1990 *Gachet* $82.5m, a record until
  2004 (F90). The gold dot from Ch1 recurs at 1890. "Stedelijk acquisitions from 1905" is cut (F79).
- **Johanna, corrected:** she was 28; her son was almost a year old when Theo died, not newborn; she **did** go home
  to the Netherlands, to Bussum, where she ran a boarding house, but she kept the work instead of selling it off
  (F76). **She remarried in 1901** (the painter Johan Cohen Gosschalk, who died in 1912; F77), so "she never
  remarried" goes. The "widowed him twice" line gets a literal second half: in 1914, the year the letters came out,
  she had Theo's body moved from Utrecht to Auvers beside Vincent (F86, two sources). **What she cut from the 1914
  letters isn't documented beyond "errors and omissions" and one cut letter (F85)**, so the brief's "sanded the
  friction out of the brothers' marriage" can't stand as fact. What can: the 1914 edition printed only Vincent's
  side, so Theo appears as the man being written to, never as a voice (F05, F85). That carries the brief's
  caveat (a) and the "buried the husband a second time, in print" line without claiming edits nobody has listed.
- **Footnotes:** F70, F72, F74–F90.

## Chapter 7 · The Scoreboard (red field)
- **Rows:** ~860–900 paintings (F01) · more than 1,100 works on paper (F02) · 820 letters (F04) · about 16,000 F
  wired, 13,000–19,000 (F25) · 1 documented sale (F71) · ~30 names (F58) · 37 years (F95) · 100 years from death to
  the record (July 1890 → May 1990, F90).
- **Closing:** as the brief. The gyre's last turn reads: fired clerk, unrenewed preacher, a man in love with a Japan
  he invented, the ex-Communard paint dealer, the self-mythologizing stockbroker, the widow with a ledger.
- **Footnotes:** all of the above.

## Claims in the brief the research contradicts or can't support

**Wrong (change or cut):**
1. Paintings-per-year numbers (§6): unsourced, and wrong for 8 of 10 years against the only tally found (F14).
2. Goupil "fired for reading the Bible at the counter": no source (F32).
3. Borinage "dismissed": a trial post not renewed (F36).
4. "Ursula Loyer ... verdict delivered by her mother": Ursula was the mother; the beloved was probably Eugénie
   (F37).
5. Sien "the Scheldt, 1904": a Rotterdam canal, 22 Nov 1904 (F39); "the family vetoed it" is documented only for
   Theo.
6. "A winter at Antwerp's academy": about six weeks (F40).
7. Tanguy "deported to New Caledonia, amnestied 1880": held at Brest, freed 1873 (F93).
8. Gauguin wrote Theo "the work is unsalable": he wrote about their incompatible characters (F53).
9. The portrait exchange came after Gauguin's arrival: it came before (F59–F60).
10. "Part of the left ear" / ear verdict BURNISHED: all or nearly all; verdict CONFIRMED (F55).
11. "Rachel of the rue du Bout d'Arles": Gabrielle Berlatier, a maid; "Rachel" was the press's name (F56).
12. "Studio of the South total sales: zero": one, *The Red Vineyard* (F62).
13. "Boussod & Valadon show, January 1890, ~20 works": no trace (F73).
14. "Stedelijk acquisitions from 1905 onward": no trace; 1905 was Johanna's exhibition there (F78–F79).
15. "Dr Rey portrait ... entered the Pushkin in 1960": unsupported; likely 1948 (F91).
16. Johanna "never remarried": she married Johan Cohen Gosschalk in 1901 (F77).
17. Johanna "with a newborn" and "declined to come home": the son was nearly one; she moved back to the
   Netherlands (F76).
18. "~800 letters": 820 by Vincent, 903 in all (F03–F04).
19. Van Gogh Museum "founded 1962": the Foundation dates from 1960; 1962 is when the state bought the collection
    (F87).

**Can't support yet (hedge, attribute, or wait for pass 2):**

    unsupported; the one-sided edition is the sourced version of the point.
24. "Roughly a schoolmaster's wages": no teacher's pay found; a coal miner's pay is sourced (F28).

28. Mère Tanguy's stacks as "the family fortune": the 1894 sale raised about 10,000 F (F94).
