# Research prompts: Sicilian folk magic

Brief: `proj/sicilian-folk-magic/brief.md` (APPROVED Nov 22, 2026). At Bryan's ask, Claude runs the research itself:
two independent passes per group (separate workers that don't see each other's work), each with a different
starting lens so their mistakes differ, then a gap-fill / fact-check pass. Reports are saved as
`<group>-pass<N>.md` in this folder and cross-checked into `../figures.md`.

- **Pass 1 lens:** English-language scholarship first (university presses, peer-reviewed journals in folklore,
  anthropology, religious history; museum and archive catalogues).
- **Pass 2 lens:** Italian-language scholarship and Sicilian / Italian institutions first (dioceses, the Regione
  Siciliana, universities of Palermo, Catania and Messina, Museo Pitrè, state archives, Italian journals), then
  English.

## Rules for every prompt (pasted into each)
1. **Sources:** for every figure, date, name, count or quoted text give the title, author or institution, year, page
   or table, and a link. Primary sources (archives, published trial records, diocesan or civic records, ethnography,
   museum catalogues) before secondary; scholarly secondary before popular.
2. **Banned as citations:** Holocaust-denier or revisionist sites; wikis and aggregators (Wikipedia, Fandom, Kiddle and
   similar) except as leads to their own references; Medium and Reddit are leads only, never the citation for a figure.
   Trace every figure to the original institution, book or journal. Tourism sites and listicles are leads only.
3. **Disagreement:** where reputable sources differ, list each value with its source and why they differ
   (definitions, dates, what was counted, method). Never "give me the number".
4. **Myths:** popular claims (especially "this is an unbroken survival of a pagan cult") are traced to where they
   started and labeled: supported, contested, or unsupported, with who argues each side.
5. **Estimates are labeled** as estimates; no unsourced round numbers.
6. **Say whether you opened the source** yourself or only saw it cited by another work.
7. **Belief is reported as belief,** respectfully: what practitioners hold, what a charm or rite is for, who holds it,
   and where that is recorded. Never written as if it works, never mocked.
8. **Output:** a short summary, then a table
   `| Figure / claim | Value or range | Unit | Source (title, author/institution, year, page) | Link | Opened? | Notes |`,
   then disagreements, then myths, then a full source list.

## Group A: primer and timeline (brief Q5, Q1)
1. What do scholars mean by "syncretism", "folk Catholicism" / "popular religion" (religione popolare), "vernacular
   religion" and "pagan survival", and what are the main arguments against the "pagan survival" model (for example
   the critique of the survivals idea in folklore studies)? Name the key scholars and works, especially those writing
   on Sicily and southern Italy (for example Giuseppe Pitrè, Ernesto de Martino, Annabella Rossi, Luigi Lombardi
   Satriani, Gustav Henningsen, and any others the sources show).
2. A dated timeline of Sicily's successive rulers and peoples relevant to religion (Sicani/Siculi/Elymians,
   Phoenician/Punic, Greek, Roman, Byzantine, Arab/Islamic, Norman, Hohenstaufen, Angevin, Aragonese/Spanish,
   Bourbon, unification), with start and end years as the sources give them.
3. For each practice in groups B and D, the earliest date it is recorded in Sicily and the source of that record.

## Group B: what's distinctly Sicilian; saints and feasts (brief Q2, Q3)
1. Which religious practices are distinctive to Sicily (or far stronger there than in Catholicism at large)? For each:
   what it involves, where and when, and its first recorded date. Include, where the sources support them: the cult of
   Saint Rosalia in Palermo (festino), Saint Agatha in Catania, the Altars / Tables of Saint Joseph (tavolate di San
   Giuseppe), the cult of the souls in Purgatory (anime del Purgatorio) and the decollati (the beheaded souls), the
   Day of the Dead gifts from the dead (Festa dei Morti, Nov 2) and its sweets, ex-votos, vare/fercoli processions,
   Holy Week rites (for example the Misteri of Trapani), the Madonna della Lettera in Messina, and others the sources
   show.
2. Which of these are claimed to sit on older (Greek, Punic, Roman, Arab) cults (for example Rosalia or Agatha and a
   Demeter/Persephone, Isis or other goddess cult; the Enna / Demeter claims), what evidence is offered, and what
   scholars say about each claim?
3. Counts: how many patron saints and patronal feasts are there across Sicily's municipalities (390-ish comuni; give
   the source's figure), how many major processions or feste are documented, and any official lists (for example
   UNESCO or Regione Siciliana registers of intangible heritage, the REIS register). Give the counting rule each source
   uses.

## Group C: relics (brief Q4)
1. Which notable relics are held in Sicily (for example of Rosalia, Agatha, Lucy, others), where, since when, and how
   they reached Sicily (translations, thefts, gifts), with dates.
2. How many churches or reliquaries claim the same relic of a single saint (fingers, arms, heads, bodies of the same
   saint in several places; the famous cases of multiple heads or bodies, including any Sicilian saints split between
   Sicily and elsewhere, for example Saint Lucy between Syracuse and Venice), with counts and sources. Include the
   history of relic multiplication and its critics (for example Calvin's *Treatise on Relics*, 1543) and the Church's
   own rules on relics (classes of relics, the ban on selling them in canon law), with citations.
3. Any count of reliquaries or relic-holding churches in Sicily or in a Sicilian diocese, with the counting rule.

## Group D: the Inquisition, healers, the evil eye and the donne di fuora (brief Q6)
1. The Spanish Inquisition in Sicily: when it operated (dates of establishment and abolition), how many cases it
   tried, and how many concerned magic, superstition or witchcraft; the published counts (for example Gustav
   Henningsen's work on the *donne di fuora* / "ladies from outside", the relaciones de causas, and others the sources
   show), with years and pages.
2. Who the *donne di fuora* were in the trial records: what they were said to do, how many cases, and how scholars
   read them (fairy cult, shamanistic, other), with the debates.
3. Folk healers and charms: the *ciarmavermi*, *magare* / *maàre*, the evil eye (*malocchiu*, *jettatura*, the
   *affascinu*), the prayers and oil-in-water rites used against it, and how they mix Catholic prayer with older
   material; recorded charm texts with their source (Pitrè and later ethnography). Report what practitioners believe
   the rites do.
4. Any modern survey or ethnographic count of belief or practice (for example how many people in Sicily or southern
   Italy report belief in the evil eye, or use healers), with method and year.

## Group E: Sicily and Latin America (brief Q7)
1. Is there a documented historical connection between Sicilian Catholic practice and Latin American Catholic
   practice? Look for: shared Spanish (Aragonese/Habsburg) rule and its religious institutions (the Inquisition, the
   Counter-Reformation, confraternities), baroque church art and its spread, shared saint cults (for example Saint
   Joseph's table, the souls in Purgatory, the Day of the Dead), Sicilian or southern Italian migration to Latin
   America (Argentina, Brazil, Venezuela), and scholarly comparisons. Separate documented links from resemblance.
2. Where Sicilian and Latin American practices look alike (candles, ex-votos, processions, altars for the dead), do
   scholars attribute it to common origin (Spain, Counter-Reformation baroque), to parallel development, or to
   migration? Give each view with its source.

## Group F: images (for the cutout look, brief §8)

   saints and goddesses in groups B–D (Rosalia, Agatha, Lucy, Joseph, Demeter/Ceres, Persephone/Proserpina, Isis),
