## Summary

1. **Sicily has 391 comuni** (municipalities). That has been true since 20 Feb 2021, when Misiliscemi split from
   Trapani (ISTAT). ISTAT's change log runs to 2026 and shows no later change in Sicily except a name change
   (Tripi became "Tripi - Abakainon", 13 June 2025). Before 2021 it was 390 (ANCI/IFEL atlas, data to 12 Jan 2015).
   In 1899 Pitrè wrote of 357.
2. **No modern count of patron saints across Sicily's comuni was found.** The only count is Pitrè's own, in
   *Feste patronali in Sicilia* (preface dated 23 Dec 1899). His counting rule is one principal patron per town in
   his sample of "150" or "160" towns (he uses both figures). Out of 150: 50 different saints are patrons of 88
   towns, Mary of 45, the Crucifix of 13. Among the saints, **St Joseph leads with 13 towns** ("9 per 100, quasi",
   almost 9 in 100). Female saints are patrons of no more than 16 towns, and Rosalia of 7. A side figure from a
   different measure (towns *named* after saints, not patrons): **40 of Sicily's 390 comuni** have a saint's name,
   15 of them female, the highest female share of any region (Ausloos & Cerqueti 2016).
3. **No count of reliquaries for Sicily or any Sicilian diocese was found.** BeWeB (the Italian bishops'
   catalogue) failed to load on three tries (home page, section page, and a work page with no filters), so I couldn't
   filter it. One church-level figure exists: Palermo Cathedral's own page counts **17 reliquaries** in its Chapel of
   the Relics (6 silver-topped urns, the urn of St Cosmas, and 10 carved wooden reliquaries). For Agatha, the City of
   Catania lists **31 places outside Catania** said to hold her relics: 17 in Italy and 14 abroad, across 6
   countries.
4. **REI recount** (the Regione's 2013 register, in the copy held on UNESCO's site): **177 entries**. By book:
   **Celebrazioni 86, Saperi 38, Espressioni 32, Tesori Umani Viventi 21** (my count). Every number from 1 to 177
   was checked: each appears once, with no gaps. **St Joseph: 11 entries**, all in Celebrazioni, one of them shared
   with St Blaise (Salemi). The feasts book is not all religious: at least 16 entries are plainly secular by their
   names (carnivals, jousts, the Targa Florio race, anti-mafia memorials). That classification is mine, not the
   register's. No current REIS total was found from the Regione itself.
5. **Agatha 1039 vs 1040: neither year rests on a primary source.** Papasidero (2025) gives 1040, with no footnote.
   He says the Byzantine historians don't mention the removal, and Bishop Maurice's letter gives no year for it. The
   City of Catania's "86 years" before 17 Aug 1126 works back to 1040. 1040 is the last year of Maniakes's campaign
   (1038–1040, Samčević 2024), so either year is an inference. **Print it as "about 1040, during George Maniakes's
   campaign of 1038–40".**
6. **Messina.** Legend: a letter from Mary, dated 42 CE, tied with a lock of her hair. Evidence: the oldest known
   text of the letter is on an **engraving of 1522** (Campagna 2025), and the oldest printed Latin version is by
   **Maurolico, 1556** (Franceschini 2019). Rome's Holy Office condemned the text as apocryphal in 1598. Inchofer's
   1629 defence of it was censured by the Congregation of the Index. The cult grew after a famine ended on 3 June
   1636 and the Senate's vow of 30 July 1636 (Ciaramitaro 2005). **The Hair** is a real object: the state catalogue
   (ICCD) records a small gold-and-enamel capsule reliquary, the "Reliquiario del capello della Madonna", Sicilian
   workshop, late 16th to early 17th c., in the Cathedral Treasury. It was carried in the 1657 procession
   (Ciaramitaro), and the diocese still "enthrones" the "sacri capelli" (sacred hairs) on the silver *vara*
   (processional float) each year. Its first record was not found.

## Disagreements

- **Agatha 1039 vs 1040.** Neither has a primary source. Papasidero (2025) says 1040 without a footnote. The City
  of Catania says 1040, and so does its "86 years" before 1126. NLM (popular) and C-pass1's "popular accounts" say
  1039. The Byzantine chronicles are silent (Papasidero), and Maurice's letter gives no year. The campaign ran
  1038–1040 (Samčević 2024, from Skylitzes). **Why they differ:** each year is worked out from the campaign or
  from the "86 years", not read in a source. Suggested wording: "around 1040".
- **Maurice's letter date.** Papasidero says it was "composed between 1155 and 1169", but also that Maurice died in
  1143/44. B-pass1 and C-pass1 flagged this. It stays unresolved. Cite the letter as "Bishop Maurice's account
  (BHL 139)" with no date.
- **Oldest record of the Messina Letter.** 1522 engraving (Campagna 2025) vs 1556 Maurolico print (Franceschini
  2019). They measure different things: Franceschini's 1556 is the oldest *Latin* version *in print*, and
  Campagna's 1522 is an engraving carrying the text in Italian. Both agree the cult is 16th-century, and that the
  15th-century "Lascaris" find is a claim made by supporters. "Fin dal medioevo" (since the Middle Ages;
  Ciaramitaro 2005) is unsupported.
- **Pitrè's denominator: 150 or 160.** He uses both in §VII. His counts (45, 13, 88, 16, 7) are of 150. Write
  "about 150 towns he studied".
- **Agatha's breast relic.** Catania's city page says Gallipoli had it from 1126 to 1389 and Galatina since. C-pass2
  (Inveges 1651) gives Galatina since 1389. These agree. Papasidero's Taranto story (a breast lost during Mass and
  found by a nursing child) is a separate legend.

## Myths and labels

- "Agatha's body was taken in 1039" / "in 1040": **contested**. Say "about 1040".
- "The Letter has been venerated since the Middle Ages": **unsupported**. The oldest firm date is 1522.
- "The Letter was found in Greek and translated by Constantine Lascaris": **unsupported**. It is the supporters'
  claim (Franceschini 2019).
- "The lock of hair is Mary's": **belief**. The reliquary is real and dated c. 1600 (ICCD). What it holds is held by
  faith, and the Church condemned the letter's text in 1598.
