## Short summary

- **Dates.** The Spanish Inquisition reached Sicily by papal act in 1486 (24 March, Innocent VIII) and royal
  appointment in 1487; its district tribunal is usually dated as working steadily from 1500 (formally 8 Nov 1500).
  It was abolished by Ferdinand IV's decree of **16 March 1782**, and its archive was ordered burned on
  **27 June 1783**. From 1601/1605 to 1782 it sat in Palazzo Chiaromonte, the **Steri**, in Palermo, whose prison
  graffiti Pitrè uncovered in **1906**.
- **How many cases.** Totals differ by what was counted: **7,161** people tried (Renda 1997); Messana's database of
  **6,393** trials ("more than 6,000"; the publisher says "up to 6,500"); **3,188** Sicilian cases in the surviving
  *relaciones de causas* for 1540–1700 (Contreras & Henningsen 1986), recalculated to 4,057 and estimated at 6,302
  if the lost years are filled in (Henningsen 1993).
- **Magic and witchcraft.** About **921** defendants (427 men, 494 women) in Renda's categories; Messana counts
  **976** witchcraft causes, ending in **565 acquittals** and only **2** handed to the secular arm (death). Henningsen
  knew of **no witch-burning** by this tribunal. That is the headline: Sicily's Inquisition tried many magic cases
  and burned (almost) no witches.
- **The *donne di fuora*.** "Ladies from outside": women (and a few men) who said they went out at night, in spirit,
  with fairy companies, and who healed people in return for respect and small payments. Counts in the trial summaries
  differ: **65 trials (8 men), 1579–1651** (Henningsen, seen only as cited), **193** "ladies" (Geremia's reading of
  Henningsen), **77** (Messana). Readings: a fairy cult with a "white" sabbath (Henningsen), a piece of a wider
  Eurasian substrate of night-travel beliefs (Ginzburg), and a mostly therapeutic, Christianized healing practice
  (Messana). New full trials from the Monreale diocesan archive (Geremia 2024) show healers like **Diana La
  Viscusa** (1638–39) and **Gracia la Chiaczisa** (1619) in their own words.
- **Healers, charms and the evil eye.** Pitrè's *Medicina popolare siciliana* (1896) records healing by prayer, by
  touch, by hereditary "gift" families, and by oil and wool with an *orazione*; episcopal courts (Isnello, 1557–1614)
  tried *magare* for love charms, harm and healing, including an oil divination ("lo sortilegio dello oglio").
  Recorded evil-eye charm texts and modern surveys are **not sourced in this pass** (see gaps).

## Disagreements

1. **When the Inquisition "began" in Sicily.** 1486 (papal act, Geremia n. 3), 1487 (first inquisitor appointed,
   La Motta; Fiume; Liberti), 1500 (district tribunal formally set up, 8 Nov 1500, La Motta; "only began in 1500",
   Geremia). They measure different things: authorisation, appointment, a working court. La Motta also notes earlier,
   medieval papal inquisitors in Sicily from 1252 (52 of them by 1500). Suggested wording: "brought to Sicily in
   1487, sitting in Palermo from 1500 to 1782".
2. **How many people the tribunal tried.** 7,161 (Renda 1997) vs. 6,393 / "more than 6,000" / "up to 6,500"
   (Messana's database) vs. 3,188 → 4,057 → est. 6,302 for 1540–1700 only (Contreras & Henningsen; Henningsen
   1993). Different spans (whole life vs. 1540–1700), different sources (sentences and letters vs. surviving annual
   reports), and different units (persons vs. trials). Show as a range, "about 6,000 to 7,000 people over three
   centuries", with the note.
3. **How many magic/witchcraft cases.** 921 (Renda's category, persons) vs. 976 (Messana, witchcraft causes) vs. 456
   (1547–1701, *relaciones* only, blog lead attributed to Henningsen). The first two agree within ~6% and come from
   different counting projects; the third is a narrower window and is unverified.
4. **Executions for witchcraft.** Messana: 2 handed to the secular arm (Fiume n. 36); Profeta: only two men, no
   woman; Henningsen: no witch-burning known for this tribunal. These fit together if the two men were necromancers
   or the handover did not end in a burning for *witchcraft*; the sources don't say. Safe wording: "almost none:
   two men handed over for execution in three centuries, and no woman executed as a witch."
5. **Executions overall.** 52 in 1540–1700 (Monter) and 207 in person before 1540 (Del Col) are scholarly; "at
   least 284 burned" (Liberti, journalism) gives no source. Most were for Judaizing in the early decades, not magic.
6. **How many *donne di fuora*.** 65 trials with 8 men, 1579–1651 (Henningsen as cited by Gijswijt-Hofstra &
   Levack, not opened); 193 "ladies" (Geremia reading Henningsen); 77 (Messana). The likeliest reason is unit: one
   trial could name a whole fairy "company" of women, so 193 people in 65 trials is possible, and Messana's 77 may
   count only defendants charged as such. **Not resolved here**; needs Henningsen 1990 pp. 191–215 opened.
7. **When the Steri became the Inquisition's seat.** 1600 (royal order), 1601 (move, La Motta; Liberti), 23 July
   1605 (formal possession, Fiume). Different events.
8. **Messana's first edition year.** 2007 (most sources) vs. 2002 (La Motta). Treat 2007 as correct.

## Myths

| Popular claim | Where it starts | Label | Who argues each side |
|---|---|---|---|
| "The *donne di fuora* were an unbroken pagan fairy cult surviving from antiquity." | Pitrè's 19th-c. folklore framing (fairy and witch at once); Henningsen 1990 reconstructs a "fairy cult"; Borghini 2008 proposes a Greek ancestor-spirit parallel; neo-pagan writers (lead seen: a "Stregheria" site, not opened) push it further. | **Contested** (a cult of some kind in the 16th–17th c.: supported by trial records; an unbroken line from antiquity: **unsupported**) | For a deep substrate: Ginzburg 1989 (Eurasian night-travel beliefs). For a reading as early-modern, Christianized healing practice: Messana 2007 (curative spells, therapeutic exorcism). Henningsen sits between: real collective belief and dream-experience, documented only from the 15th c. on (Confessionale). Borghini labels his own link a hypothesis. |
| "The Sicilian Inquisition burned thousands of witches." | General Inquisition and "Burning Times" lore. | **Unsupported** | Messana (2 handed over), Profeta (no woman executed as a witch), Henningsen (no witch-burning known). Most acquitted (565 of 976). |
| "Witch-hunting there was all about women." | Common image of the witch. | **Contested / mostly unsupported for Sicily** | Renda's figures: 427 men vs. 494 women among magic defendants; Lovison (p. 15) and Messana note men were not condemned less. Male necromancers and treasure-hunters were a big group. The *donne di fuora* subgroup was mostly women (65 trials, 8 men, if Henningsen's figure holds). |
| "The Steri graffiti are by witches." | Tours and press. | **Unsupported as a general claim** | Fiume 2017: prisoners of all charges; only one graffito securely by a woman; the women's prison for *magare* was a separate, later complex (1650/1658). |
