## How sources were counted

- **Counts toward the two:** primary documents (Church law, medieval texts in an edition, state heritage catalogue
  records, diocesan texts), scholarly work (books, journal articles, university theses at master's level or above,
  named reference works such as the *Catholic Encyclopedia*, flagged as old), and national press (*Avvenire*,
  *L'Osservatore Romano*) as support only.
- **Church and state bodies** (a cathedral, an archdiocese, a sanctuary, Vatican City State, Vatican News, the Comune
  di Venezia's 1600 site, the Regione) count as **primary for their own tradition, acts and objects** ("the Church
  holds", "the archdiocese enthroned the hair in 2026"), and as ordinary support for claims about the distant past.
- **Never counts:** Wikipedia, the Comune di Catania **tourism portal**, blogs (New Liturgical Movement, History Walks
  in Venice, ScienceBlogs), web mirrors, image-agency captions, and local online news and magazines (Balarm,
  BlogSicilia, LiveSicilia, LiveUniversity, NewSicilia, Quotidiano di Ragusa, *La Nuova Bussola Quotidiana*). The
  local news sites are listed as leads; several restate a diocese's own announcement, so they aren't independent of it.
- **Same source, several reports:** Papasidero 2025 (C1, G2), Franceschini 2019 (B1, G2), Scalisi 2023 (B1, B2),
  the 2017 Instruction (C1, C2, EN and IT texts), the Catania archbishop's 2026 texts (C2, G2; one author, four
  texts) each count **once**.
- Aaron 2015 (a BA thesis) is a lead, not a scholarly source.

**What the tally can honestly say.** The reports contain **no** case of two places claiming the same finger. The
strongest same-part double claims are Lucy's body (Venice and Metz) and Lucy's head (Venice and Bourges, X2
synthesis), with Louis IX's heart disputed between Monreale and Saint-Denis. Agatha is the "spread" story (pieces said
to be in 31 places beyond Catania, per one tourism page) more than a "duplicate" story: where the parts can be
counted (breasts, femurs), the numbers add up. No reliquary count exists for Sicily or any diocese (X2-148); the only
counted figure is one chapel in Palermo Cathedral, 17 reliquaries, from the cathedral's own page (X2-97).

## Myth-check summary (the four examples in the brief)

- **"1040" removal (X2-03):** not a myth about the event; Maniakes taking Agatha is in Maurice and the Latin
  chroniclers (X2-02, `checked`). The precise year is an inference: no primary source gives it. Status `disputed`,
  not `myth`; print "about 1040" or "11th century".
- **Buckland's goat bones (X2-93):** `myth`. Traced to Tuckwell (1900) via a 2009 blog; Gordon (1894) shows only
  that Buckland had some Rosalia episode. Lead for anyone with web access: Gordon's ch. IV (from p. 87) on archive.org.
- **"First / second / third class" relics as Church law (X2-132):** `myth`. The Vatican's 2017 text has two
  categories, *insigni* and *non insigni*. The origin of the three-class scheme isn't traced.
- **"A dozen heads of John the Baptist" said by Calvin (X2-145):** `myth` as an attribution. It's Krasinski's
  19th-c. footnote.
- Also labelled: Lionheart's crown (X2-21), the nightclothes origin of the *sacco* (X2-34), Lucy's body in Syracuse
  (X2-66), the Lascaris translation (X2-107), the Letter venerated since the Middle Ages (X2-108).
- **Beliefs are not myths.** The veil stopping lava (X2-28), Rosalia ending the plague (X2-80) and the hair being
  Mary's (X2-98, X2-111) are recorded beliefs. Per the brief's landmines, they go on the page as "devotees hold…",
  not under a myth-check label.

## Decisions for Bryan

Only disputes that would change what the page shows.

1. **When Maniakes took Agatha (X2-03, with X2-09).** No primary source gives a year. Show "about 1040, during
   Maniakes's campaign of 1038–40" (and then "86 years" away), or "in the 11th century, under Maniakes" (and "more
   than 80 years")?
2. **Louis IX's heart (X2-116, tally T10).** His confessor's account puts the heart at Monreale with the entrails;
   Le Goff thinks it went to Saint-Denis with the bones. Show "his entrails (and, by one medieval account, his
   heart)", or count the heart as a Monreale relic with a note?
3. **When Lucy reached Venice (X2-48).** 1204 is the sack of Constantinople; 1206 is the official translation the
   Venice sanctuary uses (via a local news site). Show "after 1204", or "1204, officially welcomed 1206"?
4. **Who found Rosalia's bones (X2-68).** The 17th-century accounts disagree (a sick woman's vision; a sailor who
   dug; a woman "also named Rosalia"). Name Geronima La Gattuta as the tradition's guide, or keep it nameless ("a
   sick woman's vision")?
5. **Who received Agatha's relics in Constantinople (X2-07).** Emperor Michael IV or Empress Theodora, in late or
   popular sources. Leave it out, or say the sources don't agree?
