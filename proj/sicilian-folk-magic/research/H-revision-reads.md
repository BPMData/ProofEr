**Answers to your questions**

1. **What the veil is.** Ruiz Jiménez (Zenodo abstract, Spanish original): "el velo de Santa Águeda, al que se presuponía el poder taumatúrgico de frenar las coladas de lava." English (the same page's English abstract): "the veil of Saint Agatha, which was believed to have the thaumaturgical power to stop the lava flows." The veil is one of several relics carried in processions. The Wikipedia article calls it "the veil of St. Agatha" and does not describe it further.

2. **Start date.** Wikipedia infobox: "Start date: 11 March 1669", end date 15 July 1669. Italysvolcanoes: seismicity from more than two months before, "earthquakes became particularly intense on 8 March 1669," and "On the afternoon of 11 March, probably at around 16 h, several vents burst open." Wikipedia says seismic activity began 25 February, and the first fissure opened "after midnight on 11 March." Ruiz Jiménez gives "Entre marzo y julio de 1669" (between March and July).

3. **When lava reached Catania.** Ruiz Jiménez: "en la ciudad de Catania a la que la lava llegó el 30 de abril" (lava reached the city on 30 April). That date is the one for lava entering through the walls. Italysvolcanoes says the flow reached the walls "in early April," and that on 30 April "a section of the walls gave way," with lava entering from the west. Wikipedia says the flow reached the walls "on the 1 or 16 April," with sources disagreeing. Wikipedia: "The city walls resisted the advancing lava for 15 days." It adds: "Beginning on 30 April, some flows overtopped the walls and penetrated Catania."

4. **Which parts of Catania were destroyed.**
- Western city and walls: Italysvolcanoes says "Damage was most widespread on the W side of the Sangiuliano hill" (paraphrased; this is a quote from the page). Wikipedia says "the western part of the city sustained damage."
- Benedictine monastery and San Nicolò l'Arena: Italysvolcanoes says "Two lobes of lava surrounded and partially destroyed churches, monastries and other buildings" around San Nicolò l'Arena. Wikipedia says the Monastery of San Nicolò l'Arena "and the Castello Ursino were damaged." The Benedictine monastery is mentioned as lying in the damage zone, but I found no source that separately confirms its destruction.
- Harbour and sea: Italysvolcanoes: "On 23 April the lava ... entered the sea with a front more than 1 km wide and rapidly filled the harbor of the city." Wikipedia: "The city walls held up the lava, which began to flow into the Ionian Sea," and "the already poor port was further damaged by the eruption" (the port was later rebuilt).
- Castello Ursino: Wikipedia says its lower part "was buried beneath 9–12 m (30–39 ft) of lava."
- Scale: Wikipedia says "Contrary to common reports, not all of Catania was destroyed," and that "about 730 [sic]–300 buildings were destroyed." The 730 figure is garbled in the source, so treat that figure as uncertain.

5. **City centre and cathedral.** No source I read says the 1669 lava destroyed the cathedral or the city centre. Italysvolcanoes' figure caption says the cathedral's medieval architecture "was destroyed in the 1693 earthquake, along with large part of the city." Its caption also says "The Castello Ursino ... survived both the eruption and the earthquake." The Wikipedia summary says the historic Baroque centre "was built after the eruption and earthquake" and that the 1693 earthquake caused more damage in Catania than the eruption.

6. **Did the veil save the city? How was it interpreted?**
- Wikipedia (quote): "Some sources suggest the veil of St. Agatha spared the city from total destruction." It cites Chester et al. (1986), p. 356. The full reference is not in the excerpt I read.
- Wikipedia (quote): "Eruptions of Etna were interpreted as the consequence of divine wrath and suffering being inflicted on the sinful people."
- Wikipedia (quote): "during processions the relics of St. Agatha, the Martyr of Catania, were carried around and people flagellated themselves." (paraphrased in part)
- Ruiz Jiménez: the abstract describes the veil as "believed to have the thaumaturgical power to stop the lava flows." It does not say whether people credited it with saving the city.
- Italysvolcanoes gives the credit for limiting damage to the city's authorities. Vicar "don Stefano Reggio" ordered stone barriers across the main streets, and Pagnano (1992) judged them "highly functional and successful." Wikipedia spells the vicar "Riggio," and says the viceroy appointed "Prince Stefano Riggio as vicar-general." The spelling differs between sources. Italysvolcanoes says nothing about the veil.
- Conclusion: the sources do not show that the veil saved the city. The lava did enter Catania. Wikipedia also records religious objections to diverting lava, which it calls sacrilegious.

7. **Processions, March to July 1669.** Ruiz Jiménez (abstract): "Durante los primeros meses fueron muy numerosas las procesiones de rogativas" (in the first months, prayer processions were very numerous). They were held in the mountain villages and in Catania, and "fue habitual la presencia de distintas reliquias, entre ellas el velo de Santa Águeda" (relics, including the veil, were customary). The full article could not be read, so no dates per procession are available. Wikipedia: Catania sheltered about 20,000 refugees, and "religious ceremonies took up much of the daily lives of Catania's populace." The eruption ended with lava ceasing on 11 July and the eruption ending on 15 July.

**Other dates and facts with sources**

**2. Soraci, "Agata non è una dea riciclata: smontata la leggenda delle origini pagane"**
- URL read: https://www.prospettive.eu/2026/01/07/agata-non-e-una-dea-riciclata-smontata-la-leggenda-delle-origini-pagane/
- Author: Cristina Soraci, professor of Roman history at the University of Catania. The page metadata lists "Redazione" as author. Published 7 January 2026, modified 15 January 2026. Page in Italian, with a PDF of the full article linked.
- Who proposed the parallels:
  - Emanuele Ciaceri, "La festa di S. Agata e l'antico culto di Iside in Catania", ASSO 2, 1905, pp. 265-298. He argued the feast took on the features of the cult of Isis, which had been imported to the East in the 2nd century BC. He drew on Pietro Carrera's 1639 description of the 17th-century festival, and on Apuleius, Metamorphoses 11.9-10, the Isis festival at Corinth.
  - Ciaceri's specific parallels: the procession itself, white robes, and lit candles (ceri). He called these "un indizio inequivocabile di derivazione" (an unmistakable sign of derivation).
  - Demeter: the page says Agatha was assimilated to Demeter, citing Theodor Trede, Das Heidentum in der römischen Kirche (1890). Blunt (1823) is not mentioned.
- What Soraci says against this:
  - In Corinth, all followers wore white, while in 1126 only the priests did, and they walked barefoot.
  - In Corinth the faithful carried lamps and torches, but in 1126 only two children carried lit candles ahead of the relics.
  - Quote: "storicamente infondata" (historically unfounded), for the claim of continuity over fifteen centuries.
  - Isis worship in Catania is attested only by a few statuettes, a lamp and some coins, all from the 3rd to 1st century BC.
  - For Demeter, there are no explicit sources: "Non esiste, quindi, alcuna prova storica" (there is therefore no historical proof).
  - Her reasoning: such rites are universal anthropological phenomena, and similarity alone does not prove derivation.
  - Her positive point: the cult is documented from the 4th century (the inscription of Iulia Florentina, the Calendar of Carthage, the Passions recounting the lava miracle). Agatha "emerge precocemente come protettrice della città" (emerges early as the city's protector).

## Report: Sicilian Inquisition, Gratteri charms, donne di fuora, and Bosch images

Source tiers: all pages below are non-preapproved, so quotes are kept under 125 characters and my own translations are marked. Two Sciuti-Russi 2004 figures come from a summary, not verbatim text, and are flagged where they appear. The Guidobono Commons page could not be read.

### Part 1: Sicilian Inquisition (Palermo)

**(a) Who burned the archive, and why**

- Sciuti-Russi 1998 (dialnet 157815) dates the bonfire to 27 June 1783, more than a year after abolition. It says the bonfire was "una hoguera política, dictada por la razón de Estado" (my translation: a political bonfire, dictated by reason of state). It says contemporaries (Villabianca, Münter) reported that the inquisitor general Salvatore Ventimiglia requested it, and that aristocracy and church hierarchies welcomed it. The article does not say who physically ordered the burning.
- The same article says the archive held more than 5,000 heresy trials, plus records of noble privileged jurisdiction and limpieza de sangre checks.
- Sciuti-Russi 1998 also says that at the abolition ceremony in 1782 the viceroy Caracciolo "ordenó el secuestro de los archivos y de los muebles." That is seizure, not burning.
- Digital History (Teramo, https://digitalhistory.unite.it/en/?p=6358): "especially for this reason all sensitive materials of the tribunal were burned after its abolition." It explains the motive as noble families wanting to erase their links to the Inquisition.
- Museum of the Inquisition page (turismo.cittametropolitana.pa.it): "A year later the entire archive was unfortunately burnt." It does not say who ordered it.
- Sciuti-Russi 2004 (usal) does not mention the archive fire: "not on this page."

**(b) Charges for which people were relaxed to the secular arm**

No clean tribunal-wide breakdown by charge is available. The best sources are narrow:

- **Judaizers (verbatim, Sciuti-Russi 2004, usal, first part):** "340 hogueras de neófitos judaizantes y a los cerca de dos mil procesos celebrados por judaismo entre 1511 y 1540." This is about 340 burnings and about 2,000 trials, 1511-1540.
- **Judaizers (summary only, not verbatim):** The model summary of the unread part gives "440 los relajados" for 1511-1546. It also gives 25 relaxed "en persona" out of 3,188 defendants for 1547-1701, and "solamente 50 (y la mitad en efigie)" later. These figures conflict with the 340 above and I could not verify them.
- **Protestants/Lutherans, 16th century (verbatim, Abbate 2022, dialnet 9145808):** "284 proceedings against 253 individuals." Of these, "Only in 36 cases the heretic was rilassato in persona by being burnt alive, while 33 times" the person was relaxed in effigy. Also: "215 ended in reconciliation." Only 12 women were sentenced, and no woman was relaxed in person.
- **Renegades/Muslims:** Museum of the Inquisition page: 846 apostates presented, 392 absolved, 282 reconciled. No relaxation count: "not on this page."
- **Witchcraft:** Museum page says "only a few ended up at the stake." Barteredhistory (citing Henningsen 1990) says of 456 Sicilian cases 1547-1701 "not a single execution was carried out." These two conflict.
- **Blasphemy, propositions, sodomy:** Museum page gives 636 blasphemers and 496 suspects of "heretical propositions," with no relaxation numbers. It names sodomy and sollecitatio among sexual offences without counts. Relaxations by charge: "not on this page."
