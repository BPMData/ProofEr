**Henningsen's own later work gives no total.** Henningsen, "El vuelo de las brujas y los inquisidores españoles o

each with its archive reference. He gives no count of *donas de fuera*. Examples: Lauria de Pavia, 1587–88; Catarina

Terranova, 1638–40. His reference list confirms the chapter as **"'The Ladies from Outside': An Archaic Pattern of the

Clarendon Press, Oxford, 1990, pp. 191–215**. It is chapter 7, per the Wellcome Collection catalogue's contents list

**The 2009 *Folklore* article:** I could not confirm its title or contents, so I did not open it. The 2020 Spanish
article above covers the same ground (the witches' flight).

reports to Madrid. 77 is Messana's charge category "contact with the dead", taken from her database for the

be checked without Henningsen's page.

**For the piece:** "Dozens of people were tried. One count of the surviving reports finds 65, eight of them men,
between 1579 and 1651." Label it "as cited". Don't print 70. Don't print 193 unless someone opens Henningsen 1990 in
a library. Lead: the chapter is also said to be excerpted in Oldridge (ed.), *The Witchcraft Reader* (Routledge).
That comes from a search result only, and I have not checked it.

## 3. Start date and archive burning: **CONFIRMED, as a sequence of steps**

| Step | Date | Sources (opened) |
|---|---|---|
| Papal act giving Torquemada authority over Castile and Aragon, which covered Sicily | **24 Mar 1486** (Innocent VIII) | Geremia 2024, n. 3; La Motta, *Ereticopedia* |
| First Spanish inquisitor for Sicily (Antonio de la Peña, named by Torquemada) | **1487** | La Motta; Sciuti Russi 2004 §1/§3; Ruiz Rodríguez, "La Inquisición siciliana", *Revista de la Inquisición* 9 (2000), p. 102 (Dominicans named 1487, "largely inactive") |
| Court established as a stable tribunal | **1500** (new inquisitors' decree of 8 Nov 1500) | La Motta (citing her 2015 thesis); Sciuti Russi ("se estabiliza en 1500"); Geremia n. 3 |
| 1516 | **A riot in Palermo**, after which the tribunal was driven out for "unos tres años" (about three years) | Ruiz Rodríguez 2000, p. 104. Geremia uses "1516–1782" as the span of her documents but doesn't explain it. |
| Abolished | **16 Mar 1782** (Ferdinand IV, at viceroy Caracciolo's request) | Geremia n. 1; La Motta; Bazzano (citing Renda 2010); Sciuti Russi |
| Archive burned | **27 Jun 1783** order; public burning in 1783 | Geremia n. 1 (citing Rodríguez 2010, pp. 1422–23); La Motta ("un anno dopo, nel 1783"); Bazzano, *Ebrei, conversos e Inquisizione nella Sicilia spagnola*, p. 91, citing Renda 2010 ("the following year") |
| "1782" for the burning | **1782** | Henningsen 2020, p. ~1060: "Todos los registros procesales originales fueron quemados en 1782" ("All the original trial records were burned in 1782"), citing his own 1986 work, p. 58 |

**Why the sources differ:** "start" can mean the papal act (1486), the first appointment (1487) or a working court
(1500). Henningsen folds the burning into the abolition year. Three sources, two of them citing documents, put the
order and the bonfire in 1783.

**For the piece:** "set up in 1487, sitting in Palermo from 1500 until 1782; its archive went on a bonfire in 1783."

## 4. St Joseph patron of New Spain, 1555: **CONFIRMED by the primary text** (with a wording fix)

- **Primary text (opened):** First Mexican Provincial Council (1555), **cap. XVIII**, "Qué fiestas se han de guardar…"
  (which feasts must be kept). The council, petitioned "de parte de toda la república, así eclesiástica como seglar"
  (by the whole commonwealth, church and lay), "recibimos al dicho glorioso san José **por patrón general de esta nueva
  Iglesia**" (we receive the said glorious St Joseph as general patron of this new Church). His feast is fixed on
  **19 March**, at the rank of "doble mayor". Edition: *Concilios provinciales mexicanos. Época colonial*, coord. M. del
  Pilar Martínez López-Cano, UNAM–Instituto de Investigaciones Históricas, 2004 (online 2014), Concilio Primero, p. 23.
  https://historicas.unam.mx/publicaciones/publicadigital/libros/concilios/docs/1er2do_002.pdf
- **Second source:** Villaseñor Black, *Creating the Cult of St. Joseph*, Princeton UP, 2006. Its date of 1555 was
  seen in a CAA review (E-pass1). The book itself is not opened.
- **Wording:** the council made him patron of the *new Church* of the province of Mexico (New Spain). It was not a
  civil act of the viceroyalty. Say "patron of New Spain's Church (1555)".

## Summary
1. *Donne di fuora*: still disputed. 65 (8 men, 1579–1651) is the defendants in the surviving reports; 77 is Messana's
   charge category "contact with the dead"; 193 is Geremia's unpaged reading of Henningsen; 70 is unsupported (drop it).
2. Inquisition totals form a range explained by period and unit: 7,161 (Renda p. 203, whole life), about 6,000
   (Messana), 3,188 to 4,057 (surviving reports 1547–1701; est. 6,302). Magic about 921–976, most acquitted, 2 men executed.
3. Dates confirmed as steps: authority 1486, first inquisitor 1487, working court 1500 (1516 was a riot), abolished
   16 Mar 1782, archive burned 1783. Henningsen's "1782" folds the burning into the abolition year.
4. St Joseph, 1555: confirmed from the council's own text (cap. XVIII): "patrón general de esta nueva Iglesia",
   feast on 19 March. Say "patron of New Spain's Church".
5. Benedict: canonized 24 May 1807; beatified May 1743 (1763 is a slip); born 1524 (or 1526). Rio 1612 and Salvador
   1630 are still thin, and Rio's "1640" is the Rosary brotherhood's date. Mulegé 1705 is confirmed; that Sicilian
   Jesuits chose the name is unsupported.
