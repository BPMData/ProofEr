## Summary

- **The words.** The key terms are *syncretism* (mixing of religious elements; Shaw & Stewart 1994 turned it into a
  question of who calls something "mixed" and why), *folk religion* (Yoder 1974), *vernacular religion* (Primiano 1995),
  *local religion* (Christian 1981) and *popular religion* (religione popolare). In the Italian tradition the frame
  came from Gramsci and then de Martino: "the culture of the subaltern classes". In this pass the definitions could
  be checked only as citations. The bot checks at Érudit and Project MUSE and the fetch limit blocked the full texts,
  so the definitions are not quoted (see Gaps).
- **The case against "pagan survival".** The case against the survivals model (Tylor 1871 → Frazer → Murray) is well
  documented in English:
  - Hutton (2023) on Britain.
  - Filotas (2005): "pagan survivals" are "controversial, nebulous concepts".
  - Magliocco (2002) on the Aradia legend.
  - de Martino himself, who rejected reading southern Italian magic as a pre-Christian leftover (Lepage 2017 review
    of *Magic: A Theory from the South*).
  - Carroll's two Johns Hopkins books (1992, 1996) date the shape of Italian popular Catholicism to the period
    after the 15th century, not to antiquity. His Italian-American book argues the "pagan Catholicism" story was
    largely invented.
  - Against this, Pitrè (1881) framed Sicilian customs as "remains of ancient mythologies", and a 2024 article
    (Geremia) still argues a "long continuity" of pre-Christian belief as a hypothesis. The survival idea has both a
    Sicilian origin and current defenders.
- **The timeline.** A dated timeline from Thucydides to 1861 is sourced below, from the Met's Heilbrunn Timeline,
  Britannica, the Catholic Encyclopedia, the ICOMOS 2015 evaluation and Thucydides. The sources differ on several
  dates: Arab Palermo 831 vs. 820/835, Norman Palermo 1071 vs. 1072, Norman conquest "1060" vs. 1061–1091, the Two
  Sicilies 1815 vs. 1816, and the founding of the Inquisition tribunal 1486/1487 vs. 1500 vs. 1516.
- **Earliest records of the practices** (Group A.3) were found for only a few:
  - The Agatha cult: 5th–6th-century martyrologies.
  - The Rosalia cult surge: the 1624 plague.
  - The Trapani Misteri: a notarial deed of 20 April 1612.
  - The *donne di fuora*: a mid-15th-century confessors' manual cited at second hand, and Inquisition trials from
    1579/1588 to 1651.
  - Pitrè's volumes: 1881, 1889 and 1896 are the first systematic records of feasts, supernatural beings and folk
    medicine.
  - No earliest record could be sourced in this pass for the Saint Joseph tables, the *decollati*, the Day of the
    Dead gifts, ex-votos, the Madonna della Lettera, the *ciarmavermi* or the evil eye.

## Disagreements

1. **When the Spanish Inquisition began in Sicily.** One article (Geremia 2024, *Religions*) gives three dates:
   - 24 March 1486, Innocent VIII's authorization (note 3);
   - 1500, when its district court began working (note 3);
   - 1516, the start of the period it covers, in its abstract and introduction ("1516–1782").

   Messana's book title uses 1500–1782. The usual "1487" was not seen in an opened source.
   - *Why they differ:* papal authorization, the first sitting tribunal, and the start of Habsburg (Charles V) rule
     are three different events.
   - *For the essay:* say "established in the 1480s–1500, abolished 1782", or give each event its own date.
2. **Arab capture of Palermo.**
   - 831 is the date most modern accounts use, but no opened source in this pass gave it.
   - The 1911 *Catholic Encyclopedia* gives 820 (temporary) and 835 (permanent).
   - Britannica compresses the conquest to "965", the completion date.
   - Aramco World says it "took 75 years" from Mazara in 827.
   - *Why:* old vs. modern chronologies, and the start vs. the end of the conquest. Pass 2 should settle 831 from a
     modern scholarly source.
3. **Norman conquest.**
   - Britannica: "1060". Met: 1061–1091.
   - Palermo: 1071 (ICOMOS 2015; Catholic Encyclopedia 1911) vs. the widely quoted 1072. The siege ended in
     January 1072, which may explain it; not verified here.
4. **Two Sicilies.** Britannica says the united title "became official in 1815". The common date is 1816 (the decree
   of December 1816), which was not verified here. Likely cause: the Congress of Vienna settlement (1815) vs. the
   administrative decree (1816).
5. **Pitrè's *Archivio*.** Founded in 1882 (Treccani DBI, Dei 2015) vs. 1880 (Wikipedia). Treccani is the
   authority.
6. **Sicilian autonomy.** Britannica gives 1947. The statute is commonly dated 1946. Not resolved here.
7. **Counts of *donne di fuora* cases.** These bear on Group D:
   - 65 people tried, 8 of them men: Wikipedia citing Gijswijt-Hofstra & Levack 1999, p. 140. Not opened; Google
     Books blocks automated reading.
   - 77 *donne de fora* accused: Messana 2007, p. 75, cited by Geremia 2024.
   - 193 "ladies from outside": Henningsen 1990, pp. 191–215, cited by Geremia 2024. Geremia notes these
     *relaciones* cover all of the Spanish Empire's tribunals, not Sicily alone.

   *Why:* different counting rules (people vs. cases), sources (the full *relaciones* run vs. selected years) and
   scope. Group D should resolve this from Henningsen's chapter directly.
