# Layout plan: Sicilian folk magic (working title "Saints & Spells")

Stage: layout · APPROVED Nov 23, 2026 by Bryan ("Lock it in")

_Written from `brief.md` and `figures.md` only; nothing on the restricted list is open yet. Concept chosen Nov 23:
A, the festa stage, with B's myth-check stamp and C's candle wall, and puppets on visible sticks (Decisions log)._

Format: long scrolling essay, phone first (390 px), also tablet and desktop · Read alone, close, as long as they like.

The one sentence: **The Church moved into Sicily's temples and calendar, but the "goddess under the saint" stories are
mostly 19th-century inventions; what is truly Sicilian is the local form: its saints, relics, tables, souls and
healers.**

The visual idea: **a cut-paper puppet theatre (teatrino) on a majolica-tile wall.** The essay is a show in acts. A
proscenium stays on screen while you scroll (sticky on phone, top 45% of the screen; left half on desktop); puppets
cut from public-domain paintings ride on visible wooden sticks, pushed on from below by an unseen puppeteer, and the
text scrolls past in plaster-white cards. Three running devices:
- **The myth-check stamp (from B):** every `myth` row lands as a red rubber stamp on the puppet it's about, with
  "first claimed" and who said it. Mulder ("I want to believe") and Scully ("Show me the source") speak in small
  margin bubbles at most once an act.
- **The relic cabinet (T20):** a small baroque reliquary cabinet in the stage's corner fills one drawer per relic the
  reader meets, with a counter; tap it for the tally so far. It pays off in Act 7.
- **The candle wall (from C):** Acts 6 and 8 (the dead, the healers) drop the stage into night: a dark chapel wall,
  swinging tin ex-votos, flickering votive candles. The rest of the piece stays on the light tile ground (T12 met).

Every Sicilian term is underlined in tile blue and opens a one-line gloss (T15, T17). Belief is written as belief.

## Sources and "About these numbers"
Last on the page, after the curtain. Sources grouped by act, each with title, author or institution, year and page,
and every image with its artwork, artist, holder and license. "About these numbers" explains: the research was run
as independent passes and cross-checked (two sources per figure); ranges where sources differ (1071–72, 6,000–7,000,
25–52, "about 1040"); "dozens tried" for the *donne di fuora* and why (65, 77 or 193, by what is counted); counts from
a single document are named in the text (D3); the relic drawers are what the sources report, not an inventory; no
survey of evil-eye belief in Sicily exists in what was found.

Palette and roles: tile cream ground, plaster-white text cards, ink near-black brown for text, majolica blue for
glossed terms and links, festa red only for myth-check stamps, gold for highlights with dark text on it (T07), night
ground plum-black to oxblood with candle gold text. Type: Gloock (display and stamps) and Literata (body, labels),
both open-licensed (SIL OFL); two families (CRAFT §5).
