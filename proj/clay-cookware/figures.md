# Checked Figures: Glazed vs. Unglazed Clay Cookware

_Synthesized from Pass 1 (`pass1/`), Pass 2 (`gap_fill/`), and Clay-Types Primer (`pass2_primer/`) under `proj/clay-cookware/research/workers/`._
_Rule: Every figure placed on the page must be `checked` (>= 2 independent sources, primary preferred) or explicitly labeled if `single source` / `gap`. Disagreements stay as honest ranges and notes._

## Summary of Key Disagreements & Evidence Gaps
1. **Römertopf +50°F vs. +100°F (+50°C):** Official US Römertopf booklet prints `+100°F (~+50°C)` and `-30 min` vs. conventional recipes; German `roemertopf.de` uses direct targets (`180–200°C` convection, `~220°C` top/bottom, max `250°C`); "+50°F" on blogs stems from confusing $\Delta 50^\circ\text{C}$ ($\approx 90^\circ\text{F}$) with $\Delta 50^\circ\text{F}$ ($27.8^\circ\text{C}$).
2. **2 / 4 / 8-hour slow-cook evaporation table:** No authoritative test kitchen (ATK, Serious Eats, Modernist Cuisine, Stiftung Warentest) or university extension publishes a controlled 2h/4h/8h % evaporation table comparing unglazed clay, glazed clay, and metal pots side-by-side. We have verified mechanism/operational numbers instead (slow-cooker 30–50% less added liquid, VitaClay VM7900 double-lid vs. VM7800 stockpot evaporation split, Dutch oven sealed `~210°F` vs. lid-ajar `~190°F`, and `20–40%` meat juice expulsion). We must NOT fabricate a 2/4/8h % evaporation curve.
3. **Weighed soak water in Römertopf & La Chamba ASTM porosity:** Neither Römertopf nor any test kitchen weighed a soaked Römertopf in grams, and no lab published an ASTM C373 water-absorption % specifically on modern La Chamba. Both use verified ASTM/ISO material class benchmarks (and low-fired micaceous potsherd analogs) with explicit gap notes.
4. **Zisha CTE & k:** Natural Zisha `k = 1.3 W/(m·K)` is a `single source` peer-reviewed modeling value (Lee & Attinger 2016); subtype-resolved CTE ($4.5\text{–}6.5 \times 10^{-6}/\text{K}$ on tea blogs) is `unverified` in primary open literature. Use verified `GB/T 10816` quench standard (`180°C → 20°C`) instead.
5. **Induction diffuser plates:** Direct conflict between induction cooktop OEMs (Miele/Bosch prohibit; GE/Sub-Zero warn against) and some flameware makers (Emile Henry Flame / Clay Coyote allow with disk), while Toiro Iga-yaki donabe explicitly forbids diffusers due to cracking risk. Show this conflict visibly.

## Odd Units Explained (for reader clarity, per `T17`)
- **Thermal conductivity ($k$, $\text{W}/(\text{m}\cdot\text{K})$):** How fast heat travels *through* the pot wall—higher means hot burner spots spread faster, while low-$k$ clay keeps heat trapped near the source (raising hotspot gradients).
- **Volumetric heat capacity ($C_v = \rho \cdot c_p$, $\text{MJ}/(\text{m}^3\cdot\text{K})$):** How much heat energy a fixed *volume* (say, one cubic centimeter) of pot wall stores per degree. Aluminum holds more heat *per pound*, but cast iron is nearly 3× denser, so cast iron holds far more heat *per millimeter of wall thickness*.
- **Thermal diffusivity ($\alpha = k / (\rho c_p)$, $\text{mm}^2/\text{s}$):** How quickly a temperature wave races across the material; aluminum equalizes ~100× faster than terracotta.
- **Linear CTE ($\alpha$, $10^{-6}/\text{K}$ or $\text{ppm}/\text{K}$):** How many millionths of its length a pot expands for every 1°C of heating; bigger mismatch across a hot vs. cold spot means higher cracking stress.
- **Water activity ($a_w$, $0\text{–}1$):** Free, chemically available moisture at the food surface (pure water $= 1.0$), distinct from total moisture weight.
- **ASTM C373 water absorption (%) vs. open porosity (%):** Water absorption measures water weight gained relative to *dry clay weight*; open porosity measures pore *volume* relative to bulk pot volume (always a larger number because clay is ~1.6–2.5× denser than water).

## Myths Checked (per `T10`)
- **M01 — "Cast iron sears best because it conducts heat best":** FALSE. Cast iron's conductivity (`40–72 W/(m·K)`) is ~3–5× *lower* than aluminum (`159–237`). It excels at searing because thick walls and high volumetric heat capacity (`3.27–3.69 MJ/(m³·K)`) resist temperature crash when cold wet food lands.
- **M02 — "Soaking a clay pot protects it in a preheated oven":** FALSE. Pore-trapped water flashes to steam inside a 400–450°F oven, adding internal vapor-pressure spalling on top of steep thermal gradients; soaked pots *must* start in a cold oven.
- **M03 — "Römertopf recipes only need +50°F" / "cook at 450°F":** NUANCED / PARTIAL MYTH. Official US Römertopf manuals actually specify `+100°F` (`~+50°C`, `-30 min`), and blogs often misread `+50°C` as `+50°F`; meanwhile, evaporative cooling pins the wet interior near `100°C (212°F)` until wall moisture exhausts or the lid comes off.
- **M04 — "La Chamba is shiny black because of a glaze or black coating":** FALSE. 0% glaze—the sheen is river-stone burnishing aligning flat clay platelets, blackened by organic smoke reduction (`ahumado`).
- **M05 — "'Volcanic clay' pots are molded from lava and emit special far-infrared rays":** FALSE. Lava rock has zero plasticity; modern zero-absorption *ttukbaegi* rely on added petalite, and any pot at 100–250°C emits `~5–20 µm` IR—clay simply has higher surface emissivity ($\varepsilon \approx 0.90\text{–}0.95$ vs. `<0.1` for shiny metal).
- **M06 — "All unglazed clay pots must be pre-soaked and never touch soap":** FALSE. Römertopf oven bakers require a `10–20 min` soak; VitaClay *prohibits* soaking the exterior pot (risks cracking & electrical damage) and allows mild dish soap if thoroughly rinsed (even modern Römertopf allows a few drops of mild soap, though harsh detergents adsorb onto clay minerals and leach on reheat).
- **M07 — "Induction diffuser plates safely make any clay pot induction-compatible":** FALSE / CONFLICTED. Miele & Bosch explicitly prohibit adapter plates (risking generator destruction), GE & Sub-Zero discourage them, and Toiro forbids them on Iga-yaki donabe (cracking risk), even though Emile Henry Flame & Clay Coyote allow them at low heat.
- **M08 — "Modern commercial glazed slow-cooker inserts pose the same lead risk as artisanal barro—and vinegar/garlic curing fixes lead glazes":** FALSE. Compliant commercial inserts pass FDA `CPG 545.450` / Prop 65 leach testing; household vinegar/milk/garlic boiling does NOT seal unstable low-fire lead glazes.
