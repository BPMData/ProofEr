Thermal Conductivity (k, W/m·K) vs. Volumetric Heat Capacity (Cv)

Zero-baseline bars: k measures how fast heat crosses the wall; Cv measures how much heat a cubic centimeter of wall stores per degree. Cast iron conducts 3–5× slower than aluminum, yet stores ~50% more heat per unit volume.

What to Actually Do With Any Unglazed, Water-Soaked Clay Baker

Applies to all unglazed, water-soaked clay pots (Römertopfs and unbranded clay roasters alike)—not dry-used Zisha or glazed stoneware.

10–20 min Cold-water soak (30 min if new/dry)
+100°F Above metal recipes (400–450°F dial; −30 min)
15–20 min Uncovered at the end for browning
Do / Don't rule: Do put the soaked pot into a cold oven—the trapped water buffers food near 212°F (100°C) steam while the dial runs ~400–450°F (200–230°C, max 500°F). Don't put wet clay into a preheated oven (flash steam + ΔT shatters it), and don't under-set by +50°F if converting a standard recipe.
Why do half the clay-pot blogs say "+50°F" while the booklet says "+100°F"?

A 50°C jump equals +90°F (rounded to +100°F in the US Römertopf booklet); blogs that advise "+50°F" confused Δ50°C with Δ50°F (only 27.8°C). Meanwhile, roemertopf.de skips offsets entirely and specifies 180–200°C convection (~220°C top/bottom).
