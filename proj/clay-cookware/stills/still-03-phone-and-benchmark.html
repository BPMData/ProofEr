Supporting Benchmark · Multi-Cooker Inserts (F29–F31)

Magnifique vs. VitaClay vs. Metal Inserts

Two clay multi-cookers sit on opposite sides of the glaze divide—and neither acts like cast iron or PTFE aluminum.

Magnifique MCR51P 1,130 W · 4.5 qt

Wall
Kaolin clay + kaolin glaze (fired >1,500°F; low-CTE additives undisclosed)
Sear / Oven
Base Sear/Sauté, gas/electric stovetop, oven to 750°F (450–500°F on MCP41P); NO induction
Care / Flavor
Glass-sealed (~0% open pores); soap & dishwasher-friendly; zero flavor carryover

VitaClay VM7900 / VM7800 500–600 W · 3–6.5 qt

Wall
Unglazed Zisha (40–50% quartz, 1,100–1,220°C; ~2–5% water absorption)
Sear / Oven
NO stovetop, oven, or searing; brown meat in a separate skillet
2–8h Liquid
VM7900 double-lid cuts 8h slow-cook to ~2h; VM7800 dome vents faster (needs 4–8c + warm top-ups)
Spec Gap
"480°F thermal shock" appears on vitaclaychef.com & YouTube, omitted from PDF manuals

Metal Benchmarks 1,000–1,780 W · 5–8.5 qt

PTFE / Sol-Gel Al
Fast heat-up (k 159–237); PTFE degrades >260°C (500°F); sol-gel release fades 250–300°C
Enameled Cast Iron
Slowest to cool (Cv 3.27–3.69 MJ/m³·K); 21–22 lb unit; induction/gas/450–500°F oven safe
About These Numbers · What No Lab Has Measured Yet Neither America's Test Kitchen, Serious Eats, Stiftung Warentest, nor university extensions have published a side-by-side 2/4/8-hour container evaporation (%) table comparing clay inserts against metal slow cookers—nor has any lab weighed a soaked Römertopf in grams. Those missing curves stay off the chart rather than invented.
