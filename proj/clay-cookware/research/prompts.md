# Research Prompts: Glazed vs. Unglazed Clay Cookware

All prompts require >= 2 independent authoritative sources per quantitative claim (materials science / ceramic engineering handbooks, peer-reviewed papers, university extension/food-science guides, ASTM/ISO/FDA standards, and official manufacturer manuals), explicit units, and documented disagreements.

## P1 — Thermal Physics & Oven Temperature Setpoints (`thermal-physics`)
Find exact thermal conductivity (W/m·K), specific heat capacity (J/kg·K or J/g·K), density (kg/m³), and thermal diffusivity values for: (1) unglazed terracotta / earthenware, (2) stoneware / vitrified glazed ceramic, (3) Zisha / purple clay, (4) cast iron, and (5) aluminum cookware alloy. Also investigate whether lidded unglazed soaked clay bakers (specifically Römertopf) require higher oven setpoints (e.g. +25°F to +50°F / +15–30°C vs. standard recipes) or cold-oven starts, and measure the physics behind evaporative wall cooling and thermal mass lag.

## P2 — Porosity, Water Absorption & 2/4/8-Hour Slow-Cook Moisture Loss (`porosity-water-loss`)
Find measured open porosity (%) and water absorption (%) for unglazed earthenware/terracotta, unglazed Zisha clay, burnished/smoked micaceous clay (La Chamba), and vitrified glazed stoneware. Find quantitative data or test measurements on: how much water a soaked Römertopf absorbs and releases during cooking; moisture/steam loss rates over 2-, 4-, and 8-hour slow cooks in unglazed clay vs. glazed clay vs. metal slow cookers/Dutch ovens; and whether users need to top up water during multi-hour slow cooking.

## P3 — Thermal Shock Resistance & Cracking Mechanics (`thermal-shock`)
Find thermal expansion coefficients (CTE, ppm/K or ×10⁻⁶ /°C) and critical thermal shock temperature differentials (ΔT_c in °C/°F) for earthenware, stoneware, Zisha clay, flameware ceramics (cordierite/petalite/lithium aluminosilicate), cast iron, and aluminum. Document why clay cookware cracks from thermal shock (rapid ΔT, uneven burner contact, wet/cold pots placed in hot ovens or on hot surfaces) and how enclosed electric multi-cooker heating jackets / gradual PID ramps alter thermal shock stress.

## P4 — Maillard Browning, Surface Moisture & Flavor Memory (`browning-surface-flavor`)
Find food-science and materials-science data on: (1) Maillard reaction temperature thresholds and how surface moisture/steam boundary layers in porous unglazed clay vs. glazed vitrified ceramic vs. aluminum non-stick vs. cast iron affect searing and browning; (2) oil, fat, and volatile aroma compound absorption into open clay pores ("flavor memory" / seasoning in Zisha, terracotta, and Römertopf)—what compounds adsorb, why manufacturers say not to use soap, and documented evidence of flavor carryover vs. when seasoning helps.

## P5 — Stovetop/Induction Compatibility, La Chamba Burnishing & Glaze Safety (`stovetop-la-chamba-glaze-safety`)
Verify stovetop compatibility rules and physical failure modes for clay cookware across: gas flame, electric coil, radiant glass-ceramic smooth-top, and induction (including steel/ferromagnetic induction diffuser plates—do diffuser plates cause dangerous localized overheating/thermal shock on clay?). Document La Chamba clay's material composition and production (micaceous clay, stone burnishing, reduction/smoke firing—why it is shiny despite being unglazed, and why it tolerates direct flame/stovetop heat better than standard earthenware). Also check FDA/CPSC/ISO 6486 standards and empirical status on lead/cadmium glaze leaching: confirm whether modern commercial glazed cookware/multi-cooker inserts pose real risk vs. unverified artisanal low-fire lead glazes.

## P6 — Multi-Cooker Hardware Benchmark: Magnifique vs. VitaClay vs. Metal Inserts (`multicooker-inserts`)
Gather verified manufacturer specifications and engineering/material details for: (1) Magnifique clay multi-cooker / slow cooker (glazed ceramic/clay insert composition, capacity, wattage, cooking modes/temps, browning/stovetop/oven rules), (2) VitaClay multi-cooker (unglazed Zisha clay insert composition, capacity, wattage, cooking modes/temps, soaking/soap/browning rules), and (3) standard aluminum PTFE/ceramic non-stick and enameled/bare cast-iron multi-cooker inserts (e.g., Instant Pot / traditional slow cooker benchmarks for weight, heat-up behavior, and moisture retention).
