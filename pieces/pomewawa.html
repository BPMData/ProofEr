The Pomewawa · a field guide

Tiny.
Ancient.
Unbothered.

The Pomewawa is a Pomeranian × Chihuahua. Two tiny breeds, one long and strange history. Every number on this page is checked; the sources are at the bottom.

By Bryan Murphy

Plate I · Aztec roots

On the menu
since 1520.

  1. From 1100 BCEAt Ceibal, a Maya city in today's Guatemala, dogs ate so much corn it shows in the chemistry of their bones. Some were brought in alive from the highlands.
    Sharpe et al., PNAS, 2018
  2. 1520Hernán Cortés writes to Emperor Charles V about the great market of Tenochtitlan, where “little dogs” are raised for eating.
    Cortés, Second Letter to Charles V, Oct 30, 1520
  3. TodaySome Chihuahuas still carry DNA from ancient Mexican dogs on their mother's side.
    van Asch et al., Proceedings of the Royal Society B, 2013
MYTH CHECK
“Aztec dogs were living hot-water bottles”
No old record backs it
A popular story, repeated for decades. We found no early written source for it, and it's told about the hairless Xolo, not the Chihuahua's ancestors. Unproven, not disproven.
LEGEND CHECK
“A dog carried the dead across the river of the underworld”
Real Aztec belief
Recorded in the Florentine Codex (16th century). The detail about which dog colors would refuse comes from later retellings.

Plate II · Drawn to scale

A big dog,
made small.

The Pomeranian and the Wolfspitz share one breed standard (FCI 97), at opposite ends. Height at the shoulder, from the ground up.

The Wolfspitz is a bit over twice as tall. It's a cousin in the same standard, not a proven ancestor; the Pomeranian's own ancestors were bigger Spitz dogs too.

Weight, then and now

Breed histories say the Pomeranian's size about halved during Victoria's lifetime. Years before, around 1777, Thomas Gainsborough painted a Pomeranian and her puppy; the painting hangs in the Tate, London.

FCI standard 97; American Pomeranian Club; Wikipedia, “Pomeranian dog”; Dogs Queensland; Frick Collection and Tate.

Plate III · Attitude

Small.
Armed.
Documented.

Top tier

In a 2008 survey of dog owners covering more than 30 breeds, Chihuahuas were among the breeds most likely to bite, or try to, with strangers, and above average for aggression toward other dogs.

Duffy, Hsu & Serpell, Applied Animal Behaviour Science, 2008. Owner-reported; a later Swedish study found lower Chihuahua scores.
8,301 dogs

Across 49 breeds, the shorter the breed, the more problem behaviors owners reported, including aggression toward the owners themselves.

McGreevy et al., PLOS ONE, 2013. A pattern across breed averages, not proof that height causes it.
67th

Where the Chihuahua lands in Stanley Coren's famous ranking of breeds by obedience. Unbothered.

Coren, The Intelligence of Dogs, 1994 (based on surveys of obedience judges).

MYTH CHECK
“A Cane Corso bites at 700 PSI. A Kangal, 743.”
Nobody ever measured it
No study has tested these breeds. The numbers have no source.

The hardest bite actually measured in a pet dog with a sensor: 1,394 newtons, about 313 pounds of force. Most of the 22 dogs tested bit far softer (middle value: 163 newtons).

Lindner et al., Journal of Veterinary Dentistry, 1995

Plate VII · The menu

Everyone eats
the dog eventually.

Bryan's theory: “actually everyone would eat a dog like a cinnabon if they had to.” The record agrees. Hunger, war, custom: it happened all over the world. Below, in date order. Tap one to open it.

Bryan's favorite · South Pole, 1911

Amundsen's Butcher's Shop

Roald Amundsen planned it in advance: how much each sled dog could haul, and on which dates dog after dog would stop pulling and become food. His party killed 24 dogs at a camp they named “the Butcher's Shop.” The meat fed the men and the remaining dogs.

Amundsen's diary, Nov 20, 1911 (Norwegian Polar Institute); Ralph Magazine, “Eating Your Transport”
Maya and Aztec Mexico1100 BCE–1520

Maya dogs at Ceibal were fed corn and some were traded in alive. In 1520 Cortés saw little dogs raised for eating in the Tenochtitlan market (Plate I).

Sharpe et al., PNAS, 2018; Cortés, Second Letter, 1520
Hawaiʻithe ʻīlio

The Hawaiian dog, the ʻīlio, was fattened on poi and eaten. Under the old rules women couldn't eat pork, but they could eat dog.

Wikipedia, “Hawaiian Poi Dog”; Images of Old Hawaiʻi
Jamestown, Virginia1609–1617

English colonists, starving, ate dogs during and after the “Starving Time.” DNA shows at least six of the dogs they ate had Native American ancestry. The study's authors say the colonists depended on their Native neighbors to survive.

Thomas et al., American Antiquity, 2024; Smithsonian Magazine
Lewis and Clark1805–1806

With game scarce, the expedition bought dogs from Native peoples along the way, and the men ate them. William Clark couldn't bring himself to.

HistoryLink, citing Moulton's edition of the journals; Confluence Project
Paris, under siege1870

Cut off by the siege, Paris ran out of meat. From November 1870, dog was sold at market for 20 to 40 centimes a pound.

Atlas Obscura; Mental Floss, citing Édouard Manet's letters
Germanybanned 1986

Germany made slaughtering dogs and cats for food illegal only in 1986.

inFranken.de; World Dog Alliance
Switzerlandstill legal at home

Selling dog meat is banned, but eating your own dog at home is legal. It's best known among farmers in Appenzell and St. Gallen. After a 1993 petition, parliament decided it wasn't necessary to regulate people's eating habits.

Swissinfo; The Local (2023); AFP
South Koreaban from 2027

On Jan 9, 2024, the National Assembly voted 208–0 to end breeding, slaughtering and selling dogs for meat, starting in 2027: up to 3 years in prison or a 30 million won fine.

Global News; ABC News; and other outlets

About these numbers

How we
checked.

Research ran in Gemini Deep Research and ChatGPT deep research; every figure was then cross-checked against the sources below. A number made it onto this page only if at least two independent sources agreed.

Lifespans

Typical (median) age at death from one big UK study. Other studies count differently: one gives the Chihuahua 7.9 years “at birth,” because it counts the many puppies who die young. We used age at death. We found no study of the Pomewawa itself; its numbers come from its parents.

Attitude

The behavior studies are owner surveys: they show rankings and patterns, not lab measurements, and one later study found lower Chihuahua scores.

Sizes

Breed standards describe show dogs, not every dog. The Wolfspitz is a cousin in the Pomeranian's breed standard, not a proven ancestor. We left out old weights for the Pomeranian's ancestors: the sources gave three different numbers and none was solid.

Bite force

No study has measured Kangals or Cane Corsos. “PSI” is a pressure, not a force, and the popular numbers have no source.

Myth checks

A myth check means we found no old record. That's unproven, not disproven.

Left out

Two figures we couldn't confirm, a percentage of ancient Mexican DNA in Chihuahuas and a lifespan for Pomeranians, are left off until the papers can be checked.
